# Products and prices across the 19 companies

Written 24 November 2026 from the company digests (`synthesis/digest/`), the company data files
(`companies/<slug>/company.json`), `foundations/market-economics.md` (section 4), the clinical and regulation files in
`foundations/`, and the two market sweeps in `market/`.

## How to read this file

**Evidence labels.** Every strength and price carries the label the company's own digest gives it:

- **[obs]** observed: seen on screen in a live walk of the site.
- **[code]** read from the site's own code or data feed (for example the price table a page loads).
- **[docs]** read from public pages, leaflets, help articles, search-engine summaries or third-party reviews.
- **[inf]** inferred: worked out by us, not read anywhere.
- **[sweep]** from `market/uk-sweep.md` or `market/us-intl-sweep.md`. These were not independently fact-checked.

**Licence codes used in the tables.**

- **L** = licensed medicine, used for what its licence covers (for example finasteride 1 mg tablets for male
  pattern hair loss).
- **O** = off-label: a licensed medicine used outside its licence (for example minoxidil tablets, which are licensed
  for high blood pressure, used for hair loss; dutasteride, licensed for an enlarged prostate).
- **G** = licensed medicine sold without a prescription (general sale or pharmacy medicine), for example Regaine.
- **C** = compounded: an unlicensed "special" made up by a pharmacy or specials manufacturer for a named patient. It
  has no licence at all. In the US the wording is "compounded, not FDA-approved".
- **S** = supplement or cosmetic (not a medicine).
- **–** = not sold. **?** = sold, but the strength is not published.

**Prices.** Read on 23 or 24 November 2026, during Black Friday. "List" is the normal price. "First order" is an
introductory price. A "month" of tablets usually means 28 or 30 tablets. Where two sources disagree, both are shown
and marked **CONFLICT**. Old prices are marked **OLD**.

**How far to trust each company's rows.**

## Ten findings that matter most

1. **The same licensed finasteride tablet costs 2 to 4 times more at the subscription brands.** £7.25 to £14 a month
   at Dr Fox and Oxford Online Pharmacy, against £22 to £35 at Numan, Sons, Hims UK, Treated, Lloyds, Boots and
   Superdrug. UK median: £28.50 for a single month, £22.83 a month on a 6-month supply (section 3.2).
2. **Compounded finasteride + minoxidil scalp products cost £12 to £58 a month.** Dr Fox sells a triple-active
   spray for £12 to £13 a month with no subscription. The subscription brands cluster at £31.50 to £37.91. Hims UK
   reaches £16.50 only on a 6-month plan paid up front (section 3.3).
3. **Headline prices are first-order prices.** Voy "from £13", Sons "from £13.60", Hair + Me £14.99 for three months,
   Philip Kingsley 50% off the first delivery, Numan 40% off (its banner says 50%: **CONFLICT**). Renewals are at
   list price (section 3.1).
4. **Hair + Me bills every 4 weeks, not every month.** £34.99 × 13 = £454.87 a year, which is £37.91 a calendar month.
5. **Unlicensed products lead the subscription ranges even where a licensed option exists.** MHRA guidance says a
   licensed medicine, even used off-label, should be chosen before an unlicensed special, and that cost or convenience
   is not a reason. Yet Sons, Voy and Hair + Me supply oral minoxidil as unlicensed capsules while licensed 2.5 mg
   tablets exist (Lloyds and Oxford sell them) (section 5).
6. **The "extra-strength" minoxidil sold as a selling point has no evidence behind it.** Voy (10%), Numan (6% and
   10%), Hims (6% and 7%) and Happy Head (8%) sell strengths above 5%; the one trial of 10% found it worse than 5%
   and more irritating (section 5.3).
7. **Women are badly served in the UK.** Of the 12 UK companies, 6 treat men only, 1 offers women only Regaine and
   supplements, and only Hers UK sells an anti-androgen (a fixed 44 mg spironolactone dose inside a compounded
   capsule). No UK company in the set sells plain spironolactone tablets, topical spironolactone or a blood-test-led
   women's plan with a named treatment (section 4).
8. **What US firms sell that no UK company in the set sells:** latanoprost, topical cetirizine, steroid-containing
   scalp mixes, topical spironolactone, plain oral spironolactone, laser helmets, DNA tests and prescription
   ketoconazole bundles. Most of these have weak or no evidence. The useful gaps for a UK entrant are spironolactone
   for women, licensed-first adjustable plans, and add-ons with moderate evidence (microneedling, laser) (section 5).
9. **Services are thin where it matters clinically.** Scalp photos before prescribing are not routine at any of the
   12 UK companies as far as public material shows; blood tests are built into a hair plan only in Oxford's women's
   programme; money-back promises exist at Voy, Numan, Sons and Hers UK but not for Hims UK men; Hims UK cancels only
   by message to support, and Hair + Me's prepaid bundles cannot be cancelled mid-term (section 6).
10. **Evidence limits:** Voy, Hers (US) and Oxford rest on public pages, XYON is unverified, Sons and Ro were in final
    review, and no company's post-sign-in price or prescribed formula was observed.

## 2. Forms and vehicles

A **vehicle** (or base) is the liquid, gel or foam that carries the drug onto the scalp. It decides how the product
feels, how often it is used and who reacts to it. **Propylene glycol** is a common solvent in minoxidil solutions;
it is a frequent cause of itch and irritation. **Ethanol** (alcohol) helps the liquid dry fast but stings and makes
the product flammable.

### 2.2 What the companies say about bases, feel and safety

**Propylene glycol and alcohol**

- Hims UK's 4-in-1 spray lists ethanol and propylene glycol [code]; Hims (US) lists alcohol and propylene glycol for
  its finasteride + minoxidil spray [docs].
- Hims UK sells a separate "alcohol-free minoxidil serum" that "does not contain alcohol or propylene glycol" [docs].
- Hair + Me's drops are a water-based, alcohol-free suspension labelled "PGF" (usually meaning propylene-glycol-free)
  [docs]. This is its main answer to the commonest complaint about high-street minoxidil.
- Philip Kingsley's 5% drops contain ethanol, water and propylene glycol; the 3% leaflet lists ethanol and water
  only [docs].
- Happy Head's "aqueous" formula still "contains some alcohol" [docs]; XYON offers an alcohol-based solution beside
  its gel [docs].
- Ro's consultation separates a reaction to the minoxidil liquid from a reaction to foam. A reaction to the liquid
  only is probably the propylene glycol base [inf]; a reaction to both is read as true minoxidil allergy and ends the
  visit [code].
- Superdrug filters Regaine for Women out for anyone allergic to minoxidil, ethanol or propylene glycol [obs].
- Numan warns that alcohol-based sprays can worsen asthma and lets the man opt out of topicals [obs]; the opt-out
  does not remove the products on screen [obs].
- Dr Fox ships its spray and Regaine solution to mainland UK only because they are flammable [docs].
- In the UK sweep, Courier Pharmacy offers a propylene-glycol-free "TrichoSol" base, and City Skin Clinic and Hair
  Repair Clinic let the prescriber choose the base [sweep]. None of the 12 UK companies in this set offers a choice of
  base for the same formula.
- The clinical evidence supports switching to a propylene-glycol-free foam when the solution irritates (licensed
  route) (`foundations/clinical-men.md`, section 11).

**Feel and use**

- Customers complain about greasy or smelly products (about 5% of negative reviews), scalp irritation (about 7%)
  and faulty sprays or pumps (about 7%) (`foundations/customer-voice.md`, 3.5). Named examples: Hims spray nozzles
  that clog; Manual's (now Voy) 10% solution that stayed sticky; a Hair + Me serum that smelled and was labelled to
  expire after one month.
- Voy's women's ads sell feel: "absorbs in seconds, with no greasy roots and no rewashing" [docs].
- Scents: Hims spray (eucalyptus), Strut (peppermint), Ro shampoo (peppermint) [docs/code].
- Once-a-day use is a selling point for Voy (10% spray), Numan (triple spray), Sons and Hims UK sprays, Hair + Me
  drops and the US gels. Tretinoin is added in several mixes so that minoxidil can be used once a day. The only trial
  of this found it equal to, not better than, standard minoxidil (`foundations/clinical-men.md`, 3.4).

**Pets**

- Minoxidil on the skin or hair is toxic to cats and dogs. Cats Protection reported 20 cat poisonings in one year,
  and a US study of 211 animal cases found that 8 of 62 cats with signs of poisoning died
  (`foundations/customer-voice.md`).
- **Hair + Me** asks about pets, shows a warning and badges its capsules "Better for pet owners" [obs]. It is the only
  company in the set that does this on screen.
- **Hers UK** shows a pet-safety warning on its serum in the app [code]. A Voy testimonial chooses tablets because of a
  dog [docs].
- Reviewers report asking Hair + Me for tablets because of a cat and being sent drops anyway
  (`foundations/customer-voice.md`).

### 2.3 Combination pills and fixed doses

- Fixed-dose pills are sold by Hims UK, Voy, Sons and Hair + Me in the UK, and by Hims, Keeps, Happy Head and Strut
  in the US.
- **No trial of a fixed combination pill was found.** The MHRA says finasteride 1 mg must be stopped at once if
  depression or suicidal thoughts appear. With a combined pill the patient must stop everything, or keep taking a drug
  that should be stopped (`foundations/clinical-men.md`, section 9).
- **Dose flexibility varies.** Sons sells one strength per medicine [code]. Hims UK sells one strength per product
  (topical finasteride 0.3% with minoxidil 6% or 7%; chew with finasteride 1.1 mg and minoxidil 3 mg) [code]. Hair + Me
  has four oral minoxidil steps (0.5, 1.25, 2.5 and 5 mg) [docs]. In the US, Keeps sells 2.5 mg tablets in four pack
  sizes for 1.25 to 5 mg a day [obs], and XYON lists 0.5 mg to 5 mg [code/docs].
- **Unusual strengths with no licensed equivalent:** finasteride 1.1 mg and minoxidil 3 mg (Hims UK chew), finasteride
  1.2 mg (Hims US), spironolactone 44 mg (Hers UK). None of these matches a licensed tablet strength.
