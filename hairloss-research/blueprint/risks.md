# Risks and the compliance checklist before launch

Part of the final blueprint (see `plan.md` for the summary, evidence marks and labels). Likelihood and impact are our
judgement **[our view]**, from the research files. **Likelihood:** Low, Medium, High. **Impact:** Low, Medium, High,
Severe (Severe = harm to a patient, a criminal breach, or the end of the business). This is not legal or tax advice.

---

## 1. The three biggest risks

### Risk 1: precision costs more, and rivals have far more money

**The risk.** Photos, calls, a second pharmacist and follow-up cost clinician time: our contribution is about 30% of
revenue in year 1 [estimate, `launch.md` 5.3], against about 67% in the market's typical example, which spends only
about £1.50 a month on clinicians [market-economics 5.6]. Hims & Hers spent US$919m on marketing in 2025 and owns Hims
UK, ZAVA and Juniper UK [market-economics key facts 12, 13]. Rivals sell first orders at about half price
[key fact 22]. Each extra step can lose people: Sons† and Hair + Me win "easy process" praise because they never stop
anyone [engines-experience 4.2]. And "precision" is hard for a customer to see; LloydsPharmacy and Numan already show
results and prices before sign-in [judging 2].

**How we handle it.**
- Keep the typical case fast: about 5 minutes, phone photos, no call for Tier A.
- Fight where precision is visibly worth paying for: women, shedding, men worried about finasteride, people failed by
  tick-box services. **Do not fight Dr Fox or Oxford\* on price.**
- Win customers cheaply: guides, free tools, women's partnerships, GP goodwill, reviews. Track CAC by channel; scale a
  paid channel only when its payback is under about 12 months.
- Protect year 2, where contribution rises by about a third: honest 6-month verdicts, reminders, reliable delivery.
- The lean set-up first; the price floor rule every 6 months.

### Risk 2: clinical and regulatory liability

**The risk.** Minoxidil tablets and spironolactone are off-label; any finasteride spray is unlicensed in the UK; the
MHRA strengthened finasteride warnings on 11 May 2026; the GPhC now tests verification and second checks; advertising
breaches are criminal [uk-regulation key facts 10, 15, 16, 21]. A single serious adverse event or a ruling would hurt a
brand whose promise is care.

**How we handle it.**
- Licensed first; every off-label or unlicensed choice backed by a recorded reason, a call, two blood pressure readings
  where needed, written information and a GP letter.
- Online ceilings (women's minoxidil tablets 2.5 mg; spironolactone 100 mg); no new minoxidil tablets at 66 or over.
- A second pharmacist checks every prescription; monthly audit; one automated test per rule.
- **Sign off the protocol only after reading NICE CKS, the BNF, UKTIS and the full oral minoxidil consensus.**
- Indemnity that names online prescribing, off-label use and any specials; tell the insurer in writing.
- The copy check on everything; no GSL product adverts at launch; legal review before launch.
- Specials delayed until a real recorded-need group exists; imported licensed spray route first.

### Risk 3: a small team, promises that depend on it, and supply

**The risk.** The owner may be prescriber, clinical lead and superintendent at once. Same-day side-effect replies,
one-day decisions, next-day replacements and "no gaps in treatment" all depend on a few people. Delivery and stock are
the main complaint in about 28% of negative reviews [customer-voice summary]. Women's low-dose minoxidil depends on
one tablet brand whose SmPC allows halving [clinical-women 3.2]. **A trust brand that breaks its own promises is hurt
more than a rival that never made them** [VT risk 3].

**How we handle it.**
- **Promise only what we measure**, and publish the measures every quarter, bad quarters included.
- **The capacity rule:** when reply times or review backlogs slip, new sign-ups pause behind a waiting list.
- A second pharmacist from day one and a bank of sessional prescribers trained on the protocol.
- Two wholesalers per line; buffer stock; never recommend what cannot ship; tell patients before any charge.
- Automate the dull, safety-critical parts (reminders, check-in scheduling, run-out warnings, rule tests).
- An incident process: any missed promise is logged, the patient is told and put right, the cause is fixed.

## 3. Compliance checklist before launch

Tick every line before the closed pilot, unless it says otherwise.

### 3.1 Registration, governance and people

- [ ] GPhC-registered pharmacy (own, partner or shared premises), superintendent, responsible-pharmacist procedures,
      SOPs (including remote supply and flammable goods), RP record kept five years [uk-regulation "You must"].
- [ ] Written risk assessment for the whole service, reviewed regularly, with an audit plan [uk-regulation 3.2].
- [ ] Second pharmacist in place to check every prescription; patients told in writing they may use another pharmacy.
- [ ] Indemnity (personal and pharmacy) naming online prescribing, off-label use and any specials; insurer told in
      writing; product liability; cyber.
- [ ] Written CQC advice that the pharmacist-only service is out of scope.
- [ ] No sales targets or per-prescription pay for prescribers.
- [ ] Website shows the pharmacy's GPhC number, owner, superintendent, each prescriber's name, registration number and
      that they are a pharmacist, and how to complain [uk-regulation key fact 17].

### 3.2 Clinical protocol

- [ ] NICE CKS, the BNF, UKTIS and the full oral minoxidil consensus read; every dose checked before it goes into
      software.
- [ ] Ceilings written in: women's minoxidil tablets 2.5 mg; spironolactone 100 mg; no new minoxidil tablets at 66+.
- [ ] Definitions written: "effective contraception"; the ketoconazole-with-minoxidil rule; PHQ-2 and distress
      cut-offs from the published tools. [reviewed: corrected] Sources now in the engine for effective contraception
      (MHRA 2019, rule W9) and the PHQ-2 (3 or more, Kroenke 2003); the clinical lead still signs both off.
- [ ] Gender-affirming hormones: call-only route, protocol in progress.
- [ ] Yellow Card workflow; side-effect channel separate from customer service.
- [ ] One automated test per rule; route tests; the engine walked against the protocol.
- [ ] Records hold: ID check, history, photos, two-way contact (or why not), options discussed licensed first, reasons
      for off-label use, recorded need for any special, written information given, GP sharing, monitoring plan
      [uk-regulation 5.5].

### 3.3 Advertising and website

- [ ] Home page: condition and service only; indicative condition price; no POM names; no "Buy now"; no free offers.
- [ ] Domain and name contain no POM; no "Doctor" in the name.
- [ ] Every template through the copy check and signed off by the regulatory adviser.
- [ ] Result screen reviewed by the regulatory adviser.
- [ ] Landing-page rule built into every advert's first screen.
- [ ] **No GSL product adverts** until written legal advice says how (or the channel is dropped).
- [ ] LegitScript (or NABP) and Google certification before any paid search.
- [ ] Fair Deal wording reviewed (care, not results; replacements and swaps "at no extra cost").

### 3.4 Data

- [ ] ICO fee paid; DPIA done (covers the anonymous stage, health data and biometric ID); records of processing;
      Article 6 basis plus Article 9(2)(h); UK or equivalent hosting; privacy notice; retention policy
      [uk-regulation key fact 23].

### 3.5 Consumer and subscription

- [ ] Terms built to the DMCCA draft and checked against the final text: pre-contract information, reminders 7 days
      before every charge, two-tap cancel by the same route as joining, refunds within 14 days.
- [ ] Cooling-off rules for dispensed medicines settled with a solicitor.
- [ ] Review invitations sent to every customer at the same points; no fake or selectively gathered reviews.

### 3.6 Supply

- [ ] Two wholesaler quotes for every licensed line; prices checked against the price floor rule.
- [ ] Foam pack lengths confirmed (men's foam at 2 g a day; women's foam at 1 g a day).
- [ ] Courier contract for tracked, flammable goods; delivery areas known.
- [ ] Laboratory accredited and contracted; ID vendor and payment provider contracted.

### 5.10 Result

- **Items checked: 101.** **Confirmed: 60** (some with a small clarification noted in the row). **Corrected: 40.**
  **Removed: 1** (item 23).
- **[to verify] items resolved by this review:** lactose (3.7 item 3), trimethoprim (item 6), spironolactone strengths
  and "effective contraception" (item 7), the PHQ-2 cut-off (item 20, first half), erection tablets with oral
  minoxidil, iron's legal class.
- **Still open:** NICE CKS, the BNF, UKTIS and the full consensus paper (3.7 items 1 and 2); the testosterone unit;
  the distress threshold; a "fast pulse"; blood-donor deferrals; the ketoconazole-with-minoxidil rule; CQC advice on
  blood sampling and whether finger-prick potassium is reliable (new 3.7 item 25).

**The corrections that matter most.**

1. **Men's scalp minoxidil:** both men's SmPCs say not to use it when there is no family history, and contraindicate
   high blood pressure in the foam as well as the solution; the engine did not ask men about family history and
   treated arrhythmia in men as counselling only.
2. **Red flags that did not lead to the right stop:** marked itch and boggy swellings had no route; new androgen signs
   after the menopause and Cushing's or acromegaly signs were routine, not urgent; distress and possible eating
   disorders were flagged but not referred; and no high blood-pressure reading triggered any action.
3. **Contraception:** condoms alone were allowed through as a FLAG for spironolactone and oral minoxidil; the MHRA
   says such methods should not be relied on alone.
4. **Missing contraindications:** soya or peanut allergy for dutasteride; the lactose conditions for finasteride and
   Loniten; a logic clash that would have blocked the women's two-medicine plan.
5. **Advertising:** the product file must never be published; the "diagnosis" trust line, "free swap" and a free
   shampoo inside a free route would each breach the medicines advertising rules if used publicly.
