# Judging the three concepts: which company to build

Written 24 November 2026 for the owner, a UK pharmacist independent prescriber. This file marks three draft
company concepts, lists the errors in each, decides what to build, and gives the writer a brief for the final
blueprint.

**The three drafts judged (all in this folder):**

- **CP**: `clinical-precision.md`, "the clinical precision company" (diagnose first, licensed-first ladder,
  measured outcomes).
- **WF**: `women-first.md`, "women first, men equally well".
- **VT**: `value-trust.md`, "the value and trust company" (low all-in monthly price, fair terms).

**Also read for the marking:** `synthesis/cross-company.md` in full; the "You must", "You can" and "You cannot say or
do" lists, key facts and verification log of `foundations/uk-regulation.md`; and targeted checks in
`foundations/clinical-men.md`, `foundations/clinical-women.md`, `foundations/market-economics.md`,
`synthesis/engines-experience.md`, `synthesis/products-prices.md` and `market/uk-sweep.md`.

---

## 0. How sure is this judgement?

- **No new web research was done.** Every check below is against the project's own files.
- **Weaker evidence, marked the same way as the drafts.** Voy\*, Hers (US)\* and Oxford Online Pharmacy\* could not
  be walked live (bot checks). XYON‡ was never independently verified. Sons† and Ro† were still in final review when
  the work started (their stage 2 data is complete). For **every** company, what happens after the sign-in wall is
  described from public documents or served code, not watched. Where a mark below leans on one of these, it says so
  (see section 7).
- **The money figures are estimates.** No file holds a wholesale price, a courier rate or a UK rival's cost to win a
  customer. When this file re-works a draft's numbers, the result is labelled **[our estimate]**.
- **NICE CKS, the BNF and UKTIS were never read** by the research. All three drafts say so. None of the doses below
  is final until they are read.

### Words used

- **Contribution:** what is left from a customer's monthly payment after the medicine, post, card fees, clinician time
  and similar direct costs. It pays for staff, premises, technology and marketing.
- **COGS (cost of goods sold):** what the medicine and packaging cost us to buy.
- **CAC (customer acquisition cost):** marketing money spent to win one paying customer.
- **Payback:** months of contribution needed to earn back the CAC.
- **Tier A / Tier B:** Tier A is questions plus photos, reviewed by a prescriber. Tier B adds a call, a checked blood
  pressure, blood tests where needed and a GP letter. Every off-label tablet is Tier B.
- **Off-label:** a licensed medicine used outside its licence (for example minoxidil tablets for hair).
  **Unlicensed special:** a medicine made for one patient with no licence.
- **GSL:** general sale list, a licensed medicine any shop may sell (for example minoxidil 5% foam).
- **POM:** prescription-only medicine. **TE (telogen effluvium):** sudden shedding about 3 months after a trigger.
- **Reg 286:** regulation 286 of the Human Medicines Regulations 2012. A medicine advert may not offer "to provide a
  diagnosis or suggest a treatment by post or by means of an electronic communications network". CAP Code rule
  12.2.1 says the same for the ASA.

## 1. The verdict in one paragraph

All three drafts share the same strong skeleton, taken from the same research: stop questions before contact
details, the result and full price before sign-in, licensed medicines first, separate medicines (no fixed mixes),
5% minoxidil at most, photos for every new patient, a follow-up schedule, a crisis screen that cannot be passed, and
monthly plans with an easy exit. They differ in the lead idea and in how honest the money is. **CP** has the safest
and most complete clinical engine and a mid-market price, but its costs for branded scalp products are too low.
**WF** has the best women's pathway and the clearest market gap, but two logic errors in its engine and a brand that
may push men away. **VT** fixes the most customer complaints, but its prices leave almost nothing to run the
business (about £4.23 a customer a month in year 1 on its own figures), and its main selling point, price, is the
hardest thing to advertise for prescription medicines. **Build CP as the base, give it WF's women's pathway as an
equal front door, and wrap it in VT's fair-deal service promises at CP's price level.**

## 2. Scores (1 = poor, 10 = excellent; hard marking)

| Criterion | CP | WF | VT |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Clinical evidence and patient safety | **8** | **7** | **7** |
| (b) UK legal feasibility (specials, advertising, GPhC distance guidance, CQC) | **7** | **6** | **7** |
| (c) Real difference from the 19 competitors | **8** | **9** | **6** |
| (d) Unit economics and pricing | **5** | **5** | **3** |
| (e) Feasible for a small team led by a pharmacist prescriber | **6** | **6** | **7** |
| (f) Fixes the top customer complaints | **7** | **7** | **9** |
| (g) Appeal to women **and** men | **7** | **6** | **8** |
| **Total (out of 70)** | **48** | **46** | **47** |

The totals are close. The decision (section 4) rests on **which weaknesses can be fixed by grafting**. CP's weak
marks (money, workload) are fixable with real quotes and a few cuts. VT's weak mark (money) is built into its brand
promise. WF's best part (the women's pathway) can be lifted into CP whole.

### Reasons for each score

**(a) Clinical evidence and safety**

- **CP 8.** The most careful engine. It splits scarring signs from ordinary dandruff and from psoriasis (B1), routes
  men with sudden shedding to a no-medicine shedding programme instead of a dead end (B3, C5), asks the trigger
  question as a timing question, records a sexual-function baseline, needs a verified blood pressure before oral
  minoxidil, and has written stop rules. Doses and figures match the fact-checked files (checked: finasteride 1 mg;
  minoxidil 5% solution 1 ml twice daily and foam 18 to 49; oral minoxidil men start 2.5 mg, range 1.25 to 5 mg;
  women start 1.25 mg, range 0.625 to 5 mg; dutasteride half-life 3 to 5 weeks and sperm count 23% down at 24 weeks;
  spironolactone 50 mg then monthly steps to 200 mg; the licensed-first order of GN14 Appendix 2). Lost marks: no
  online ceiling for women's oral minoxidil or for spironolactone (both run to the top of the published range
  online).
- **WF 7.** The best women's safety detail: a Cushing's and acromegaly question, an eating-intake question,
  hairline and eyebrow photos for every woman of 45 or over, the tirzepatide and pill rule, GP consent asked before
  the result, two blood pressure readings on different days, and sensible online ceilings (spironolactone 100 mg,
  women's oral minoxidil 2.5 mg). Lost marks for two engine errors (section 3.2, items 2 and 3): men with sudden
  shedding hit a dead end, and ordinary dandruff-type scalp redness is sent for urgent referral.
- **VT 7.** Sound and well sourced (same rule library), with good extra touches: "not losing hair means no
  prescription", a date-of-birth re-entry check, a weight baseline before tablets, a safe hand-over for people
  treated elsewhere. Lost marks: Tier B calls budgeted at 10 to 15 minutes, a non-standard PHQ-2 threshold, and a
  route to make finasteride specials in-house with no handling caution.

**(b) UK legal feasibility**

- **CP 7.** Pharmacist-only (no CQC), licensed first with GN14 recorded need, MHRA hierarchy for an imported licensed
  spray before a UK special (correct: GN14 Appendix 2), second-pharmacist check, GP sharing, DMCCA-ready terms. Lost
  marks: it lists adverts for licensed GSL minoxidil as a channel feeding "the free hair check" without flagging
  reg 286 / CAP 12.2.1; it lets Wales continue in the engine (A3) while its own launch plan says "Wales after
  advice".
- **WF 6.** Equally careful in most places (and it alone notes that an English service treating patients in Scotland
  was not researched). Lost marks: its licensed-product advert row says "the advert leads to our consultation", which
  is what reg 286 and CAP 12.2.1 forbid, one row after it cites reg 286 itself.
- **VT 7.** Flags CAP 12.2.1 correctly and keeps product adverts and the consultation offer apart; has a six-question
  copy check. Lost marks: it serves Wales at launch without the caveat the regulation file gives; it does not tell
  patients they may take a prescription to another pharmacy (a "You must"); and its whole pitch is the price of
  prescription treatment, which Appendix 6 lets a clinic show only as an indicative price for the condition on the
  home page, and on inner pages only as a factual list that must not "actively encourage viewers to choose a product
  based on the price".

**(c) Real difference from the 19 competitors**

- **CP 8.** No company in the set diagnoses first, publishes a licensed-first ladder, verifies blood pressure, or
  measures outcomes (cross-company 4.6, 5). But "precision" is hard for a customer to see, and LloydsPharmacy and
  Numan already show results and prices before sign-in.
- **WF 9.** The women's gap is the clearest gap in the research: 6 of the 12 UK companies treat men only, no UK
  company sells stepped spironolactone, no UK women's pathway acts on periods, PCOS, menopause, HRT or contraception
  (cross-company 4.9). Hers UK could copy it in time (WF says so).
- **VT 6.** Monthly with no prepayment is different from the subscription brands, but not from Dr Fox, Boots,
  Superdrug or Oxford\*, which sell one-off orders. Dr Fox and Oxford\* stay cheaper for a tablet alone. A rival with
  far more money can cut its own price faster than we can.

**(d) Unit economics and pricing**

- **CP 5.** Prices sit at the honest middle of the market (finasteride £20, two medicines £32 to £38). But it uses one
  22% COGS figure for everything, which comes from companies that make their own compounded products. For branded
  licensed foam this is far too low (section 3.1, item 1). Its blended year-1 contribution of about £11 is more like
  **about £9 [our estimate]**. Free calls on request and a free shedding programme add uncosted time.
- **WF 5.** Honest about its weak spot (the women's foam, at a cautious 50% COGS). But it leaves out ID checks,
  packaging, technology and refund costs, prices men's foam (twice the daily amount) below women's foam, and assumes
  35% of subscribers on women's Tier B tablets from day one. Its £10.70 to £12 blended contribution is more like
  **about £8 to £9 [our estimate]**.
- **VT 3.** On its own figures: about £4.23 contribution a customer a month in year 1 (22%), foam plans at or below
  zero even on optimistic costs, break-even at about 2,650 to 5,200 active patients, and "about £0.5 million before
  break-even" at a £50 CAC that no file supports. Its "no plan over £24" and 12-month price lock leave no room to
  correct.

**(e) Feasibility for a small team**

- **CP 6.** Good phasing (Tier A first, Tier B at months 3 to 6, specials later) and a cap on new patients. Heavy
  extras: a free prescriber call for anyone, blinded second-reviewer photo ratings, a yearly public outcomes report,
  sourcing an imported spray.
- **WF 6.** About 30 screens for a woman; a women's Tier B pathway with calls, two BP readings, U&E, potassium plans
  and contraception checks at every supply; partnerships and heavy content from month 2. All right, but labour-heavy
  for one prescriber.
- **VT 7.** The most practical launch: a 100-patient closed pilot, a lean set-up (share a registered pharmacy),
  per-parcel costing, a capacity rule that pauses sign-ups before promises break. Its promises (2-hour side-effect
  target, next-day replacements, quarterly public report) are heavy for its thin margin.

**(f) Fixes the top customer complaints**

The complaints (customer-voice, via cross-company 4.10): cancelling or renewals in 35% of negative reviews; main
complaint in a hand-read sample: delivery or stock about 28%, "no results" about 22%, subscription or refund about
18%; "refunds refused" in 24%; early shedding nobody warned about 9%; side effects handled by sales; unexplained holds
6%; greasy, irritating or faulty products 5% to 7%.

- **CP 7** and **WF 7.** Monthly default, two-tap cancel, reminders, pay only on approval, early-shed warning, a
  clinical side-effect channel, ship with 3 weeks left, diagnosis against "no results". Less on product feel and
  faulty packs.
- **VT 9.** Built around the complaint list: a plain "Fair Deal" promise, one free swap of base or form, free faulty
  pump replacement, next-day replacement of lost parcels, one payment retry only, an honest 6- and 12-month verdict,
  a public service report.

**(g) Appeal to women and men**

- **CP 7.** Even-handed, with a full women's pathway; the clinical tone may feel cold.
- **WF 6.** "Show women in every image", names such as "The Parting": strong for women, weaker for men, who are the
  main customers of most rivals in this research (6 of the 12 UK companies serve only men).
- **VT 8.** "Fair price, named pharmacist, leave any time" speaks to both sexes equally.

## 3. Errors and weak points found

Severity: **Must fix** (safety, law or a number that changes the decision), **Should fix**, **Minor**.

### 3.1 Clinical precision (CP)

1. **Must fix: COGS for branded scalp products is wishful.** CP plans every product at 22% of its price (3.3, 7.2).
   That benchmark comes from Skin + Me and Hims UK, which sell their own compounded products. For licensed branded
   stock the files point much higher: men's Regaine foam retails at £20.33 a month on 6 cans at Dr Fox; the women's
   once-a-day foam is listed at £34.99 for 2 months (UK sweep, not fact-checked), so about £17.50 a month retail.
   CP plans £5.28 (men's foam) and £5.72 (women's foam). If branded foam costs about £12 a month (WF's cautious
   figure) and about a third of patients use foam [our assumption], CP's blended year-1 contribution falls from about
   £11 to **about £9 [our estimate]**; payback at a £100 CAC moves from about 11 to about 14 months.
2. **Must fix: licensed GSL product adverts and reg 286.** CP 6.3 lists adverts for minoxidil foam, solution and
   Regaine for Women "within their licence", with the consultation as the landing page. A medicine advert may not
   offer diagnosis or treatment online (reg 286; CAP 12.2.1). The product advert and the consultation offer must be
   kept apart, on legal advice, or the channel dropped.
3. **Should fix: no online ceilings for women's Tier B doses.** W3 runs oral minoxidil to 5 mg and W4 spironolactone
   to 200 mg online. WF's ceilings (2.5 mg and 100 mg, higher only with GP or specialist input) are safer for a remote
   service that cannot examine.
4. **Should fix: Wales is inconsistent.** Engine A3 lets Wales continue; the launch plan says Wales only after advice.
   The regulation file says Welsh rules were not checked.
5. **Should fix: the optional 3-monthly plan "about 10% less"** breaks the "one price" idea customers value and that
   the other drafts keep. Not unlawful, but drop it.
6. **Should fix: uncosted or risky extras at launch:** a free prescriber call for anyone on request, blinded photo
   re-rating, and a yearly public **outcomes** report (CP itself says outcome figures for prescription medicines need
   legal advice before publishing). Replace with VT's service report and an internal audit at launch.
7. **Minor: "a verified blood pressure, which no company in the set checks".** Engines-experience says no company was
   seen to **verify** a reading, but Oxford\* says in its documents that it does blood-pressure checks for oral
   minoxidil. Say "no company was seen to verify a reading".

### 3.2 Women first (WF)

1. **Must fix: the licensed-product advert row breaks reg 286.** 6.3 says the minoxidil product advert "leads to our
   consultation". That is offering treatment through an electronic network in a medicine advert (reg 286; CAP 12.2.1).
   The row above it cites reg 286 correctly, so the draft contradicts itself.
2. **Must fix: a dead end for men with sudden shedding.** Question 2.10 stops every man whose loss started suddenly or
   is all over (STOP-REFER). Yet 3M.3 and W5 say men with shedding get the shedding check. No man can reach it. CP's
   route (sudden shedding with a trigger goes to a free shedding programme, no medicine) is right.
3. **Must fix: urgent referral for ordinary scalp problems.** Question 2.1 lumps "very itchy, red, scaly around the
   hairs" with pain, pustules and crusting, and sends "Yes" for urgent referral. Seborrhoeic dermatitis and psoriasis
   would be sent urgently, and question 5.8 (psoriasis or eczema: no scalp minoxidil, tablets possible) can then never
   be reached. Use CP's B1, which separates scarring signs, dandruff and diagnosed psoriasis or eczema.
4. **Should fix: men's foam priced below women's foam.** M2 foam is £20 with a 22% COGS (£3.74 is the figure used for
   the whole M2 card), while the women's foam is £24 at a 50% cautious COGS. Men use 2 g a day, women 1 g. Men's foam
   needs the same caution.
5. **Should fix: the economics leave out costs.** No ID check, packaging, technology or refund (promise) costs; the
   blended mix puts 35% of subscribers on women's Tier B tablets while Tier B is capped at launch. Realistic blended
   contribution is about £8 to £9 [our estimate], not £10.70 to £12.
6. **Should fix: £29 for the shedding check.** It earns about £6.91 per check on WF's own figures, and charging
   someone to be told "this will usually settle by itself" works against the trust it is meant to build. CP and VT make
   it free, with blood tests charged only when needed.
7. **Should fix: crisis call "within one working day".** For thoughts of self-harm in the last month, offer a call
   the same day (VT), with 999, NHS 111 and Samaritans on the screen as WF already does.
8. **Minor: one ruling misattributed.** 5.9 says "the ASA ruled against ... Hims UK (2023)" on a regrowth refund. The
   Hims UK money-back ruling of January 2023 was by the **MHRA** (regulation 287); the Numan rulings (2022, 2024) were
   ASA (uk-regulation 2.5).
9. **Minor: brand.** "Women first" imagery and names like "The Parting" risk losing men; WF itself says "less obvious
   for men".

### 3.3 Value and trust (VT)

1. **Must fix: the price list does not pay for the promises.** £14 finasteride, £15 scalp minoxidil, £16 any off-label
   tablet, £24 for any two, "no plan over £24", price locked for 12 months. On VT's own assumptions the blended year-1
   contribution is about £4.23 (22%); the foam plans are at about zero; a woman on spironolactone and foam (a Tier B
   plan with a call, U&E and contraception checks) earns £3.84. VT's own sensitivity shows medicine costs 50% higher
   than assumed would leave about £0.60.
2. **Must fix: COGS assumptions are optimistic for foam.** £8 a month for men's foam against £20.33 retail a month at
   Dr Fox; £8 for women's foam against about £17.50 retail a month. With branded foam nearer £12, every foam plan
   loses money in year 1 [our estimate].
3. **Must fix: the main selling point is the hardest to advertise.** The pitch is a cheaper price for prescription
   treatment. On the home page, in search and in social posts the law allows only an indicative condition price ("from
   £14"); naming rivals' prescription plans and prices risks being read as promoting a POM (VT says so). The
   difference customers would notice cannot be shouted.
4. **Should fix: a CAC target with no basis.** "£50 or less per paying patient" is a hope; no UK rival publishes CAC,
   and the market file's planning range starts at £60. At £50 VT's own payback is about 12 months.
5. **Should fix: Wales at launch.** VT serves England, Scotland and Wales from day one; the regulation file says Welsh
   rules were not checked.
6. **Should fix: missing "You must" item.** Patients are not told they may take their prescription to another
   pharmacy (GPhC prescriber guidance; uk-regulation "You must").
7. **Should fix: PHQ-2 threshold.** B11 acts if **either** item is "more than half the days". The PHQ-2 is scored as a
   total of both items; the clinical lead should take the cut-off from the published tool, not invent one.
8. **Should fix: in-house finasteride specials.** M6 lists making it under the pharmacy exemption (Route A) with no
   caution. CP notes finasteride powder must not be handled by pregnant women and recommends buying specials in.
9. **Minor: year-1 price comparisons lean on weak data.** The Voy\* figures come from search summaries and older pages;
   Numan's first-order cap on annual billing is unconfirmed; Sons† prices are Black Friday code readings.

### 3.4 Problems shared by all three

- **Paid-search landing pages.** The ASA upheld against Voy (September 2025) for a no-name advert whose landing page
  talked about "weight loss prescriptions". The first screen of the hair check, when it is the landing page, must talk
  about the condition and the service only. Medicines appear only on the result screen, after the questions.
- **The result screen names POMs before sign-in.** All three treat it as part of a consultation the person chose to
  start. WF rightly asks for a regulatory adviser to review it. Keep that step.
- **"No GP consent means scalp products only"** (all three exclude finasteride too). This is stricter than the GPhC
  minimum (record why it is still safe), but defensible because the MHRA asks prescribers of finasteride to review the
  medical record. Keep it, and say so plainly on screen.
- **Unread references.** NICE CKS, the BNF, UKTIS and the full oral minoxidil consensus paper must be read before any
  dose goes into software.
- **The cost-of-goods hole.** No supplier quote exists. Every price stays provisional until two wholesalers quote.

## 4. The decision

### 4.1 Base concept: Clinical precision (CP)

Why:

1. It scores highest on the three things hardest to bolt on later: safety, legal design and a real difference.
2. Its price level (mid-market, monthly, below rivals' long-plan prices) is the only one of the three that can pay for
   photos, calls, a second pharmacist and follow-up.
3. Its weak points are fixable: real COGS, cut a few costly extras, add ceilings, fix two legal lines.

Why not the others as the base: WF's lead idea is best used as a **front door**, not the whole brand, because the
service must also win men. VT's low price is its brand, so it cannot be raised later without breaking the promise.

### 4.2 Graft in from Women first (WF)

1. **The women's pathway as an equal front door**, with its own landing pages and content (two front doors, one
   service and one engine).
2. **Online ceilings:** spironolactone up to 100 mg online, higher only with GP or dermatologist agreement; optional
   25 mg start for women aged 45 or over or with risk factors; women's oral minoxidil up to 2.5 mg a day online; the
   0.625 mg alternate-day start (an approximate quartered dose, said so) for women aged 65 to 74.
3. **GP registration and consent asked before the result screen** (WF 1.8), so the result is honest about whether
   tablets are possible.
4. **Extra women's questions:** Cushing's or acromegaly signs (2.7); eating much less or worried about eating (3W.3a);
   "had a baby in the last 12 months" pre-ticking childbirth as a trigger (1.5); crown tenderness or breakage in
   tightly curled hair (3W.5a); new face or body hair or acne after the menopause (4.4).
5. **Hairline and eyebrow photos required for every woman in perimenopause, after the menopause or aged 45 or over**
   (4.2).
6. **The tirzepatide and oral contraceptive rule** (5.12: switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4
   weeks after starting and after each dose rise, as the Mounjaro SmPC says) before spironolactone or minoxidil
   tablets.
7. **Two blood pressure readings on different days, each photographed**, before oral minoxidil (WF 4.12 E).
8. **The free pregnancy, trying-to-conceive and new-mum route (W7) and the traction route (W8)** as named products.
9. **Women's content and partnerships:** guides on shedding after a baby, perimenopause, PCOS, weight-loss
   injections, iron and thyroid, traction and textured hair; a free shedding-timeline tool with no email; referral
   links with postnatal, menopause, PCOS and weight-management services (condition and service only).
10. **Talk to 15 to 20 women before launch** and test prices (the UK women's voice in the research is thin).

### 4.3 Graft in from Value and trust (VT)

1. **The Fair Deal promise** (VT 5.8), worded about care, never results: pay only if approved; full refund before
   dispensing; a 90-day satisfaction refund of the most recent payment with no conditions; side-effect refund and
   billing paused; tracked delivery and a free replacement of a lost or late parcel; free replacement of a faulty pump
   or can; one free swap of base or form in the first 3 months if clinically suitable; an honest 6- and 12-month
   photo verdict.
2. **One price per calendar month whatever the delivery rhythm**; no first-order, retention or plan-length discounts.
3. **The price floor rule** (VT 7.4): every plan must clear a minimum contribution at real quotes, or its price rises
   at the next review with 30 days' notice and the right to leave.
4. **Product feel:** choose liquid or foam at the same price; an application card and a short how-to video in every
   first parcel; ask about washing and styling routine before choosing.
5. **Engine extras:** "not losing hair" ends politely with no prescription and a free photo tracker (C1);
   date-of-birth re-entry must match; weight baseline before tablets (E11); safe hand-over for people still being
   treated elsewhere (C7); one payment retry only; "Talk to a prescriber first" button on the result screen.
6. **The trust line on scalp minoxidil:** "Minoxidil 5% is on general sale. You can buy it in a shop without us.
   People choose us for the diagnosis, the follow-up and the photo reviews."
7. **Free tools for anyone:** photo tracker, "shedding or thinning?" explainer, printable GP summary made on the
   person's own device.
8. **A quarterly public service report** (reply times, parcels on time, time to cancel, refunds, complaints): service
   facts, not medicine claims.
9. **The capacity rule:** when reply times slip past target, new sign-ups pause behind a waiting list.
10. **The six-question copy check** for every advert and page, with the reg 286 fix added (section 3.1, item 2).
11. **A 100-patient closed pilot before public launch**, and the lean set-up option (share an existing registered
    pharmacy's premises).
12. **VT's per-parcel cost lines** in the economics: packaging, dispensing and second check, ID check, promise
    costs.

### 4.4 Drop

- **From CP:** the 10% 3-monthly discount; the free call for anyone without a capacity rule; blinded photo re-rating
  and the public outcomes report at launch (keep both as internal audit until legal advice); adverts for GSL minoxidil
  that lead into the consultation; the single 22% COGS figure.
- **From WF:** the £29 shedding-check fee; women-only brand imagery and names that exclude men (keep women-first
  content); question 2.10's dead end for men; the lumped scalp question 2.1; the licensed-product advert row.
- **From VT:** the £14, £15, £16 and £24 price list, "no plan over £24" and the 12-month price lock; price-led
  marketing and named-rival price comparisons; the 2-working-hour side-effect target (keep "same working day"); the
  in-house finasteride special route; the £50 CAC target as a plan figure; Wales at launch.
