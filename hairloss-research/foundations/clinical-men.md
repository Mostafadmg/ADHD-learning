# Male pattern hair loss: clinical evidence review

**For:** a UK pharmacist independent prescriber planning an online hair-loss service with compounded products.
**Written:** 23 November 2026. **Scope:** men with male pattern hair loss (also called androgenetic alopecia, AGA).

**Rules used in this file**

- Every figure has a source link beside it (PubMed, PMC, MHRA, EMA, emc and others). Sources were opened and read on
  23 November 2026. Where a figure came from a news report or a search summary and not the original, the text says so.
- Where a figure could not be found it says **unknown** or **unverified**. Nothing is invented.
- Company press releases are labelled as such. They are not peer-reviewed and should be treated as provisional.
- "Compounded" means a medicine made up to a prescriber's order. In UK law this is an unlicensed medicine (a "special").
  A special has no marketing authorisation, so the prescriber carries more of the responsibility.

**How to read the numbers**

- Most trials count hairs in a small tattooed circle of scalp, usually 1 cm². A typical balding vertex has about
  150 to 200 hairs per cm² at baseline (201 in the topical finasteride trial, 151 in the minoxidil 5% trial).
  Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34634163/ and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc
- So "+15 hairs/cm² over placebo" means roughly a 7 to 10% gain in hair number. That is real but modest. The bigger
  practical benefit of treatment is **stopping further loss**: in the finasteride trials the placebo group lost
  239 hairs from a 5.1 cm² circle over five years (https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc).
- Most of the trials cited here measured a spot on the crown (vertex), so the figures say less about the frontal
  hairline.
- A network meta-analysis (NMA) ranks treatments by combining head-to-head and indirect comparisons. It is useful for
  order of size, but many of the hair-loss trials in these networks are small, so the rankings are not precise.

## 1. The short version

1. **Two treatments have strong evidence and a UK licence for male pattern hair loss:** oral finasteride 1 mg daily
   (prescription only, private) and topical minoxidil 5% twice daily (on general sale). Everything else is weaker,
   off-label or unlicensed.
2. **Oral dutasteride 0.5 mg is the most effective single drug in network meta-analyses,** ahead of finasteride 1 mg
   by about 7 hairs/cm² at 24 weeks. In the UK it is licensed only for prostate enlargement, so use for hair is off-label.
   Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/
3. **Low-dose oral minoxidil was not better than topical minoxidil 5% in the two head-to-head trials that included
   men.** Its value is convenience and adherence, not extra power. It is off-label and needs cardiovascular screening.
   Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598226/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43047224/
4. **Topical finasteride works about as well as the tablet over 24 weeks with far lower blood levels, but it is not
   risk-free.** It still lowers blood DHT by about a third, and regulators have received reports of the same sexual
   and mood effects seen with tablets. No licensed topical finasteride was found on the UK emc on 23 November 2026.
   Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34634163/ and
   https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded
5. **The main safety duty for a remote prescriber is the finasteride and dutasteride mood and sexual-function warning.**
   The MHRA acted in April 2024 (patient card) and again in May 2026 (stronger warnings, dutasteride added). The
   European review finished in May 2025. Details are in section 10.

## 2. 5-alpha reductase inhibitors (finasteride and dutasteride)

**Why they work.** Testosterone is turned into dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by an enzyme called 5-alpha reductase.
DHT shrinks genetically sensitive scalp follicles ("miniaturisation"). Blocking the enzyme lowers DHT and slows or
partly reverses the shrinking. Men born without the type 2 enzyme do not get male pattern hair loss.
Source: finasteride 1 mg SmPC, section 5.1, https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc

### 2.1 Oral finasteride 1 mg

**How it works (one line).** Blocks type 2 5-alpha reductase, so less testosterone becomes DHT in the scalp and blood.

**Best evidence**

| Study | Design | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kaufman 1998 (two phase III trials) | 1,553 men aged 18 to 41, finasteride 1 mg vs placebo, 1 year, then 1,215 continued a second year | Hair count in a 5.1 cm² circle (baseline 876 hairs): +107 hairs vs placebo at 1 year and +138 vs placebo at 2 years (both P < .001). Placebo group kept losing hair. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777765/ |
| Five-year extension | Same trials, up to 5 years | At 5 years: finasteride +38 hairs from baseline, placebo −239 hairs from baseline (5.1 cm² area). On blinded photographs 48% of finasteride men were improved and a further 42% unchanged; only 25% of placebo men were improved or unchanged. | SmPC section 5.1: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11809594/ |
| Mella 2010 systematic review | 12 randomised trials, 3,927 men | Patient-rated improvement: relative risk 1.81 short term (number needed to treat 5.6) and 1.71 long term (NNT 3.4). Hair count rose 9.4% more than placebo short term and 24.3% long term. Erectile dysfunction relative risk 2.22 (number needed to harm 82). Stopping because of sexual effects was no more common than with placebo (RR 0.88). Evidence quality: moderate. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20956649/ |
| Gupta 2022 network meta-analysis (JAMA Dermatology) | 23 trials of minoxidil, finasteride and dutasteride in men | Finasteride 1 mg gave the largest gain in **terminal** (thick) hair count at 48 weeks: 32.1 hairs/cm² more than topical minoxidil 2% and 26.2 more than topical minoxidil 5%. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/ |

Plain meaning: about half of men see visible regrowth and about nine in ten at least hold their ground over five
years. Without treatment most men get visibly worse over the same time. Caution: by year 5 the placebo hair-count
figure rests on only 15 men (n = 219 on finasteride), so the 5-year placebo number is imprecise
(SmPC section 5.1, link above).

**Dose.** 1 mg once a day, with or without food. Higher doses do not work better. Expect 3 to 6 months before loss
steadies. If treatment stops, the gain starts to reverse by 6 months and is back to baseline by 9 to 12 months.
Source: SmPC section 4.2, https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc

**Side effects (from the SmPC, https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc)**

- In the three 12-month trials: decreased libido 1.8% vs 1.3% on placebo; erectile dysfunction 1.3% vs 0.7%;
  reduced ejaculate volume 0.8% vs 0.4%. All drug-related sexual effects together: 3.8% vs 2.1% in the first year.
- Uncommon: depression. Frequency not known: anxiety, **suicidal ideation**, palpitations, raised liver enzymes,
  breast tenderness or enlargement, testicular pain, blood in semen, infertility.
- **Sexual dysfunction that continues after stopping** has been reported after marketing (frequency not known).
- Male breast cancer has been reported. Ask men to report any lump, pain, breast enlargement or nipple discharge.
- A meta-analysis of 15 placebo-controlled trials (4,495 men) found a relative risk of sexual dysfunction of 1.66
  (95% CI 1.20 to 2.30) for finasteride 1 mg. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30206635/

**Mood and suicidality: what the evidence shows**

- World Health Organization pharmacovigilance data (VigiBase): 356 reports of suicidality in finasteride users.
  The reporting signal was raised overall (reporting odds ratio 1.63) and stronger in men under 45 (3.47) and in men
  treated for hair loss (2.06), but not in older men treated for prostate enlargement. The authors note that publicity
  can drive reporting. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33175100/
- A cohort of men aged 66 and over found no increase in suicide (hazard ratio 0.88) but more self-harm (HR 1.88) and
  depression (HR 1.94) in the first 18 months of a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor. Absolute increases were small: 17 and
  237 per 100,000 patient-years. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28319231/
- The European regulator confirmed suicidal ideation as a side effect of finasteride tablets in May 2025; frequency
  unknown. See section 10. Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/measures-minimise-risk-suicidal-thoughts-finasteride-dutasteride-medicines

**Contraindications and cautions (SmPC)**

- Women (risk to a male fetus) and anyone under 18.
- Do not combine with another 5-alpha reductase inhibitor.
- Crushed or broken tablets must not be handled by women who are or may be pregnant. Intact tablets are coated.
- Semen: less than 0.001% of the 1 mg dose per ejaculation; the SmPC judges this unlikely to harm a male fetus.
- Fertility: reports of poor semen quality that improved after stopping. In a randomised trial in 99 healthy men
  that used **finasteride 5 mg** (the prostate dose, not the 1 mg hair dose), total sperm count fell 34.3% at
  26 weeks; the fall was no longer significant at 52 weeks (−16.2%) or 24 weeks after stopping (−6.2%). No equivalent
  trial of the 1 mg dose was checked here. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17299062/ *(Corrected on
  verification, 23 Nov 2026: the earlier text did not say this trial used 5 mg.)*
- **PSA (the prostate blood test):** finasteride lowers PSA (mean 0.7 to 0.5 ng/ml at 12 months in men aged 18 to 41).
  The SmPC says doubling the PSA result "should be considered" before comparing it with normal ranges.

**Interactions.** Metabolised by CYP3A4. The SmPC says inhibitors and inducers will probably change levels but this is
unlikely to matter clinically. No important interactions are listed.

**Monitoring.** No blood tests are required. What is required is a mood and sexual-function check before starting
and at each review (section 10).

**UK status.** **Licensed for male pattern hair loss** (Propecia and generics), prescription only. The MHRA notes that
finasteride 1 mg is prescribed privately only. Trials were in men aged 18 to 41.
Sources: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2194/smpc and
https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/finasteride-and-dutasteride-updated-safety-warnings-for-psychiatric-side-effects-and-sexual-dysfunction

**Formulation notes.** A licensed tablet exists, so there is no clinical reason to compound oral finasteride 1 mg on
its own. Combination capsules (for example finasteride with minoxidil) are unlicensed specials and lose the
patient card and leaflet that come in the licensed pack; the service would need to supply the same warnings itself.

### 2.2 Topical finasteride

**How it works (one line).** Delivers finasteride to the scalp to lower scalp DHT while keeping blood levels low.

**The licensed European spray.** A finasteride 2.275 mg/ml cutaneous spray (0.25% w/w) has been authorised in
European countries since 2020 for the same indication as the tablet. Brand names seen in EMA and national lists
include Fynzur, Finjuve, Caretopic and Fincrezo. Almirall staff co-authored the phase III trial below.
Source: EMA assessment report EMA/PRAC/55203/2025,
https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/referral/finasteride-dutasteride-containing-medicinal-products-article-31-referral-assessment-report_en.pdf

From the French product information (Fincrezo, https://agence-prd.ansm.sante.fr/php/ecodex/rcp/R0422737.htm):

- Indication: mild to moderate male pattern hair loss in men aged 18 to 41.
- Dose: 1 to 4 sprays once a day on separate, non-overlapping bald areas. Each spray is 50 microlitres and delivers
  114 micrograms. Maximum 4 sprays (200 microlitres, about 0.46 mg) a day. Leave on for at least 6 hours.
- Vehicle: ethanol, propylene glycol, hydroxypropyl chitosan and water.
- Blood levels more than 100 times lower than the 1 mg tablet; relative bioavailability about 2 to 3%.
- Contraindicated in women. Pregnant women and children must not touch the product or the treated scalp.
- The product information says there is **no clinical experience beyond 6 months**.

**Phase III trial (Piraccini 2022).** 458 men at 45 European sites were randomised to topical finasteride, placebo or
oral finasteride 1 mg for 24 weeks. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34634163/ (full text
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297965/)

| Outcome at 24 weeks | Topical finasteride | Placebo | Oral finasteride 1 mg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Change in target-area hair count (1 cm², baseline about 201 hairs) | +20.2 | +6.7 (P < 0.001) | +21.1 |
| Same outcome, sensitivity analysis (the figures used in the product information) | +16.3 | +6.3 | +18.7 |
| Fall in blood DHT | 34.5% | no change | 55.6% |
| Peak blood finasteride at week 24 | 48 pg/ml | n/a | 5,029 pg/ml |
| Treatment-related sexual side effects | 2.8% | 3.3% | 4.8% |
| Itching / redness (treatment-related) | 2.2% / 2.2% | 0.6% / 0% | 1.2% / 0% |

Limits to keep in mind:

- Only 250 of the 458 men (54.6%) had usable hair counts, and 135 did not finish. This weakens the result.
- It lasted 24 weeks. There is no long-term efficacy or safety trial.
- It was not designed to prove that topical equals oral. "Numerically similar" is the honest wording.
- Patients' own rating of hair growth did not differ between groups (French product information above).

**Dose matters for blood DHT.** An earlier 7-day study that applied the 0.25% solution **twice** daily found blood
DHT fell by 68 to 75%, the same as the tablet (62 to 72%), even though blood finasteride was much lower. So more
volume or more frequent use removes the "low systemic effect" advantage.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25074865/

**Compounded topical finasteride (alone or with minoxidil)**

- A small trial (40 men, 24 weeks) of finasteride 0.25% mixed with minoxidil 3% beat minoxidil 3% alone on hair
  density, hair diameter and photographs (all P < 0.05), with about a 5% fall in blood DHT.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29972712/
- A 2018 systematic review found only seven studies of topical finasteride, all showing benefit, and called the
  evidence limited. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29601622/
- **Regulator warning.** On 22 April 2025 the US FDA warned about compounded topical finasteride after 32 adverse
  event reports (2019 to 2024) including depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation, brain fog, fatigue, insomnia,
  reduced libido and testicular pain; most said the symptoms continued after stopping. Some patients had been told a
  topical product carried no risk. The FDA also warned about accidental exposure of others, "specifically females",
  through transfer of the applied product. (The FDA alert does not mention children; the advice to keep children
  away comes from the European product information, https://agence-prd.ansm.sante.fr/php/ecodex/rcp/R0422737.htm.)
  Source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded
- The European 2025 review found no evidence linking the licensed skin spray to suicidal ideation and made no change
  to its product information. Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/measures-minimise-risk-suicidal-thoughts-finasteride-dutasteride-medicines

**UK status.** **Unlicensed special.** A search of the emc on 23 November 2026 listed ten
finasteride products, all tablets (https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/search?q=finasteride). A 2025 peer-reviewed paper
states topical finasteride had not been approved by the MHRA (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207719/).
On verification (23 November 2026) the MHRA products register listed only finasteride 1 mg and 5 mg tablets under
the substance finasteride, and a search for "Finjuve" returned nothing
(https://products.mhra.gov.uk/substance/?substance=FINASTERIDE). One UK pharmacy website claims Finjuve has a UK
licence; the register does not support this. Re-check the register before launch, because new licences can appear.

**Formulation notes**

- The licensed spray is a low-volume, once-daily hydroalcoholic solution. A compounded lotion that
  mixes finasteride into a minoxidil base and is dosed like minoxidil (1 ml per application) is a different thing.
  At 0.25%, 1 ml contains 2.5 mg of finasteride: more than five times the licensed spray's maximum daily dose of
  about 0.46 mg, and more again if used twice a day as in the published combination trial
  (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29972712/). This is arithmetic from the figures above. The safety data for the
  licensed spray therefore **do not transfer** to higher strengths and volumes.
- Systemic absorption for a given compounded vehicle is **unknown** unless the compounder has tested it.
- Stability data for finasteride in minoxidil/propylene glycol/ethanol bases should be requested from the specials
  manufacturer; no published UK beyond-use data were found (**unknown**).
- Counsel on transfer: wash hands, let the scalp dry, cover the pillow, and keep partners who are or may be pregnant
  and children away from the treated scalp.

### 2.3 Oral dutasteride

**How it works (one line).** Blocks both type 1 and type 2 5-alpha reductase, lowering blood DHT further than
finasteride (94% vs 73% in a head-to-head study in healthy men of dutasteride 0.5 mg vs finasteride **5 mg**,
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17299062/).

**Best evidence**

| Study | Design | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gubelin Harcha 2014 | 917 men aged 20 to 50; dutasteride 0.02, 0.1 or 0.5 mg, finasteride 1 mg or placebo; 24 weeks | Dose-dependent rise in hair count and width. Dutasteride 0.5 mg beat finasteride 1 mg on hair count (P = .003), hair width (P = .004) and frontal photographs (P = .002). Side-effect rates similar across groups. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24411083/ |
| Zhou 2019 meta-analysis | 3 trials, 576 men, dutasteride vs finasteride, 24 weeks | Total hair count mean difference 28.57 hairs in favour of dutasteride (95% CI 18.75 to 38.39). No significant difference in altered libido, erectile dysfunction or ejaculation disorders. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30863034/ |
| Gupta 2022 NMA | 23 trials | Dutasteride 0.5 mg had the largest gain in total hair count at 24 weeks: 7.1 hairs/cm² more than finasteride 1 mg (95% CI 5.1 to 9.3). | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/ |
| Gupta 2025 NMA | 33 trials, 19 comparators | Dutasteride 0.5 mg ranked first for total hair density at 24 weeks (SUCRA 96.3%). | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40586152/ |
| Korean post-marketing study | 712 men aged 18 to 41, mean 205 days | Drug reactions in 9.3%: decreased libido 1.3%, impotence 1.0%, gynaecomastia 0.3%. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27489426/ |

The exact hair counts for each arm of the Gubelin Harcha trial were not re-checked from the full paper, so they are
not quoted here.

**Dose.** 0.5 mg once a day is the dose studied and the dose approved for hair loss in Japan, Taiwan and South Korea
(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207719/). Lower doses (0.1 mg) worked less well in the dose-ranging trial.

**Side effects, contraindications, interactions (Avodart SmPC, https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc)**

- Same class effects as finasteride: impotence, reduced libido, ejaculation disorders, breast tenderness or
  enlargement. Meta-analysis relative risk for sexual dysfunction 1.37 (95% CI 0.81 to 2.32), not statistically
  significant but based on few trials. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30206635/
- **Very long half-life: about 3 to 5 weeks** at steady state. Side effects, and the drug in blood and semen, take
  months to clear.
- Semen: at 52 weeks total sperm count fell 23%, semen volume 26% and motility 18%; sperm count was still 23% below
  baseline 24 weeks after stopping. The SmPC says reduced male fertility cannot be excluded.
- The SmPC recommends a condom if the partner is or may be pregnant.
- **PSA falls by about 50% after 6 months.** A new baseline is needed and any confirmed rise from the lowest value
  should be investigated.
- Contraindicated in women, children and adolescents and (per the SmPC) severe liver impairment. Capsules must not be
  handled if leaking: the drug is absorbed through skin.
- Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (for example ritonavir, itraconazole, oral ketoconazole) can raise dutasteride levels.
- Mood: the product information now carries a precautionary warning about depressed mood, depression and suicidal
  ideation seen with finasteride (section 10).

**UK status.** **Licensed for something else and used off-label.** Avodart and generics are licensed only for benign
prostatic hyperplasia. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc

**Practical notes.** Dutasteride comes as a liquid-filled soft capsule that must be swallowed whole and not opened
(SmPC), so doses below 0.5 mg cannot be made by splitting; they would need a compounded (unlicensed) product.
A sensible place for dutasteride is second line, for men who have had a proper trial of finasteride (12 months) and
want more, after a fresh consent discussion that covers the longer half-life, fertility and the off-label status.
That placing is a judgement based on its licence status, not a trial finding.

### 2.4 Topical and mesotherapy dutasteride

**How it works (one line).** Tries to put dutasteride into the scalp (by solution, or by many small injections called
mesotherapy) to avoid whole-body exposure.

**Evidence**

- **Mesotherapy vs oral.** In the 2025 network meta-analysis, oral dutasteride 0.5 mg was significantly better than
  dutasteride 0.05% mesotherapy (mean difference 9.2 hairs/cm², 95% CI 5.9 to 12.6).
  Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207719/
- **Real-world mesotherapy.** A Spanish retrospective study of 541 patients (mostly 3-monthly sessions): response on
  mesotherapy alone could be judged in only 86 patients, of whom 38.4% had marked improvement. Pain was the main side
  effect (45.5%). No sexual side effects were recorded. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816059/
- **Topical solution, phase II (company-funded, India).** 135 men; dutasteride 0.01%, 0.02% or 0.05% solution vs
  placebo vs oral finasteride 1 mg for 24 weeks. Hair count rose with dose; 0.05% beat oral finasteride on hair count
  (P = 0.0083). Changes in blood testosterone and DHT were described as modest. Published in Cureus; all authors are
  employees of the manufacturer. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40909044/
- **Microneedling plus topical dutasteride:** one small randomised placebo-controlled study (research letter).
  Effect size not extracted (**unknown** here). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35648446/

**UK status.** **Unlicensed.** No topical or injectable dutasteride product is listed on the UK emc
(https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/search?q=dutasteride). Mesotherapy is a clinic procedure and does not fit a
remote service.

**Formulation notes.** Dutasteride is absorbed through skin (the capsule SmPC warns about this). Blood levels after long-term use of compounded topical dutasteride are **unknown**; with a half-life of weeks,
small daily absorbed amounts could build up. Evidence grade: weak.

## 3. Minoxidil

**Why it works.** Minoxidil is a pro-drug. An enzyme in the hair follicle (sulfotransferase) turns it into minoxidil
sulfate, the active form, which pushes resting follicles back into the growth phase. People vary a lot in how much of
this enzyme their follicles have, which is one reason some people do not respond to the lotion.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30974011/ and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc (section 4.4)

### 3.1 Topical minoxidil 2%, 5% and higher strengths

**How it works (one line).** Applied to the scalp, it prolongs the growth phase of follicles; it does not block DHT.

**Best evidence**

| Study | Design | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Olsen 2002 | 393 men aged 18 to 49; 5% solution vs 2% solution vs vehicle, twice daily, 48 weeks | Change in non-vellus hair count per cm² at 48 weeks: **+18.6 (5%), +12.7 (2%), +3.9 (vehicle)**. 5% gave 45% more regrowth than 2% and worked sooner, with more itching and irritation. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12196747/ and SmPC table https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc |
| Same trial, time course | | The gain peaks early and then falls back: 5% solution +35.3 hairs/cm² at 16 weeks, +29.0 at 32 weeks, +18.6 at 48 weeks. | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc |
| Olsen 2007 (foam) | 352 men aged 18 to 49; 5% propylene-glycol-free foam vs placebo foam, 16 weeks | **+21.0 vs +4.3 hairs/cm²** at week 16 (P < .0001). Well tolerated over 52 weeks. No efficacy data after 16 weeks. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17761356/ and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5781/smpc |
| Adil 2017 meta-analysis | Randomised trials of good or fair quality | 5% and 2% minoxidil in men were each superior to placebo (P < .00001). | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28396101/ |
| Gupta 2022 NMA | 23 trials | For thick (terminal) hair at 48 weeks, finasteride 1 mg beat topical minoxidil 5% by 26.2 hairs/cm². | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/ |
| Ghonemy 2021 (10% vs 5%) | 90 men; 10% vs 5% vs placebo, 36 weeks | **5% was better than 10%**, and 10% caused marked irritation and more shedding. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31403367/ |

Plain meaning: 5% is the right strength. No controlled trial in men was found showing that 10% or 15% works better,
and the only trial of 10% found it worse and more irritating. Strengths above 5% are unlicensed and not supported.

**Dose (licensed).** Solution: 1 ml twice a day to a dry scalp, maximum 2 ml a day, men aged 18 to 65. Foam: 1 g
(half a capful) twice a day, maximum 2 g a day, men aged 18 to 49. Allow 2 months or more to see growth; stop if no
improvement after one year; regrown hair is lost 3 to 4 months after stopping.
Sources: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5781/smpc

**Side effects and cautions (SmPCs above and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100306/smpc)**

- Itching, dermatitis, scalp irritation. More with 5% than 2%.
- A temporary increase in shedding in the first 2 to 6 weeks, as resting hairs are pushed out by new ones. Warn
  every patient before they start.
- Unwanted hair elsewhere (hypertrichosis) and headache. The product information also lists chest pain,
  palpitations, fast heartbeat, low blood pressure and swelling (peripheral oedema).
- **Contraindicated in the Regaine 5% solution SmPC (section 4.3) in people with treated or untreated high blood
  pressure,** in women, in anyone sensitive to minoxidil, ethanol or propylene glycol, on any scalp abnormality
  (including psoriasis and sunburn), on a shaved scalp, and with occlusive dressings or other scalp medicines.
  Sudden or patchy loss is a section 4.4 warning in the Regaine SmPC (use only for androgenetic alopecia) and a
  listed contraindication ("sudden or uneven hair loss") in the generic axunio 5% spray SmPC, which also lists
  burns and scarring (https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100306/smpc). *(Clarified on verification,
  23 Nov 2026: wording differs between products.)*
- **Infants:** excess hair has been reported in infants after skin contact with a carer's treated scalp. It reversed
  within months once contact stopped. Keep children away from the application site.
- Accidental swallowing is dangerous: 2 ml of 5% solution contains 100 mg of minoxidil.

**Interactions**

- The SmPC says minoxidil "should not be used concomitantly with other medications applied topically on the scalp".
  Topical tretinoin, corticosteroids, dithranol and petrolatum can increase its absorption.
  Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc
- Low-dose aspirin may blunt the response: in 24 people, predicted responders fell from 50% to 27% after 14 days of
  aspirin (a small enzyme study, not a clinical outcome trial). Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30226287/

**Systemic absorption.** About 1 to 2% of the applied dose from normal scalp. The 5% solution delivers about twice as
much to the blood as the 5% foam (AUC 18.71 vs 8.81 ng·hr/ml). Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc

**UK status.** **Licensed for male pattern hair loss.** The 5% solution and 5% foam are on the General Sales List.
Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc

**Formulation notes**

| Product | Propylene glycol | Ethanol | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regaine for Men Extra Strength 5% solution | 500 mg/ml | 280.53 mg/ml | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc |

- **Propylene glycol is the usual cause of true allergy.** In patch-tested patients with dermatitis from minoxidil
  solution, propylene glycol, not minoxidil, was the culprit in most. Alternatives named were butylene glycol,
  polysorbate and glycerol, but a negative patch test does not guarantee the compounded product will be tolerated.
  Anyone truly allergic to minoxidil itself cannot use any topical minoxidil.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11807448/
- A licensed propylene-glycol-free option (the foam) already exists, so "PG-free" is not by itself a reason to compound.
- In a 2026 randomised trial, contact dermatitis occurred in 11% on topical minoxidil and 0% on oral.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43047224/
- Shelf life of the licensed solution is 36 months (SmPC). Stability of compounded multi-drug solutions is **unknown**
  unless the specials manufacturer supplies data.

### 3.2 Low-dose oral minoxidil (LDOM)

**How it works (one line).** The same drug taken as a small daily tablet, so every follicle on the body is exposed.

**Head-to-head trials against topical minoxidil**

**Dose-finding and other evidence in men**

- **2.5 mg vs 5 mg (Carvalho Fonseca 2026):** 100 men, double-blind, 24 weeks. 5 mg was not superior to 2.5 mg;
  photographs improved in 64% vs 62% (P = .386). Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40962189/ (no abstract).
  The figures come from a news summary of the paper, not the paper itself, so treat them as provisional:

- **Open-label 5 mg (Panchaprateep 2020):** 30 men, 24 weeks, total hair count +35.1 hairs/cm²; hypertrichosis in 93%,
  ankle swelling in 10%. No control group. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32970299/
- **Network meta-analysis 2022:** oral minoxidil 5 mg ranked first for terminal hair at 24 weeks and was
  43.6 hairs/cm² better than oral 0.25 mg, so very low doses look weak in men. This ranking came before the
  head-to-head trials above, which did not confirm an advantage of 5 mg over topical. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/
- **Pipeline:** an extended-release oral minoxidil (VDPHL01) has positive phase III results reported by the company;
  see section 7.

**Pericardial effusion (fluid round the heart)**

- At blood-pressure doses the SmPC reports pericardial effusion, sometimes with tamponade, in about 3 to 5% of
  patients not on dialysis. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc
- At hair-loss doses there are case reports (for example https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36117778/ and
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36425662/).
- US adverse-event reports show a reporting signal even at 2.5 mg or less (reporting odds ratio 13.30; 5.34 in people
  without heart impairment) and at 1.25 mg or less (16.41). This is a signal, not a rate. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39327649/
- A cross-sectional ultrasound study of 100 patients found small, symptom-free effusions in 5.8% on LDOM and 6% of
  controls (no difference). Small study. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39231077/
- The true rate at low dose is **unknown**. Screen for breathlessness, chest pain, new swelling or sudden weight gain.

**International expert consensus (Akiska 2025; 43 hair specialists, 12 countries).**
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/ (open copy read at
https://escholarship.org/content/qt2pp1b5gc/qt2pp1b5gc_noSplash_71a7c4141708a558a1b509800bdb7742.pdf)
*Verification note (23 Nov 2026): the abstract confirms the panel (43 specialists, 12 countries), that dosing,
contraindications, precautions, baseline tests and monitoring reached consensus, and that titration did not. The
open copy would not load on re-check, so the dose figures and lists below are confirmed only by secondary summaries
(for example https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/oral-minoxidil-for-hair-loss-expert-consensus-statement), not
first-hand. Read the full paper (doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2024.4593) before writing them into a protocol.*

- **Adult men:** most common starting dose 2.5 mg daily; range 1.25 to 5 mg daily. (Adult women: start 1.25 mg;
  range 0.625 to 5 mg.) No agreement was reached on how to step doses up.
- **Contraindications:** history of pericardial effusion or tamponade; history of pericarditis; congestive heart
  failure; pulmonary hypertension with mitral stenosis; phaeochromocytoma; pregnancy or breastfeeding; another
  medicine with a significant interaction.
- **Precautions:** history of fast heart rate or other arrhythmia; blood pressure below 90/60 mmHg; kidney
  impairment; dialysis.
- **Tests:** with no precautions, routine baseline bloods and ECG are **not** indicated. If a precaution is found,
  consider them with a specialist (primary care or cardiology).
- **Monitoring:** check for side effects at the start and at each dose increase; monitor blood pressure if a
  precaution exists.
- LDOM may be preferred to topical when topical causes irritation, residue or styling problems, has stopped
  working, or is not being used reliably.

**Licensed product information (blood-pressure use).** Oral minoxidil is licensed only for severe hypertension, to be
given with a diuretic and a beta-blocker. It is contraindicated in phaeochromocytoma. It can cause salt and water
retention, reflex fast heart rate and hypertrichosis in most patients at those doses. Effects add to other
blood-pressure-lowering medicines. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc

**UK status.** **Licensed for something else and used off-label.** Tablets of 2.5 mg, 5 mg and 10 mg are licensed
(https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13821/smpc).
The 2.5 mg tablet is scored and "can be divided into equal halves", so 1.25 mg, 2.5 mg and 5 mg can all be given
with the licensed product. Smaller doses and combination capsules need a compounded, unlicensed product.

**Formulation notes.** Compounded capsules combining oral minoxidil with finasteride or dutasteride have no trial
evidence as a fixed combination (none found; **unknown**). They also make it impossible to stop one drug quickly if a
side effect appears, which matters for the finasteride mood warning.

### 3.3 Sublingual minoxidil

**How it works (one line).** A small tablet or drop dissolved under the tongue, intended to be absorbed through the
mouth lining rather than swallowed.

**Evidence**

- **Phase 1B, placebo-controlled (Bokhari 2022):** published as a research letter; figures not extracted.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34420241/
- **Dose response on hair thickness (Sinclair 2025):** 33 men in a 24-week placebo-controlled trial of 0.45 mg, plus
  12 in an open extension on 1.35 mg or 4.05 mg. Mean fibre diameter changed by −2 µm on placebo, −2 µm on 0.45 mg,
  +3 µm on 1.35 mg and +6 µm on 4.05 mg. Only the two higher doses were significant.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39774750/
- **Sublingual 5 mg vs oral 5 mg in men (Sanabria 2025, double-blind, 24 weeks):** similar vertex improvement (42% vs
  40%, P = 0.69); palpitations 0% vs 9% (P = 0.048). Figures as summarised in a later network meta-analysis:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12207719/ (original letter: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39688293/).
- In that network meta-analysis, sublingual 5 mg and oral 5 mg did not differ for terminal hair density
  (mean difference −2.3 hairs/cm², 95% CI −24.1 to 19.5).

Plain meaning: sublingual minoxidil looks about as effective as the oral tablet at the same dose. The claim that it is
gentler on the heart rests on one small trial.

**UK status.** **Unlicensed special.** No sublingual minoxidil product is licensed in the UK (none on the emc search of
23 November 2026: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/search?q=minoxidil+tablets).

**Formulation notes.** How much is absorbed through the mouth and how much is simply swallowed is **unknown** for any
compounded product. Treat safety screening exactly as for oral minoxidil.

### 3.4 Minoxidil with tretinoin

**How it works (one line).** Tretinoin (a vitamin A acid) raises the follicle enzyme that activates minoxidil and may
increase its penetration.

**Evidence**

- **Shin 2007:** 31 men; minoxidil 5% plus tretinoin 0.01% **once** daily vs minoxidil 5% **twice** daily. No
  significant difference in hair counts or global ratings. Irritation in 5 of 15 vs 4 of 14.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17902730/
- **Enzyme study (Sharma 2019):** after 5 days of topical tretinoin, 43% of people predicted not to respond to
  minoxidil were re-classified as likely responders. This is a laboratory marker, not a hair-count outcome.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30974011/

Plain meaning: the combination lets a once-daily application match the standard twice-daily lotion in one small
trial. It has **not** been shown to beat minoxidil 5% twice daily.

**Cautions.** The minoxidil SmPC lists tretinoin among agents that can increase minoxidil absorption, and says
minoxidil should not be used with other medicines applied to the scalp. In the Shin trial about a third of men in
each arm had itching or irritation. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc

**UK status.** **Unlicensed special** as a combination.

**Formulation notes.** Stability of tretinoin in a hydroalcoholic minoxidil base is **unknown** here; ask the specials
manufacturer for data and an expiry date based on testing.

## 4. Adjuncts and other topical agents

These are the ingredients most often added to compounded "all-in-one" lotions. None has evidence close to
finasteride or minoxidil.

### 4.1 Ketoconazole shampoo

**How it works (one line).** An antifungal that reduces scalp yeast (Malassezia) and has some anti-inflammatory
action; it also lowered scalp sebum in the study below.

**Evidence**

- Piérard-Franchimont 1998: ketoconazole 2% shampoo improved hair density and the size and proportion of growing
  follicles "almost similarly" to minoxidil 2%. The authors said the clinical significance "awaits further
  controlled study in a larger group". Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9669136/
- Fields 2020 systematic review: only five human studies (318 participants). Increased hair shaft diameter and
  better photographs were reported. Conclusion: promising add-on, randomised trials needed.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31858672/
- Khandpur 2002 (open randomised study, 100 men, 1 year): finasteride plus ketoconazole shampoo did better than
  minoxidil 2% alone, but that arm had only 10 patients. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12227482/
- No hairs-per-cm² effect size versus placebo from a randomised trial was found (**unknown**).

**Dose.** Licensed use for dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis: wash twice weekly for 2 to 4 weeks, leaving it on for
3 to 5 minutes before rinsing; then once every 1 to 2 weeks to prevent relapse. No dose for hair loss is established.
Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1510/smpc

**Safety.** No ketoconazole is detectable in blood after scalp use. In 22 trials (2,890 people) no side effect
reached 1%. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1510/smpc

**UK status.** **Licensed for something else** (dandruff, seborrhoeic dermatitis, pityriasis versicolor); use for hair
loss is off-label. Nizoral 2% Shampoo is listed as prescription only on the emc; Nizoral Anti-dandruff Shampoo (2%,
dandruff only) is on the General Sales List. Sources: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1510/smpc and
https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6456/smpc

**Practical note.** Worth using when the man also has dandruff or seborrhoeic dermatitis; a flare of seborrhoeic
dermatitis is one of the common causes of itching and scaling on minoxidil solution
(https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11807448/). As a hair-growth agent by itself the evidence is weak. Adding
ketoconazole to a leave-on compounded lotion has no trial evidence (**unknown**).

### 4.2 Topical anti-androgens in development

These block the androgen receptor in the follicle instead of lowering DHT. No product of this kind licensed for
hair loss in the UK or EU was found (November 2026).

### 4.3 Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost, bimatoprost) and the PGD2 blocker setipiprant

**How they work (one line).** Latanoprost and bimatoprost are glaucoma eye drops known to thicken and lengthen
eyelashes, so they have been tried on the scalp; setipiprant is an oral prostaglandin D2 receptor blocker.

- **Latanoprost 0.1%:** one pilot trial in 16 men with mild hair loss; treated mini-zones had higher hair density at
  24 weeks than placebo zones (P = .0004).
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21875758/
- **Bimatoprost:** no published randomised trial in male pattern hair loss was found in PubMed (**unknown**).
- **Setipiprant (oral, 1,000 mg twice daily):** phase 2a trial in 169 men **failed**; no improvement in hair count or
  self-assessment vs placebo. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34703265/
- The Japanese guideline evaluated unapproved topical bimatoprost and latanoprost and does not include them among
  its recommended treatments. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863806/

**UK status.** No scalp product is licensed; any scalp preparation would be an **unlicensed special**. Evidence
grade: insufficient.

### 4.4 Topical cetirizine

**How it works (one line).** An antihistamine thought to lower prostaglandin D2 and inflammation around follicles.

- 2022 systematic review: two randomised trials and one non-randomised trial, all 1% cetirizine, moderate to high
  risk of bias. Probably better than placebo; **less effective than minoxidil** for total hair density.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35976065/
- 2026 meta-analysis (8 studies): benefit was confined to placebo-controlled studies. No clear advantage against
  minoxidil or when added to minoxidil. Pooled effects on total and terminal hair density were not significant.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43044787/

**UK status.** **Unlicensed special** for scalp use. Evidence grade: weak. Adding it to minoxidil is not supported.

### 4.5 Topical melatonin

- Evidence is mostly uncontrolled before-and-after studies. In 35 men, hair
  density rose from 123 to 173 per cm² at 6 months in the 55 to 58% who responded; no control group. Topical use did
  not change blood melatonin. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23766606/
- A 2026 network meta-analysis of off-label products ranked topical minoxidil 5% first, with topical melatonin among
  "other effective" options, based on few small trials. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42512824/

**UK status.** Not a licensed medicine for hair loss (no UK licence was found; products are sold as cosmetics).
Evidence grade: weak.

### 4.6 Caffeine

- One open-label randomised trial (210 men, 6 months): caffeine 0.2% liquid was "not inferior" to minoxidil 5% on a
  hair-root test (growing-phase hairs improved by 10.59% vs 11.68%). It was not blinded and used a trichogram
  (hair-root) measure, not hair counts. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29055953/
- No placebo-controlled hair-count trial of modern quality was found (**unknown**).

**UK status.** Not a licensed medicine; sold in cosmetic products. Evidence grade: weak to insufficient. It should
not replace minoxidil.

### 4.7 Adenosine

- Randomised double-blind trial in 102 Japanese men, twice daily for 6 months: adenosine lotion beat a niacinamide
  lotion on global improvement and on the share of thick hairs (60 µm or more), P < 0.05. No comparison with
  minoxidil in this trial. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25925959/
- Recommended as an option in the Japanese guideline. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863806/

**UK status.** Not a licensed medicine; a cosmetic ingredient. Evidence grade: weak.

### 4.8 Saw palmetto, pumpkin seed oil and rosemary oil

- **Saw palmetto vs finasteride (open-label, 100 men, 2 years):** improved in 38% on saw palmetto 320 mg vs 68% on
  finasteride 1 mg. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23298508/
- **Saw palmetto systematic review (2020):** five randomised trials and two cohorts of mixed products; robust
  high-quality data are lacking. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33313047/
- **Pumpkin seed oil 400 mg daily (76 men, 24 weeks, double-blind):** mean hair count rose 40% vs 10% on placebo
  (P < 0.001). One trial; no replication was found. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24864154/
- **Rosemary oil vs minoxidil 2% (100 patients, 6 months):** no difference in hair count; both rose from baseline.
  No placebo arm and the weaker minoxidil strength. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25842469/

**UK status.** Food supplements or cosmetics. Evidence grade: weak (saw palmetto is clearly inferior to finasteride).
Because saw palmetto and pumpkin seed oil are claimed to act on 5-alpha reductase, it is reasonable to ask about
sexual side effects, but no rate from trials was found (**unknown**).

## 5. Procedures and devices

### 5.1 Microneedling as an add-on

**How it works (one line).** Tiny needle punctures are thought to trigger wound-healing growth signals and may help
lotions penetrate.

- **Dhurat 2013:** 100 men; weekly microneedling plus minoxidil 5% vs minoxidil 5% alone, 12 weeks. Mean change in
  hair count 91.4 vs 22.2. 82% vs 4.5% reported more than 50% improvement. Evaluator-blinded only; no sham.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23960389/
- **Meta-analysis 2025:** 12 randomised trials, 631 patients. Microneedling plus minoxidil beat minoxidil alone on
  hair count (standardised mean difference 1.32, 95% CI 0.73 to 1.92) with high heterogeneity (I² 88%). Needle depth
  (1 mm or less vs more than 1 mm), device type and duration made no detectable difference. More (mild) adverse
  events with needling: 74 vs 59. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40056230/
- **Network meta-analysis 2023:** minoxidil 5% plus microneedling ranked first (SUCRA 95.8%) and added 13 hairs/cm²
  over minoxidil alone at 24 weeks. Microneedling **alone** ranked below minoxidil.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38058547/

**Cautions.** No trial was found that measured how much microneedling raises blood levels of minoxidil or
finasteride applied straight afterwards (**unknown**). In the Dhurat trial a 1.5 mm roller was used in clinic once a
week, and men were told not to apply minoxidil on the day of needling and to restart 24 hours later
(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3746236/). That is a sensible rule to copy, above all for lotions that
contain finasteride or dutasteride.

Evidence grade: moderate as an add-on to minoxidil (many small trials, consistent direction, weak blinding).

### 5.2 Low-level laser (light) devices

**How they work (one line).** Low-power red light is thought to stimulate energy production in follicle cells.

- **Jimenez 2014 (four sham-controlled, double-blind trials; device used three times a week):** terminal hair
  density at 26 weeks rose by 18.4 to 25.7 hairs/cm² with the laser comb in men vs 1.6 to 9.4 with the sham device.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24474647/
- **Meta-analysis 2019 (11 double-blind trials):** hair density increased vs sham (standardised mean difference
  1.316, 95% CI 0.993 to 1.639); comb and helmet devices both worked; heterogeneity was high.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30706177/
- A 2018 network meta-analysis ranked laser first but rated the laser-vs-sham evidence **very low** quality.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29797431/
- A 2024 network meta-analysis found oral 5-alpha reductase inhibitors more effective than photobiomodulation.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38725143/

**UK status.** Devices, not medicines; bought by the patient. Evidence grade: moderate (a real effect in
sham-controlled trials, but high heterogeneity, and one network meta-analysis rated the evidence very low quality).

### 5.3 Platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

**How it works (one line).** The patient's own concentrated platelets are injected into the scalp to deliver growth
factors.

- **Meta-analysis 2023 (9 randomised trials, 238 patients):** hair density higher than placebo at 3 and 6 months;
  hair count and diameter not significantly different from placebo. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37533146/
- **Meta-analysis 2024 (13 trials, 431 patients):** +27.55 hairs/cm² (95% CI 14.04 to 41.06) but I² 96%, low-quality
  evidence and clear publication bias. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39013743/
- **Gentile 2015 (half-head, 20 analysed):** +45.9 hairs/cm² in total density after three monthly sessions.
  Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26400925/

**UK status.** A clinic procedure; not a medicine. Not deliverable remotely. Evidence grade: weak to moderate.
Reviewers have called for standardised protocols (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29797431/), so results cannot be
assumed from one clinic to the next.

## 6. Nutritional correction

**Principle (a practical judgement).** Male pattern hair loss is driven by DHT, not by a vitamin shortage. Blood
tests are for the man whose story suggests something else as well: sudden diffuse shedding, a restrictive diet,
weight loss, bowel disease, or loss that does not follow a male pattern.

| Nutrient | What the evidence says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron and ferritin** | Almost all studies linking low iron stores to hair loss are in women, and results conflict. A review recommends replacing iron if a patient with hair loss is found to be iron deficient (more often women). No ferritin threshold for men with pattern hair loss was found (**unknown**). An unexplained low ferritin in a man should go to his GP to find the cause; that is general medical practice, not a hair-loss finding. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/ |
| **Vitamin D** | Meta-analysis of 23 studies: people with non-scarring hair loss had lower vitamin D (weighted mean difference −7.29) and more deficiency (odds ratio 3.11) than controls. This is an association. No randomised trial shows that vitamin D regrows hair in male pattern loss (**unknown**). Replace if deficient, on general health grounds. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38010941/ |
| **Zinc** | Hair loss is a sign of true zinc deficiency. For pattern hair loss the data are inconsistent and the review does **not** recommend screening. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30547302/ |
| **Biotin** | A review found 18 reported cases of benefit, all in people with an underlying deficiency or disorder, and no evidence for supplements in healthy people. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28879195/ |

**Biotin and blood tests (important).** High-dose biotin, common in "hair, skin and nails" supplements, interferes
with many laboratory immunoassays. The US FDA warned in 2017 and again in November 2019 that it can cause **falsely
low troponin** results (a missed heart attack) and reported one death linked to this. Ask every patient about biotin
and tell them to mention it before any blood test.
Source: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference

**Service implication.** Selling biotin or a multi-vitamin as a hair-loss treatment is not evidence-based, and adding
biotin to a product creates a laboratory-test risk with no proven benefit.

## 8. Evidence ranking

**How the grades were set (this review's own scale).**

- **Strong:** several large randomised trials plus meta-analyses, a consistent hair-count benefit, and data to
  12 months or longer.
- **Moderate:** at least one adequately sized randomised trial, or several small ones pointing the same way, with
  clear limits (short, unblinded, industry-funded or indirect).
- **Weak:** small, single or poorly controlled trials.
- **Insufficient:** no adequate controlled evidence in men, or the controlled evidence is negative.

## 9. Combinations: what adds benefit and what only adds risk or cost

### Combinations with evidence of added benefit

| Combination | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral finasteride 1 mg + topical minoxidil 5%** | 450 Chinese men, 12 months, randomised: improved in 94.1% on both vs 80.5% on finasteride alone vs 59% on minoxidil alone. Meta-analysis of 5 randomised trials: better photographic scores and more "marked improvement" with the pair; no significant difference in hair-density change or side effects. In a 2025 network meta-analysis, finasteride plus minoxidil was the best minoxidil combination in men (SUCRA 80.18%). The finasteride SmPC notes there are no manufacturer data on using the two together. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26031764/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32166351/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41041440/ |
| **Topical finasteride 0.25% + topical minoxidil** | Three small trials. With minoxidil 3% (40 men, 24 weeks): better density, diameter and photographs than minoxidil alone. With minoxidil 5% (three-arm pilot): +81 hairs/cm² at 6 months; a good photographic score in 79% vs 41% on minoxidil alone and 8% on topical finasteride alone. Single-blind 12-week trial: response in 86.7% vs 69.1% (P = 0.006). | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29972712/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37798906/ ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39496123/ |
| **Minoxidil 5% + microneedling** | +13 hairs/cm² over minoxidil alone at 24 weeks in a network meta-analysis. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38058547/ |
| **Minoxidil + low-level laser** | Meta-analysis: higher hair count and better photographs than minoxidil alone. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32478968/ |
| **Minoxidil + platelet-rich plasma** | 6 trials, 343 patients: +9.14 hairs/cm² (95% CI 6.57 to 11.70) vs either alone. Clinic only. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38789807/ |

The logic that holds up: pair one drug that lowers DHT (finasteride or dutasteride) with one that stimulates growth
(minoxidil). Two drugs from the same group add little or nothing.

**What this means in practice**

1. A compounded or repackaged finasteride product has **no patient card in the box**. The service must give the same
   information in writing and be able to show that it did.
2. "Topical" is not a way round the warning. Blood DHT still falls by about a third with the licensed low-dose
   spray, and regulators have reports of the same effects with compounded lotions.
3. Online supply is exactly the setting the MHRA criticised. A tick-box form with no follow-up will not meet the
   "review regularly" expectation.

### 10.2 Screening before prescribing

**Every patient**

- **Is this really male pattern hair loss?** Gradual thinning at the temples and crown. Sudden, patchy or diffuse
  loss, or a red, scaly, scarred or painful scalp, is not suitable for remote treatment: the minoxidil SmPC excludes
  sudden or patchy loss and abnormal scalp. Ask for photographs. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100306/smpc
- Age 18 or over (all licences).
- Identity, GP details and consent to share. The pharmacy regulator expects prescribers working with online
  pharmacies to verify information independently for higher-risk medicines, to follow national safety alerts and
  off-label guidance, and to share information with the regular prescriber. Its list of medicines needing these
  safeguards is "not limited to" the examples given and does not name finasteride or minoxidil; applying the same
  safeguards to them is this review's cautious reading, in the light of the MHRA's comments on online finasteride.
  Source: https://assets.pharmacyregulation.org/files/2025-02/gphc-guidance-registered-pharmacies-providing-pharmacy-services-distance-february-2025.pdf
- Supplements, especially biotin (section 6).

**Before finasteride or dutasteride (any route)**

| Ask about | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Past or current depression, anxiety, self-harm or suicidal thoughts | MHRA requirement; decide with the patient whether to prescribe at all | MHRA 2024 and 2026 updates above |
| Current sexual function (libido, erections, ejaculation) | A baseline is needed to judge any later change; effects can persist after stopping | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc |
| Plans for a child; partner pregnant or trying | Semen changes with both drugs; dutasteride lasts months; the dutasteride SmPC advises a condom if the partner is or may be pregnant | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc |
| Women or children in the household (for topical products and capsules) | Absorbed through skin; risk to a male fetus | https://agence-prd.ansm.sante.fr/php/ecodex/rcp/R0422737.htm |
| Prostate checks | Finasteride: double the PSA result. Dutasteride: PSA halves by 6 months. Tell the GP. | SmPCs above |
| Breast lump, pain or discharge | Male breast cancer has been reported | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc |
| Severe liver disease (dutasteride) | Contraindicated | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc |
| Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ritonavir or itraconazole (dutasteride) | Raise dutasteride levels | https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc |
| Blood donation | UK donor rules are reported to defer donors for 4 weeks after finasteride and 6 months after dutasteride. **Not verified first-hand:** the guideline site has moved; check https://www.jpac.org.uk/ | Search result only |

**Before oral or sublingual minoxidil** (consensus statement, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)

- Do not prescribe with: past pericardial effusion, tamponade or pericarditis; heart failure; pulmonary
  hypertension with mitral stenosis; phaeochromocytoma.
- Take extra care with: fast heart rate or arrhythmia; blood pressure below 90/60 mmHg; kidney impairment or
  dialysis. Involve the GP or a cardiologist.
- Ask about all blood-pressure medicines. People on doxazosin or on three or more such drugs stopped more often
  (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37652097/).
- A home blood pressure and pulse reading before starting is a reasonable minimum for a remote service. The
  consensus only requires monitoring when a precaution exists, so this is a cautious extra, not an evidence-based rule.
- Warn about: body and facial hair (15% in the largest cohort, up to half at 5 mg), ankle swelling, dizziness, fast
  heartbeat, and the red flags of breathlessness, chest pain or rapid weight gain.

**Before topical minoxidil**

- High blood pressure, treated or not, is a listed contraindication in the product information. A prescriber can
  judge otherwise but should record why. Source: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc
- Scalp disease, shaving, broken skin; known allergy to propylene glycol.
- Infants or young children who touch the scalp or pillow.

### 10.3 Follow-up

No trial tells us the best review schedule (**unknown**). The MHRA says "review patients regularly". A defensible
schedule for a remote service, offered here as a proposal:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 2 to 4 weeks | Side-effect check: mood, sexual function, dizziness, swelling, scalp reaction. Reassure about early shedding on minoxidil. |
| 3 months | Same checks. Too early to judge hair. |
| 6 months | Photographs against baseline. First efficacy judgement. |
| 12 months | Continue, change or stop. The minoxidil product information says to stop if there is no improvement after one year; finasteride needs 3 to 6 months just to show that loss has steadied. |
| Then every 6 to 12 months | Mood and sexual-function questions every time; repeat photographs. |
| Any time | A fast route for the patient to report low mood, suicidal thoughts or sexual problems, with clear advice to stop finasteride 1 mg at once and seek help. |

Report suspected side effects, including from specials, through the Yellow Card scheme, as the MHRA updates above
ask.

### 10.4 Extra duties when the product is unlicensed (compounded)

From MHRA Guidance Note 14, "The supply of unlicensed medicinal products (specials)":
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/645e19f5ad8a03000c38b3bc/The_supply_of_unlicensed_medicinal_products__special_GN14.pdf

- An unlicensed medicine may be supplied only to meet the **special clinical need of an individual patient**, on the
  order of a doctor, dentist, nurse or pharmacist independent prescriber or supplementary prescriber.
- It "should not be supplied where an equivalent licensed medicinal product can meet the special needs of the
  patient".
- A special need "does not include reasons of cost, convenience or operational needs". Examples given are
  intolerance or allergy to an ingredient, or inability to swallow solid dose forms.
- **Particular specials must not be advertised.** A specials manufacturer, importer or wholesaler may advertise its
  service but not particular products (GN14 section 7). Separately, and more important for a prescribing service,
  **no prescription-only medicine may be advertised to the general public** in the UK, licensed or not; this covers
  finasteride, dutasteride and oral minoxidil, and websites and social media.
  Source: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/advertise-your-medicines *(Added on verification, 23 Nov 2026.)*
- Serious suspected adverse reactions must be reported to the MHRA and records kept.

Plain meaning for the service: "one bottle is easier" is not a special need. Each compounded prescription needs a
recorded clinical reason why the licensed products (finasteride 1 mg tablets, minoxidil 5% solution or foam) will
not do for that patient, and the website cannot promote named compounded formulas. Take regulatory advice before
launch; this file is a clinical review, not legal advice.

## 11. An evidence-based stepped ladder

**What published guidelines say**

- European evidence-based (S3) guideline, 2018: recommends topical minoxidil and oral finasteride, and suggests
  low-level laser as an add-on (as summarised in https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9297965/; guideline at
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29178529/).
- Japanese guideline, 2017 version: finasteride 1 mg, dutasteride 0.5 mg and topical minoxidil 5% are first line for
  men; laser or LED light and topical adenosine are options; it advised **against** oral minoxidil. That advice
  pre-dates the oral minoxidil trials in section 3.2. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29863806/
- UK: the NICE CKS topic could not be opened from outside the UK (section 12).

The ladder below is this review's synthesis of the evidence above. It is not a published guideline.

**Step 0. Diagnose, photograph, set expectations.**
Confirm the pattern. Take baseline photographs. Explain that the main aim is to stop further loss, that regrowth is
modest, that it takes 6 to 12 months to judge, and that the benefit lasts only while treatment continues
(finasteride gains are lost within 9 to 12 months of stopping; minoxidil gains within 3 to 4 months).
Sources: https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc and https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc

**Step 1. Licensed first-line treatment.**

- Topical minoxidil 5% twice daily (foam if the solution irritates), **and/or**
- Oral finasteride 1 mg daily, after the mood and sexual-function screen and with the patient card information.
- Offer both together from the start to men with more than mild loss or who want the best chance of regrowth
  (section 9).
- Add ketoconazole shampoo only if there is dandruff or seborrhoeic dermatitis.

**Step 2. Solve the problem that is stopping step 1 working (review at 6 months, sooner for side effects).**

| Problem | Next move | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Scalp irritation on minoxidil solution | Switch to propylene-glycol-free foam | Licensed |
| Cannot keep up twice-daily lotion, or it has failed | Low-dose oral minoxidil, start 2.5 mg daily (range 1.25 to 5 mg) after cardiovascular screening | Off-label |
| Wants the extra effect of an add-on | Weekly microneedling (not on the same day as lotion); a laser device at his own cost | Devices |
| Has a recorded clinical reason not to take oral finasteride but accepts a lower-exposure option | Low-volume topical finasteride, with the same warnings and follow-up as tablets | Unlicensed special; needs a documented special clinical need |

**Step 3. Inadequate response after 12 months of finasteride plus minoxidil, taken reliably.**
Switch finasteride to dutasteride 0.5 mg daily (never both). New consent: off-label, half-life of weeks, fertility,
PSA, precautionary mood warning.

**Step 4. Refer.**
Dermatology if the diagnosis is in doubt. Hair transplant surgery or clinic procedures (platelet-rich plasma) for
men who want more than medicines can give. Stop treatments that have shown no benefit at 12 months.

**Stop rules at every step.**
New depression or suicidal thoughts on finasteride 1 mg: stop at once (MHRA). Sexual dysfunction: review and
consider stopping. Chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, rapid weight gain or marked swelling on oral minoxidil:
stop and arrange medical assessment.

**Not on the ladder** (insufficient evidence): minoxidil above 5%, prostaglandin analogues, topical cetirizine,
melatonin, caffeine, biotin and other supplements in men who are not deficient, and any investigational drug.

## Key facts

The 25 facts most likely to change a decision. Each has its source.

1. Oral finasteride 1 mg and topical minoxidil 5% are the only two medicines with a UK licence for male pattern
   hair loss found on the emc. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2194/smpc and
   https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc
2. Finasteride 1 mg added 107 hairs vs placebo at 1 year and 138 at 2 years in a 5.1 cm² circle (baseline 876).
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777765/
3. Over 5 years, 48% of men on finasteride improved and 42% were unchanged on photographs; only 25% on placebo were
   improved or unchanged. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc
4. Finasteride's benefit starts to reverse within 6 months of stopping and is gone by 9 to 12 months.
   https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc
5. In trials, drug-related sexual side effects in the first year were 3.8% on finasteride vs 2.1% on placebo;
   meta-analysis relative risk 1.66. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/7068/smpc and
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30206635/
6. The MHRA (29 April 2024) announced a patient card for all finasteride packs and told prescribers to ask about
   depression and suicidal thoughts before prescribing.
   https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/finasteride-reminder-of-the-risk-psychiatric-side-effects-and-of-sexual-side-effects-which-may-persist-after-discontinuation-of-treatment
7. The European regulator (8 May 2025) confirmed suicidal ideation as a side effect of finasteride tablets
   (frequency unknown; 313 finasteride reports) and added a precautionary warning for dutasteride.
   https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/measures-minimise-risk-suicidal-thoughts-finasteride-dutasteride-medicines
8. The MHRA strengthened the warnings again on 11 May 2026, extended a precautionary mood warning to dutasteride and
   told prescribers to review patients regularly.
   https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/finasteride-and-dutasteride-updated-safety-warnings-for-psychiatric-side-effects-and-sexual-dysfunction
9. The MHRA's 2024 report singled out online supply of finasteride: some online pharmacies had thorough screening
   and monitoring, "others do not".
   https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6630abd087bdbae4ab19adc9/Finasteride_PAR_Accessible.pdf
10. Minoxidil 5% solution gave +18.6 hairs/cm² at 48 weeks vs +12.7 for 2% and +3.9 for vehicle; the gain peaked at
    16 weeks (+35.3). https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc
11. Minoxidil 10% was less effective than 5% and more irritating in the only trial.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31403367/
12. Propylene glycol, not minoxidil, is the usual cause of allergic contact dermatitis from minoxidil solution; the
    licensed 5% foam contains none. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11807448/ and
    https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5781/smpc
13. Oral minoxidil 5 mg was not superior to topical minoxidil 5% twice daily in men (double-blind trial, 24 weeks);
    hypertrichosis affected 49% on the oral dose. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598226/
14. In 1,404 patients on low-dose oral minoxidil, hypertrichosis occurred in 15.1%, fluid retention in 1.3%, fast
    heartbeat in 0.9%, and 1.7% stopped because of side effects. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33639244/
15. Expert consensus: men usually start oral minoxidil at 2.5 mg daily (range 1.25 to 5 mg); it should not be used
    with a history of pericardial effusion, pericarditis or heart failure; routine baseline bloods and ECG are not
    needed if there are no precautions. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/
16. Oral minoxidil is licensed in the UK only for severe hypertension; at those doses pericardial effusion occurs in
    about 3 to 5%. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc
17. Dutasteride 0.5 mg beat finasteride 1 mg by 7.1 hairs/cm² at 24 weeks in a network meta-analysis, but is
    licensed in the UK only for prostate enlargement. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/ and
    https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc
18. Dutasteride's half-life is 3 to 5 weeks; sperm count was still 23% below baseline 24 weeks after stopping.
    https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc
19. Topical finasteride spray (up to about 0.46 mg a day) matched oral finasteride numerically at 24 weeks (+20.2 vs
    +21.1 hairs) with a 34.5% vs 55.6% fall in blood DHT; only 55% of men had usable hair counts.
    https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34634163/
20. The US FDA (April 2025) warned that compounded topical finasteride has been linked to 32 reports of mood, sexual
    and other effects, some lasting after stopping.
    https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded
21. No topical finasteride product was listed on the UK emc or the MHRA products register on 23 November 2026, so
    it is an unlicensed special in the UK. https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/search?q=finasteride and
    https://products.mhra.gov.uk/substance/?substance=FINASTERIDE
22. Finasteride plus minoxidil together did better than either alone (improved in 94.1% vs 80.5% vs 59% at
    12 months). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26031764/
23. Microneedling added to minoxidil improves hair count over minoxidil alone (12 trials; about +13 hairs/cm² in a
    network meta-analysis). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40056230/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38058547/
24. UK rules allow an unlicensed special only for an individual patient's special clinical need, which "does not
    include reasons of cost, convenience or operational needs", and particular specials must not be advertised.
    https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/645e19f5ad8a03000c38b3bc/The_supply_of_unlicensed_medicinal_products__special_GN14.pdf
25. High-dose biotin can cause falsely low troponin and other wrong blood results, and has no proven benefit for
    hair in people who are not deficient.
    https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference
    and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28879195/
