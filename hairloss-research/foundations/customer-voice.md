# Customer voice: what people love and hate about online hair-loss companies

Status: COMPLETE for this round (research 23 Nov 2026). Reddit, Mumsnet, MoneySavingExpert and BBB complaint text were blocked; see section 1.4. **Independently fact-checked 23 Nov 2026**: corrections are marked in the text and listed in the Verification log at the end.

## Summary

1. Evidence: about 1,800 hair-related 1–2-star and 1,400 4–5-star Trustpilot reviews across 10–12 companies, plus App Store reviews, ASA/MHRA rulings, BBC reports and the FTC's July 2026 case against Hims & Hers.
2. The most-mentioned complaint is the subscription: hard to cancel, no reminder, surprise renewals (mentioned in about 35% of negative reviews; Sons, Happy Head, Keeps, Hims worst). US regulators back this up (FTC case against Hims & Hers; New York settlement with Keeps's owner).
3. But it is not the biggest "by far". When a random sample of 68 negative reviews was read by hand and each given its *main* complaint, delivery and stock failures (about 28%) and "no results" (about 22%) were as common as subscription, cancel and refund problems (about 18%). Treat these three as a joint top tier. Refunds refused for "prescription medicines" (24% mention them) sit inside the subscription problem.
4. Fewer but more harmful: side effects handled by sales or admin staff rather than a clinician (11%; Manual 24%), and early shedding nobody warned about (9%; Hers US 30%).
5. Many feel there was no real clinician: tick-box forms, no photos, no follow-up; the BBC and the FTC say the same.
6. Product-feel complaints (greasy, smelly, irritating, clogged sprays) and distrust of compounded or changed formulas are steady minor themes.
7. People praise results, a helpful named human, easy sign-up, clear explanations, fast tracked delivery and fair prices; UK general online pharmacies win on speed and value with no subscription trap.
8. Top unmet needs: honest cancel-anytime subscriptions, a named prescriber who answers side effects the same day, fair refunds, photo progress tracking with honest expectations, and reliable delivery.
9. Women are under-served in the UK: most services are men-first, and women want a diagnosis, blood tests, warnings about shedding and products that suit their hair.
10. UK subscription law (DMCCA, expected January 2027) will force reminders and easy exit; a new company can lead on this now and make trust its brand.

Reader: a UK pharmacist independent prescriber planning an online hair-loss service for men and women.

Goal: a ranked list of unmet needs that a new UK competitor could win on.

- Every fact carries a source link. Anything that could not be checked is marked **[unverified]** or left out.

### 1.2 How the Trustpilot reviews were counted

- For each company, the most recent 1-star and 2-star reviews (up to 200 per star level, the logged-out limit), recent 3-star reviews and about 120 recent 4–5-star reviews were saved.
- For companies that sell many things (Hims, Hers, Numan, Voy, Ro, Musely, the high-street online doctors), only reviews that mention hair, minoxidil, finasteride, the scalp and similar words were kept. Weight-loss reviews were removed.
- Each review was screened with keyword patterns for each theme (for example "cancel", "unsubscribe", "still charged" for the cancellation theme). One review can count under several themes. **These are keyword screens, not a hand-coded study**: they over-count some themes (for example "delivery" also catches "delivered on time") and under-count others. Use the percentages to compare companies and to rank themes roughly, not as exact rates.
- Review links use Trustpilot's standard review address, `https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/<review id>`, built from the review id in the page data.

What this shows:

- **UK specialists sit at about 4.2–4.3 with 11–16% of reviews at 1–2 stars.** US hair brands are much worse on Trustpilot: Hims US 2.9, Hers US 3.3, Keeps 3.2, Nutrafol 2.5.
- **The high-street online doctors get almost no hair-loss reviews.** Hair is a tiny part of their business, so their scores say little about their hair service.

### 1.4 Limits of this research

- Trustpilot samples are the most recent reviews visible to a logged-out visitor, not all reviews. Positive reviews are often invited and early.
- Keyword screens give rough rates. A hand-coded sample would give firmer numbers.
- Reddit, Mumsnet, MoneySavingExpert, ConsumerAffairs, BBB complaint text, Feefo and Yelp were blocked; their voices are missing.
- Treated, Strut Health, Hims UK and the high-street online doctors had too few hair reviews to analyse.
- App Store items are linked to the app's page plus the review id; Apple does not offer single-review links. The Ro app link was found by Apple's search; that the 2023 review sits under this app id is assumed from the earlier download, not re-checked.

### 1.5 Regulator and press findings

- **ASA, 8 Feb 2023, Hims & Hers UK (upheld).** The website promoted prescription-only finasteride tablets and unlicensed compounded products (topical finasteride with minoxidil) to the public. The ASA noted that "unlicensed medicines had not been tested for efficacy, quality or safety". [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/hims---hers-uk-ltd-a22-1145016-hims--hers-uk-ltd-1.html)
- **ASA, 5 Oct 2022 and 6 Mar 2024, Numan (both upheld).** TV adverts for Regaine with a "see results in 180 days or your money back" offer were judged to imply that the medicine was guaranteed to work. [2022 ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/vir-health-ltd-a22-1154781-vir-health-ltd.html), [2024 ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/vir-health-ltd-a22-1178070-vir-health-ltd.html)
- **ASA, 4 Jun 2025, Menwell Ltd t/a Manual (upheld).** A Google advert said "Buy Oral Minoxidil - £14/Month ... Effective in 9/10 Men". Oral minoxidil is prescription-only and licensed only for some types of high blood pressure. [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/menwell-ltd-a25-1277445-menwell-ltd.html)
- **MHRA, Sept 2021, Sons.** A member of the public complained that mental-health side effects of finasteride were not properly discussed on the Sons website; the MHRA asked Sons to give a balanced view of risks and "avoid absolute statements of safety". [MHRA](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advertising-investigations-september-2021/september-2021-promotion-of-finasteride-by-careforsons-limited-ta-sons)
- **BBC News, 24 Jan 2025.** "Some online sites are prescribing men a hair loss drug that has potentially risky side effects without consistent safety checks." A man who bought finasteride after a "tick-box" form said he had "no consultation with a doctor. No zoom meeting", and sent no photos. In the BBC's test purchases from Hims, Superdrug and Boots, Hims mentioned the side effects, only Boots asked for a photo, Superdrug offered a chat with a doctor for an extra fee, and none of the packs that arrived contained the new patient alert card (the BBC notes manufacturers had been given up to a year to add it). [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c05p1pnvymvo)
- **BBC Radio 4, File on 4 Investigates, 20 Jan 2026.** The programme reported that "some hair loss clinics are failing to adequately warn customers at the consultation stage" about sexual side effects, and found TikTok influencer accounts promoting finasteride with discount codes for Manual (Manual said it was not involved in making the videos). [BBC Sounds](https://www.bbc.co.uk/sounds/play/m002q2k1)

- **US Federal Trade Commission, 29 Jul 2026, Hims & Hers (lawsuit, not yet decided).** The FTC, with Utah and California, alleges that Hims enrols people in recurring plans soon after the intake form so that "most consumers will unknowingly be charged for and subscribed"; that most people do not get the promised consultation with a provider; that the online cancel button appeared only after choosing "add/remove items from order" and several more steps; that refill dates are not clearly shown, making it "difficult to cancel before the next billing cycle"; and that health data were shared with Meta, Snap and others. Hims & Hers disputes the case. These are allegations only. [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/ftc-states-act-against-hims-hers-deceptive-unlawful-privacy-practices)
- **FTC complaint records (Gizmodo, 7 Aug 2026).** The FTC told Gizmodo it holds over 4,800 complaints about Hims & Hers from the past five years. Gizmodo read 200 redacted complaints released under freedom-of-information rules and could not verify them. The most common complaint was being charged for an unwanted subscription and refused a refund; others were support that could not be reached and little real medical guidance. This independent source supports subscriptions as the leading complaint in the US. [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/hims-hers-ftc-subscription-surprise-scam-2000795614)
- **New York Attorney General, Assurance No. 26-028 (2026), Thirty Madison (owner of Keeps).** The Attorney General found that Keeps did not clearly disclose automatic refills, charges or how and by when to cancel; added fees not shown upfront (a $5 annual review shown as "FREE" in the first year); made cancellation burdensome; and trained staff to demand a cancellation reason and use "win back" scripts before cancelling. Thirty Madison agreed to pay New York $400,000 and to pay restitution to affected customers. Findings mainly cover February 2021 to about December 2023. [NY AG Assurance (PDF)](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/thirty-madison-aod-2026.pdf)
- **MHRA, 11 May 2026: stronger finasteride warnings.** After a further review, the MHRA strengthened the warnings: sexual dysfunction may contribute to mood disorders and has been reported with or without mood changes; the 2024 patient alert card stays; the MHRA expects prescribers to discuss the safety information with patients. This raises the bar for the side-effect counselling customers say is missing (sections 3.6 and 3.12). [MHRA news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mhra-strengthens-safety-warnings-for-finasteride-and-dutasteride)
- **UK subscription law is about to tighten.** The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCCA) subscription rules (clear information before sign-up, renewal reminders, easy exit including online cancellation where sign-up was online, and 14-day cooling-off periods at the start and at renewals) are expected to start in **January 2027**, brought forward from spring 2027; the detailed regulations and guidance were still awaited in October 2026. [DWF, 22 Oct 2026](https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/10/dmcca-subscription-contracts-regime-preparing-for-the-new-restrictions), [Lewis Silkin, Apr 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/02/consumer-law-update-subscriptions-regime-delayed-again-to-spring-2027-102mops). The "drip pricing" and fake-review bans in the same Act already apply from 6 April 2025. [CMA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices)

How to read this:

- **Money and the subscription machine come first.** Cancellation, refunds, delivery and account problems together dominate the 1–2-star reviews. These are service failures, not medicine failures.
- **Clinical themes are smaller in count but heavier in harm.** Side effects, shedding and finasteride worries are fewer, but they are the reviews that describe emergency visits, months of distress or feeling abandoned.
- **Independent check (fact-checker, 23 Nov 2026).** The saved review data were found in the research scratchpad and re-screened with separately written patterns over 1,915 hair-related 1–2-star reviews from the same ten companies: cancellation or subscription words appeared in 37%, refunds in 22%, "no results" words in 18%, side-effect words in 16% (a wider word list than the original 11%), and shedding or "made it worse" in 11%. A strict delivery screen (not arrived, lost, delayed, tracking, out of stock) gave only 13%, but a **hand-read random sample of 68 on-topic negatives** (2 off-topic reviews removed, one about facial hair removal and one about weight loss) gave these *main* complaints: delivery, stock or wrong items 19 (28%); no results or unmet expectations 15 (22%); subscription, cancel or refund 12 (18%); shedding or worse 5 (7%); side effects 4 (6%); price or value 4 (6%); reaching a person or a clinician 3 (4%); other 6. With only 68 reviews each figure is about ±10 points. **So the subscription is the most often *mentioned* complaint, but delivery failures and "no results" are as often the *main* reason for a bad review.** The ranking in section 5 has been adjusted.
- **Off-topic leakage.** The hair filter lets in a few reviews about other products (for example Musely's facial hair removal cream, or weight-loss reviews that mention hair). This affects Hers US and Hims US most (about 5–10% of their filtered negatives by a rough check) and slightly inflates some of their rates.
- **Keyword limits.** The "no clinician" screen found only about 2%, but reading the side-effect reviews shows many people asking for a clinician and getting a sales or admin reply (section 3.8). That theme is under-counted by keywords.

## 3. Themes in detail

Each theme gives how common it looks, who it hits hardest, two or three paraphrased examples with links, and what a better service would do. Examples are reworded and carry no names.

### 3.1 Cancelling a subscription and unwanted renewals

- **How common:** the single biggest theme, about 35% of hair-related 1–2-star reviews. About 9% say they had to phone, email or get past a chatbot to cancel; about 3% say they cancelled and were still charged; about 5% say no reminder came before a charge.
- **Hardest hit:** Happy Head, Sons, Keeps, Hims US, Hair + Me. In the UK, Sons stands out: a six-month order at £189 that renews automatically is mentioned again and again. The FTC's case against Hims & Hers makes the same allegations at scale (section 1.5).
- **Examples:**
  - Sons, Nov 2026: a customer who had not used the product for a year was charged £189 with only a "being processed" email, no warning beforehand. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6b048613cb8ea3829bff866c)
  - Sons, Nov 2026: the product was fine, but cancelling could not be done by phone (although signing up could) and needed an email to support; the reviewer said leaving should be as easy as joining. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6afc65d666be12c8050a2455)
  - Happy Head, Nov 2026: cancelling meant answering questions from an AI chat, then being told to phone, then repeating everything to a person. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6afb11072c92c6f06706c7ef)
  - Hims (UK reviewer), Oct 2026: the subscription was easy to start but there was no simple way to stop it, and the account was then hit with repeated charge attempts. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ada54d33b9a0fdb22837316)
- **A better service would:** offer one-tap cancel and pause in the account, by the same route as sign-up; send a reminder 7 days before every charge showing the date, amount and a cancel link; never auto-renew a six-month bundle without a fresh "yes"; let people cancel an order up to dispensing. This will also be the law in the UK from about January 2027 (section 1.5), so it is a chance to lead, not just comply.

### 3.2 Refunds, "prescription medicines can't be returned", and guarantees

- **How common:** about 24% mention refunds. The usual pattern is a renewal the person did not want, followed by "prescription medicines cannot be returned".
- **Hardest hit:** Musely, Manual, Sons, Hims US, Keeps.
- **Examples:**
  - Sons, Nov 2026: emailed to cancel a few hours after ordering while the account still showed "awaiting processing"; refund refused because returned medicines must be destroyed. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6af5c711f01dafa682a512cc)
  - Manual/Voy, Oct 2026: a man stopped finasteride after one tablet because of breast tenderness; a refund was first refused because it was a prescription medicine, then given after he posted a review. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aca35d2156bd21c58774bc8)
  - Keeps (US), Nov 2026: says the advertised money-back promise was not honoured when the product did not work. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6af24771c70299b656b79f7d)
  - The positive side: a Numan customer (Jul 2024) praised Numan for honouring its money-back guarantee without argument. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/66826b4fb7d8ae0be365eba3)
  - **Added by fact-checker: money-back guarantees with strings attached.** About 6% of hair-mentioning negatives name a money-back guarantee, and about 11% at Manual (18 of 167; keyword screen of the saved data). The pattern: customers followed the steps and sent monthly photos, then were told they did not meet the criteria or that a prescription medicine could not be refunded. Manual, Jun 2025: followed every step and uploaded progress photos, saw no results, then the guarantee was refused because the product was a prescription. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/685fc1e44ed283ab6945f01a) Manual, Mar 2025: sent photos monthly but missed a consultant slot offered only at awkward times, and was told this voided the refund. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/67d087aab2741af5954e436c)
- **Note on guarantees:** in the UK an advert must not suggest a medicine's effect is guaranteed. The ASA ruled twice against Numan's "see results in 180 days or your money back" Regaine adverts (section 1.5). A refund promise is allowed, but it cannot be sold as proof that the medicine works.
- **A better service would:** refund in full any order cancelled before dispensing; give a clear, written refund rule for unopened medicine sent after a missed reminder; offer a goodwill credit or refund when a side effect stops treatment early; never make "refund after a public review" the only route.

### 3.3 Delivery, stock and gaps in treatment

- **How common:** about 27% mention delivery. This keyword screen over-counts, but a hand-read random sample (section 2.1) found delivery, stock or wrong items to be the *main* complaint in about 28% of negatives, so the true scale is similar. True stock gaps are about 2% overall but 14% at Numan and 6% at Hers US.
- **Hardest hit:** Hair + Me (untracked Royal Mail parcels), Sons, Numan, Hers US.
- **Examples:**
  - Hair + Me, Sep 2026: the second order had tracking but Royal Mail never received it; the person ran out of treatment and cancelled. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a9a272deecb24b951f23b40)
  - Hair + Me, Nov 2026: untracked parcels did not arrive in the reviewer's area, and they then realised they were paying more than other suppliers charge for minoxidil capsules. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6b048ef3328c13c52c95343d)
  - Hers US, Sep 2026: a refill ordered in mid-August had not arrived by 10 September, leaving a month without minoxidil after four good months. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aa354d971ba27ab5ed72629)
  - Happy Head (US), Jul 2024: three months of missed shipments; the reviewer says all the regrown hair fell out. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/66837a22606a11a6b2c17ee6)
- **Why it matters clinically:** stopping minoxidil or finasteride lets the gains fade, so a missed parcel is not just an annoyance. (Clinical detail is in `clinical-men.md` and `clinical-women.md`.)
- **A better service would:** send every parcel tracked; ship the next supply when about 3 weeks are left; warn early of any stock problem and offer a licensed alternative; send a free replacement fast when a parcel is lost.

### 3.4 Price rises after the first order, and value

- **How common:** about 4% name a price rise after an introductory offer; about 7% say it is too expensive or cheaper elsewhere; about 1% notice less product for the same price.
- **Hardest hit:** Numan, Manual, Sons, Hair + Me in the UK; Nutrafol in the US (16% call it too expensive).
- **Examples:**
  - Hair + Me, Nov 2026: did not see on the website that the price goes from £14.99 to £34.99 a month after the trial. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aec98af4807b6429cefd43c)
  - Hair + Me, Jun 2026: signed up for a three-month introductory offer but the price went up straight away, with no way to see the offer code or manage the plan. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a3e4d82359166b533150f00)
  - Manual, Jan 2025: £60 for 90 days of an all-in-one tablet was fair, but the price doubles after the first period, before results are likely; the reviewer felt this was exploitative. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/67977309c18685ad2f343590)
  - Sons, Jul 2026: when cancelling, the reviewer was offered 40% and then 50% off, which made them feel they had been overcharged all along. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a5e037e04c8309442e98dde)
- **A better service would:** show one honest monthly price for the whole first year; if there is an offer, show the full price next to it and remind people before it ends; avoid secret "retention" discounts that tell loyal customers they paid too much.

### 3.5 Product feel: greasy, sticky, smelly, irritating, clogged sprays, short expiry

- **How common:** greasy or smelly about 5%; scalp irritation about 7%; faulty sprays or pumps about 7%.
- **Hardest hit:** Hims (sprays that clog), Musely (smell and changed formula), Manual (sticky 10% solution), Hair + Me (greasy serum, one-month expiry).
- **Examples:**
  - Hims (UK reviewer), Feb 2026: the spray nozzle clogged and stopped working after about four weeks. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69850f04dd8a505caf7c07ad)
  - Manual, Nov 2024: the 5% solution worked for years, then was withdrawn, and the 10% replacement stayed sticky and did not suit the scalp. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/673df7af158ebb9428c4d5e2)
  - Hair + Me, Sep 2026: the serum smelled bad, and each bottle was labelled to expire after one month although it held more than a month's use; the reviewer felt this forced monthly buying. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aa96a29f544761f97e7448d)
  - Musely (US), Aug 2026: after years on a serum, a new bottle smelled strongly; the reviewer, a woman with textured hair who cannot wash daily, was passed between support and the doctor. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a74eef609ab865c2bea3c6c)
- **A better service would:** offer a choice of base (foam, low-alcohol solution, tablet) and switch without a new fee; explain how to apply and what to do about itch; replace faulty sprays at once; explain any change in formula before it ships.

### 3.6 Side effects and how the company responded

- **How common:** about 11% mention side effects, rising to 24% at Manual and 18% at Hims US. Finasteride worries (sexual and mood effects) are about 3% overall but 14% at Manual and 12% at Hims US.
- **Hardest hit:** Manual/Voy, Hims US, Numan, Hers US, Hair + Me.
- **Examples:**
  - Manual, Aug 2025: severe side effects on the second prescription; a promised clinician call-back never came; after a 40-minute phone wait the person had to explain everything to two clinicians because no notes had been made. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/68961f3152e2caa15a6413ea)
  - Sons, Aug 2026: reported a significant side effect on topical finasteride/minoxidil and asked whether to stop; instead of a clinician, the reply asked whether they wanted to cancel the subscription. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a86c57ab2e7865e8cbffe0b)
  - Hair + Me, Oct 2026: tablets caused dizziness and headaches; the person asked how to stop safely and was switched to drops and had the cancelled account reactivated. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6adbe259948f6031933c0da7)
  - Manual, Nov 2026: low mood, suicidal thoughts and crying every night within about a week of starting a hair-loss product (the review does not name the medicine or say whether it was a tablet); symptoms eased within days of stopping. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ae843b94c58ba9fac6d2225)
  - Hims US (US reviewer), Jun 2026: went to the emergency department with severe fluid retention after three to four weeks of oral minoxidil chews and was in hospital for nine days (an overactive thyroid was also found, which the reviewer says was unrelated); a second batch was still charged and sent. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6afcfa0704e82fad0bb2a30d)
- **Context:** the MHRA asked Sons in 2021 to give a balanced view of finasteride risks, and the BBC (2025, 2026) reported weak safety checks and poor warnings about sexual side effects at online clinics (section 1.5).
- **A better service would:** treat any side-effect message as clinical: a named prescriber replies the same working day, with clear "stop / reduce / carry on" advice and a Yellow Card report where needed; keep notes so nobody repeats their story; pause billing automatically; never answer a side-effect report with a retention offer.

### 3.7 Shedding at the start that nobody explained

- **How common:** about 9% overall; 30% of Hers US negatives and 18% of Manual's.
- **Hardest hit:** Hers US (mostly women on minoxidil), Manual, Nutrafol.
- **Examples:**
  - Hers US, Jul 2026: lost what the reviewer felt was half their hair; shedding had not been on the side-effect list they saw, and questions got canned replies. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a63029027b33c5a9afb7d54)
  - Hims (UK reviewer), Oct 2026: hair fell out in the first week, including in areas that were fine; asked for a refund and was first offered 20% off the next order; a full refund came only after they pressed, sent before-and-after photos and said they would post a review. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ac7f0b4658381fa93b9c40c)
  - Hers US, Oct 2025: hair fell out heavily after starting minoxidil and months of messages to the care team did not help. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/68ffacd804de810db9f217a7)
- **A better service would:** warn before the first dose, in plain words, that some people shed more in the first weeks of minoxidil, how long it usually lasts and when to worry; send a check-in at weeks 2–8; have a clinician review photos if shedding is heavy. (Clinical timing is in `clinical-men.md` and `clinical-women.md`.)

### 3.8 No real contact with a clinician

- **How common:** the keyword screen finds only about 2%, but it is a thread through the side-effect, shedding and cancellation reviews. The FTC alleges that most Hims customers did not get the consultation they were told about (section 1.5), and the BBC described a "tick-box" form with no photos (section 1.5).
- **Hardest hit:** Manual, Sons, Hair + Me, Hims.
- **Examples:**
  - Manual, Mar 2026: uneasy that so little information was needed, with no ID check and no proper assessment, and that the spray was not officially approved for hair loss. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69b95009a2f72d8c89448bfe)
  - Hair + Me, Nov 2026: noted that no photo of the hair was asked for, although the medicine is prescription-only. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aedbc02223a65607746ea34)
  - Ro (App Store, Nov 2023): felt the prescribers knew less about hair loss than they did. [App Store, Ro](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ro-online-healthcare/id1585858911) (review id 10545855529 in Apple's public review feed, read 23 Nov 2026)
- **A better service would:** make the prescriber visible: a named pharmacist prescriber or doctor, photos at the start, a short video or phone call on request at no extra charge, and a way to message the same clinician later.

### 3.9 Slow, automated or copy-paste replies

- **How common:** about 13%; 24% at Musely, 18% at Happy Head and Hair + Me.
- **Examples:**
  - Hair + Me, Nov 2026: missed deliveries, a self-service portal that could not change anything, an automated email and then silence. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6afae7ef6a3616ba5168de95)
  - Hers US, Oct 2026: only bots and AI answered for a month while an order was not shipped. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6acbe3120b7e9bec3c053359)
  - Voy, Oct 2026: a repeat minoxidil order was paid for but held for an ID check the customer never knew about; then repeated automated replies and a complaint that was ignored. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ad8588b92400f494cbd5c6e)
- **Positive contrast:** Happy Head's positive reviews praise named staff by name for quick, knowledgeable phone help (67% of its sampled positives mention service), for example one expecting "the same old chatbot" and getting a helpful person quickly (Nov 2026). [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6af4dd25ace9c01c152d37e6)
- **A better service would:** reply within one working day from a named person; let a bot handle tracking but route anything clinical, billing or a complaint to a person; publish response times.

### 3.10 Results not tracked, and expectations

- **How common:** "no results" is about 21% of negatives; complaints about no check-in or progress photos about 6%. In the positives, 17% say it is "too early to tell", so many reviews are written in the first weeks.
- **Hardest hit:** Manual, Numan, Hims (UK and US), Nutrafol.
- **Examples:**
  - Hims UK, Nov 2026: a cluster of 2-star reviews in two days said results were nothing like the before-and-after pictures in the adverts after up to 12 months on tablets. [review 1](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aee27e1a9460993251d8a87), [review 2](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aeed244b3649e336d1f4d4c)
  - Hers US (App Store), Nov 2026: wished someone had checked in; nobody had in nearly four months, and it felt impersonal. [App Store, Hers](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hers-womens-healthcare/id1623878709) (review id 14782553994 in Apple's public feed)
  - Manual, Nov 2026: good results for 6–12 months on topical minoxidil, then the effect faded over the second year. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6afec916d0a4a85110a9900e)
- **A better service would:** take standard photos at the start, 3, 6 and 12 months; review them with the patient; set honest goals at the start (keep what you have, slow loss, some regrowth for some people); change the plan at month 6–12 if it is not working, instead of shipping the same thing forever.

### 3.11 Feeling pushed to one product

- **How common:** about 7% (upsells, marketing, being given a different product). "Asked for tablets, sent a topical" is 5% of Hair + Me's negatives.
- **Examples:**
  - Hair + Me, Nov 2026: asked for tablets because minoxidil is toxic to cats, but was sent the liquid with no choice. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aeee386a7ed9244935969d8)
  - Hair + Me, Sep 2026: asked for tablets because their hair gets greasy; the serum was sent instead and needed washing out daily. When they asked to switch, they were sent a health form and then a request for scalp photos, which they felt was too many steps, so they cancelled. (The extra checks were reasonable before oral minoxidil; the problem was not explaining why.) [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ab4dfcdbcf8ef7f49c4bb56)
  - Happy Head, Apr 2026: during cancellation, the staff member kept selling other options until the reviewer asked them to stop, and a charge still followed five days later. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69df3b4aa1927d175f5612a3)
- **Context:** the pet worry is real. Cats Protection warned about cat poisonings linked to hair-loss treatments ([Cats Protection](https://www.cats.org.uk/mediacentre/pressreleases/hairloss-treatment-dangerous-for-cats)), (20 cat poisonings recorded by the Veterinary Poisons Information Service in the previous year, Cats Protection, 20 May 2026), and a US study of 211 dog and cat cases found that accidental exposure while the owner was applying minoxidil to their own hair (licking skin or a pillowcase, spills) was the most common way cats were exposed; 8 of 62 cats with signs of poisoning died ([PubMed 34370845](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34370845/)).
- **A better service would:** ask about preferences (tablet or topical, pets, partners trying to conceive, hair washing routine) and explain the reason when it declines a choice; never add products to the basket by default.

### 3.12 Worry about finasteride side effects, and wanting alternatives

- **How common:** about 3% of negatives overall, 12–14% at Hims US and Manual. It also drives the side-effect reviews in 3.6.
- **Examples:**
  - Hims US, Jun 2026: reported low libido and was told that one in two men get it; the reviewer felt the service did not engage. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a3816a6199118a2924928a0)
  - Manual, Jan 2023: no regrowth, and the reviewer said their own doctor had to explain the possible sexual effects, which the service had not made clear. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/63c141964d0773066a36c60f)
  - Manual/Voy, Oct 2026: breast tenderness after one tablet; the reviewer says the risk feels like it is all the patient's. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aca35d2156bd21c58774bc8)
- **A better service would:** give a balanced, plain-language risk talk before prescribing (including the MHRA's mood warning and the patient alert card, see `uk-regulation.md` and `clinical-men.md`); offer minoxidil-only and other routes for men who decline finasteride; follow up at 4–6 weeks about mood and sexual function.

### 3.13 Wanting a proper diagnosis or blood tests

- **How common:** about 4%, higher among women and at Musely, Manual and Nutrafol.
- **Examples:**
  - Happy Head, Aug 2026: had to buy products before seeing the dermatologist, without knowing what was being bought. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a91b150d6258561fddee273)
  - Hair + Me, Oct 2026: a trichologist later told the reviewer they were too young for the formula they had been given. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ade4100dbdef1160005f5f5)
  - Hers US, Sep 2026 (positive): improvement after low iron was found and treated, alongside perimenopause. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aac023ebd266da4f6fadae9)
- **A better service would:** check the diagnosis first (pattern, photos, red flags for other causes) and offer blood tests where the history points to them (especially for women: iron stores, thyroid, and others as in `clinical-women.md`), and say plainly when someone needs a GP or dermatologist instead.

### 3.14 Distrust of compounded or changed products

- **How common:** about 5%; 24% at Musely.
- **Examples:**
  - Happy Head, Oct 2024: worried that mixes of many actives in one compound have not been tested in studies. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/66fe9fe31421951b80f07646)
  - Musely, Oct 2025: asked for two ingredients to be left out and was told the compounded product could not be changed, despite being sold as personalised. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/68e1a45de53a1b0488d13c8e)
  - Hair + Me, Oct 2026: had such bad side effects from tablets that they were sending them for independent testing to check the contents. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6add138c25f7f474ca7e6bc0)
  - XYON (US), Aug 2026: a new batch of gel felt gritty, unlike earlier orders. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a8fe028b3a31a378a794262)
- **Context:** the ASA told Hims & Hers UK not to advertise unlicensed medicines, noting they "had not been tested for efficacy, quality or safety" (section 1.5).
- **A better service would:** start with licensed products where they fit; when a special (unlicensed) product is used, say so, explain why, name the maker, and publish batch testing; tell patients before any formula change.

### 3.15 Website, app and account problems

- **How common:** about 14%, often tied to cancelling.
- **Examples:**
  - Hair + Me, Oct 2026: a mistyped email at sign-up meant no access to the account, no phone or chat, and no way to cancel. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ae31f6ec8586df56f6ccde3)
  - Voy (App Store, Sep 2026): no way to ask for data deletion inside the app. [App Store, Voy](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/voy-your-health-companion/id6471655955) (review id 14512870394 in Apple's public feed)
  - Hers (App Store, Jan 2026): the app rarely loaded after the first log-in, although the company pushes people to use it. [App Store, Hers](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hers-womens-healthcare/id1623878709) (review id 13590725436)
- **A better service would:** let people do everything on the web as well as the app; offer a phone line; make deleting data and the account self-service.

### 3.16 Safety checks done badly: surprise holds, unexplained refusals, wrong records (added by fact-checker)

This theme was missing. It is the other side of 3.8: some customers complain of *too few* checks, others of checks that appear without warning, stop their treatment or are wrong.

- **How common:** about 6% of hair-mentioning negatives mention ID checks, verification, photos, questionnaires or annual reviews (72 of 1,147; keyword screen of the saved data, 23 Nov 2026). Not every hit is a complaint about checks, so treat this as rough.
- **Examples:**
  - Voy, Oct 2026: a repeat minoxidil order was paid for but held for an ID check the customer never knew about; repeated automated replies followed. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ad8588b92400f494cbd5c6e)
  - Hair + Me, Aug 2026: the annual review was only asked for 4 days before the medicine ran out, and the customer went days without treatment. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a91755fa0d3f66d7dc97f7b)
  - Hair + Me, Oct 2026: a long-standing tablet prescription was switched to drops because the record said they had reported chest pain and blood-pressure problems, which they say they never reported. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6ace7d6f1167399987d06341)
  - Numan, Jul 2024: a renewal was charged but not sent until ID was verified, and then nothing more was heard. [review](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/66a0bf4a76d9e3e2861b0ca5)
- **A better service would:** tell people at sign-up which checks will happen and when (ID, photos, blood pressure, annual review); start reviews 4–6 weeks before supply runs out; never take payment for an order that is on hold without saying so; show patients what their record says and fix errors quickly; explain every refusal or switch in plain words. Good checks are a selling point only if they are explained and do not cause gaps.

## 6. Table stakes (what a new company must match on day one)

These are the things satisfied customers praise most often (section 4). Without them a new company will not get a hearing.

1. **Sign-up in minutes** on a phone, with plain questions (Sons and Hair + Me praised for ease).
2. **Fast, discreet, tracked delivery** in plain packaging (Oxford Online Pharmacy: fast delivery in 38% of praise).
3. **A competitive monthly price** close to the UK general online pharmacies for generic finasteride and minoxidil (see `market-economics.md`).
4. **Clear written information** in the parcel and online about how to use the treatment and what to expect.
5. **Helpful, friendly people** who answer quickly and know the products (Happy Head's 67% service praise).
6. **Something that feels personal**: questions about their own hair, a plan explained to them.
7. **Effective, evidence-based treatment** (results are the top praise at 40%).
8. **Easy pause, skip and reschedule** (praised at Musely; missing at Sons).
9. **Responses to every public review** (most UK brands reply to almost all negatives; see section 1.3).
10. **Lawful advertising**: no prescription-only medicines named in adverts, no "guaranteed results" (ASA rulings, section 1.5).

- **What women complain about:**
  - **Shedding nobody warned them about** (30% of Hers US negatives). [example](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a63029027b33c5a9afb7d54)
  - **Side effects of oral minoxidil** such as headache and sickness, or texture change, and being told it was not a known effect. [example 1](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a6b88ba60866c954cb26d18), [example 2](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aa60c77e775330d93b72c95)
  - **Products not suited to their hair.** A woman with textured hair could not wash daily to remove a smelly serum ([example](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a74eef609ab865c2bea3c6c)); serums that leave hair greasy or crispy ([example](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/69bbe8b8fb0dca1077c458b6)).
  - **Supplements that did not work after a year and a lot of money** (Nutrafol 2.5 TrustScore, 56% 1–2 stars). [example](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/6aebebc9249d5d4181880156)
  - **No check-in** in four months, feeling impersonal. [App Store, Hers](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hers-womens-healthcare/id1623878709) (review id 14782553994)
  - **Missed shipments** causing loss of regrown hair, with deep distress. [example](https://uk.trustpilot.com/reviews/66837a22606a11a6b2c17ee6)
- **What a women-first UK service would do:** take a full history (periods, pregnancy plans, contraception, menopause, recent illness or stress, diet); offer blood tests where indicated; separate shedding (telogen effluvium) from pattern loss; explain early shedding before starting; offer options that fit women's hair care (wash frequency, styling, textured hair); use women in images and language; follow up with photos; and refer when it looks like scarring or another cause. Clinical detail is in `clinical-women.md`.
- **Gap in the evidence:** UK women's forums (Mumsnet) and Reddit r/FemaleHairLoss were blocked, and the UK press found by search offered only clinic blogs with unsourced survey figures, so the UK women's voice here is thin. **Primary research (interviews) is recommended before launch.**

### Summary of corrections

1. **Ranking softened.** Subscriptions are the most-mentioned complaint (about 35–37%, confirmed by a second screen and by US regulators), but not "by far" the biggest.
