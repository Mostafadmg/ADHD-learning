## Part 1. The conditions

### 1.0 The first sort: three questions that split almost every case

**Question 1. Is the scalp skin scarred or inflamed?** This matters most, because scarring hair loss is permanent and needs fast face-to-face care.

- In non-scarring hair loss the follicle openings (the tiny pores the hairs grow from) can still be seen and the skin is not thinned. In scarring hair loss the openings are lost and the skin is thin and often shiny. Inflammation is often, but not always, visible. [PCDS, Alopecia: an overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- PCDS: "Patients with scarring alopecia require an urgent referral" because "once scarred the alopecia is irreversible." [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

**Question 2. Is the loss diffuse (all over or over the top) or in patches?**

- Diffuse, non-scarring: telogen effluvium (sudden), chronic telogen effluvium and female pattern hair loss (gradual). [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- Patchy, non-scarring: alopecia areata, tinea capitis (scalp ringworm), hair pulling (trichotillosis) and traction alopecia. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

**Question 3. Is it shedding (hair falling out) or thinning (hair getting finer and the parting getting wider)?**

- Shedding that starts suddenly about 3 months after a trigger points to telogen effluvium. [BAD telogen effluvium leaflet, Oct 2025](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)
- Slow thinning over the top with a widening parting and a normal-looking scalp points to female pattern hair loss. [BAD female pattern hair loss leaflet, Oct 2024](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- The two often overlap: FPHL is itself listed as one cause of chronic shedding. [Perera and Sinclair 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29167734/)

**What "normal" shedding is.** The BAD says most people lose between 30 and 150 scalp hairs a day ([BAD TE leaflet](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)); the NHS says 50 to 100 a day ([NHS hair loss page, reviewed 24 Jan 2024](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hair-loss/)).

### 1.1 Photo set an online service should ask for (author's synthesis)

There is no published UK standard for hair-loss photos in an online service **[unverified: none found]**. The list below is built from the signs the sources say matter:

| Photo | Why (the sign it shows) | Source for the sign |
|---|---|---|
| Top of head, hair parted in the middle, dry hair, good light | Widened central parting = FPHL. A normal parting with heavy shedding = telogen effluvium | [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) |
| Front hairline with hair pulled back, plus both temples | Hairline moving back with pale, smooth skin = FFA. A rim of retained short hairs at the edge ("fringe sign") = traction | [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/), [Billero and Miteva 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29670386/) |
| Eyebrows (close up) | Eyebrow loss goes with FFA and with alopecia areata | [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/), [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/) |
| Crown from above | Patch spreading out from the mid-crown in a woman with tightly curled hair = possible CCCA | [BAD CCCA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/central-centrifugal-cicatricial-alopecia/) |
| Close-up of any patch or sore area | Smooth, non-scaly round patch = alopecia areata. Redness and scale round each hair, or shiny skin with no pores = scarring | [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/), [BAD LPP](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/lichen-planopilaris/) |
| Back of the head | A dense back with a thin top supports pattern hair loss (PCDS: thinning is "centroparietal") | [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia) |

A photo cannot replace dermoscopy (a magnified skin view). PCDS notes dermoscopy is increasingly used to diagnose hair loss, and that in pattern hair loss more than 20% of hairs in the affected area are miniaturised (small and fine). [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia) So if the photos do not clearly fit one pattern, the safe answer is to refer.

### 1.2 Female pattern hair loss (FPHL, also called female androgenetic alopecia)

**What it is.** The most common type of hair loss in women. Follicles shrink a little with each hair cycle, so hairs become finer, shorter and lighter until the follicle stops making hair. The cause is a mix of genes and hormones. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**How common.** In a UK dermatology-clinic sample of 377 women, 6% of women under 50 and 38% of women aged 70 and over had FPHL. [Birch 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11251562/) In an Australian community survey, mid-frontal hair loss affected 57% of women aged 80 and over. [Gan and Sinclair 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16382660/)

**How it presents.**

- Diffuse thinning mainly over the top of the scalp. The central parting becomes wider and more visible. The front hairline is often kept. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- Two named patterns: Ludwig (thinning of the top with the front hairline kept, the most common) and "Christmas tree" (the same, but the front hairline is also breached). [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)
- Usual onset is in the 50s or 60s; sometimes the 30s or 40s. Earlier onset may progress faster. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- Usually no scalp symptoms (some women report itch). The scalp skin looks normal. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- It progresses very slowly, over years to decades. There is no cure; treatment works only while it is used. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- It has a real psychological cost (lower self-esteem, body image concerns), so ask about mood. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**How to tell it apart.**

| Feature | FPHL | Telogen effluvium | Scarring types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | Years | Sudden, about 3 months after a trigger | Months to years |
| Parting | Wider | Normal ("a good head of hair... with no widening of the central parting line" in chronic TE) | Varies |
| Scalp skin | Normal | Normal | Red, scaly round hairs, or smooth and shiny |
| Symptoms | Usually none | Sometimes tenderness | Itch, pain or burning are common |

Sources: [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf), [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview), [BAD LPP](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/lichen-planopilaris/).

**Red flags: refer, do not treat online.**

- Hair loss in a **male pattern** (deep temple recession, bald crown) in a woman. PCDS: "It is very uncommon for women to lose hair following the male pattern unless there is excessive production of androgens." [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)
- Any sign of androgen excess (new or worsening excess body or facial hair, acne, irregular or absent periods, deepening voice). See 1.6.
- Any scalp redness, scale, pustules, pain, burning or shiny scarred skin. See 1.9 and 1.10.
- Diagnosis unclear from history and photos, or moderate to severe emotional distress. PCDS lists both as reasons to refer non-scarring hair loss. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- Under 18, or over 65 if the plan is licensed topical minoxidil: the UK licences for Regaine for Women cover ages 18 to 65 only. [Regaine for Women 2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc), [Regaine for Women 5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)

### 1.3 Telogen effluvium (TE): sudden shedding after a trigger

**What it is.** A shock to the body pushes many follicles into the resting phase at once. Normally about 15% of scalp hairs are in the shedding phase; in TE it is 30% or more. A resting follicle waits two to four months before the old hair falls, which is why the shedding starts about 3 months after the trigger. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)

**Triggers the BAD lists:** childbirth; severe trauma or illness (especially with high fever); stressful or major life events; surgery; marked weight loss and extreme dieting; severe scalp skin problems; starting new medicines; stopping a hormone treatment such as the contraceptive pill. No cause is found in 1 in 3 people. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)

**How it presents.** Sudden, diffuse shedding noticed on washing or brushing; sometimes scalp tenderness; no bald patches. The shedding phase lasts 3 to 6 months and it can take many more months for the volume to return. It is called chronic if it lasts more than 6 months. It can come back if a cause such as iron deficiency is not treated. [BAD TE leaflet page](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/telogen-effluvium/)

**Course.** PCDS: unless the trigger is repeated, "spontaneous complete regrowth occurs within three to six months." [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) The BAD says medicine does not speed up regrowth and usually no treatment is needed. [BAD TE leaflet page](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/telogen-effluvium/)

**The four triggers the service will see most:**

| Trigger | What the evidence says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| After childbirth | Childbirth is a listed TE trigger; shedding starts about 3 months after the trigger. Note the evidence is thinner than people think: a 2016 literature review found no good data on how common postpartum shedding really is | [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf), [Mirallas and Grimalt 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27386466/) |
| After illness | High fever is a classic trigger (PCDS: 102.5 °F and above). After COVID-19, a systematic review of 465 patients found shedding began a mean of 74 days after symptom onset; 67.5% were women; most recovered | [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview), [Hussain 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34931698/) |
| Crash dieting | "Marked weight loss and extreme dieting" is a listed trigger; PCDS lists crash dieting and malabsorption under gradual diffuse loss | [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf), [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) |
| Weight-loss injections | Hair loss is a listed side effect of semaglutide (Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro). Figures are in Part 3.15 | [Wegovy 2.4 mg SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13803/smpc), [Mounjaro SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15484/smpc) |

**Chronic telogen effluvium.** PCDS describes it as affecting middle-aged women who often have long, thick hair and report losing a lot of it; on examination there is "a good head of hair appropriate to their age with no widening of the central parting line." Management is reassurance: the natural history is not progressive unless FPHL is also present. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

**How to tell it apart.** A clear trigger 2 to 3 months before, sudden onset, even thinning with a normal parting and normal scalp. If the shedding has gone on for more than 6 months, or the parting is widening, think FPHL (alone or with TE).

**Red flags: refer.**

- Symptoms of a general illness with the shedding, such as unintended weight loss, fever, night sweats, joint pain, a rash or marked tiredness (author's list, built from the causes PCDS names: malabsorption, liver or kidney disease, cancer, lupus and dermatomyositis). [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- Shedding lasting more than 6 months with no cause found (needs bloods and an examination; see Part 2).
- Signs of an eating disorder or very low intake (author's synthesis, based on "extreme dieting" as a trigger).
- A new medicine started 2 to 4 months before: ask the prescriber, do not just add a hair treatment. PCDS notes dose-related diffuse hair loss is common with acitretin. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- Any suspicion of syphilis: PCDS lists syphilis serology as a test to add where it is suspected. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

### 1.4 Menopause-related thinning

- FPHL becomes much more common after the menopause. In 178 healthy postmenopausal women aged 50 to 65 examined with standard photos and trichoscopy, 52.2% had FPHL (73.2% of those were the mildest grade, Ludwig I). After adjusting for age and family history, only a body mass index of 25 or more stayed linked to FPHL (adjusted odds ratio 2.65). [Chaikittisilpa 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35357365/)
- The BAD puts the usual age of onset of FPHL in the 50s or 60s. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- **The trap at this age is frontal fibrosing alopecia**, which "mainly affects white women after menopause" and needs referral (see 1.9). [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
- New or worsening signs of androgen excess after the menopause need investigation to rule out an androgen-secreting tumour or ovarian hyperthecosis (overgrowth of hormone-making ovary tissue). [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- What hormone replacement therapy does and does not do for hair is in Part 3.11.

**How to tell it apart.** Same pattern as FPHL at any age. Always look at the front hairline and eyebrows in every woman over about 45 before calling it FPHL (author's synthesis based on the FFA sources above).

### 1.5 Hair loss with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

- FPHL "may accompany conditions in which androgen levels are elevated such as polycystic ovarian syndrome. Acne, increased facial hair, irregular periods and infertility are all signs of PCOS." [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- In a specialist PCOS clinic, 56 of 254 women with PCOS (22.0%) had pattern hair loss. Those women were more likely to have acne or hirsutism (96.3% against 70.6%), but their blood androgen levels were no different from women with PCOS without hair loss. [Quinn 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24534277/)
- The other way round: hair loss or acne **without** hirsutism is a relatively weak predictor of raised blood androgens. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- Most FPHL is idiopathic (no cause found) or familial; "a smaller group have associated hyperandrogenism." [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)

**What this means online.** Ask every woman about periods, acne, excess hair and fertility. If any are present, arrange androgen bloods and involve her GP (PCOS also needs diabetes and cardiovascular risk checks). [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)

### 1.6 Other androgen excess (the dangerous few)

**Features of virilisation** (male-type change): extreme hirsutism, male-pattern hair loss, deepening voice, enlarged clitoris, increased muscle bulk, smaller breasts. [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)

**Red flags that mean urgent referral (endocrinology or gynaecology), not an online prescription:**

- New-onset and progressive virilisation. [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)
- Early-morning total testosterone above 4.8 on repeat testing (PCDS gives no unit on the page; UK laboratories report testosterone in nmol/L **[unit unverified on the PCDS page]**). If it stays above 4.8, evaluate for an androgen-secreting tumour and refer urgently. [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism) Fact-check note (23 Nov 2026): the page was re-read and still gives no unit. The same page describes milder causes as "total testosterone between 3-4", which only makes sense in nmol/L, but that is an inference. No UK source that states the cut-off with its unit could be opened (NICE CKS is blocked; the [RCOG page for its PCOS guideline](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/long-term-consequences-of-polycystic-ovary-syndrome-green-top-guideline-no-33/) links to the 2023 international guideline, whose recommendations give no number). Use the local laboratory's reference range and comments.
- Some androgen-secreting tumours cause only mild to moderate rises in testosterone, so a fast history matters as much as the number. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- Features of Cushing's syndrome (round face, central weight gain, thin skin, easy bruising, stretch marks) or acromegaly (enlarging hands, feet and jaw). [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)
- Stubborn acne in a woman without PCOS: check for late-onset congenital adrenal hyperplasia (17-hydroxyprogesterone, follicular phase, about 9 am). [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)

### 1.7 Traction alopecia

- **Cause:** pulling. The commonest cause is tight hairstyles: tight ponytails and buns, weaves and extensions, tight braids (cornrows, dreadlocks). Chemical relaxers, clips, tight head scarves and very long hair can add to it. [BAD traction alopecia](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/traction-alopecia/)
- **Who:** people of any ethnic background. It affects about one-third of women of African descent who wear traumatic hairstyles for a long time. [BAD traction alopecia](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/traction-alopecia/), [Billero and Miteva 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29670386/)
- **How it looks:** loss at the front, sides or back where the pull is greatest. Fine hairs remain in the patch. A rim of retained hairs at the edge (the "fringe sign"). Redness, spots and hair casts (white cylinders round the hair shaft) if tight styles continue. Long-standing cases look smooth and shiny. [BAD traction alopecia](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/traction-alopecia/)
- **Course:** early cases regrow fully once the pulling stops (regrowth may start about 3 months later). Long-term repeated pulling leads to permanent loss. [BAD traction alopecia](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/traction-alopecia/)
- **Tell it apart from FFA:** the fringe sign is kept in traction and lost in FFA. [Billero and Miteva 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29670386/)
- **Treatment:** stop the tight styles (the main step). Topical minoxidil 2% or 5% is listed by the BAD as an option. [BAD traction alopecia](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/traction-alopecia/) This is off-label: the Regaine for Women SmPCs limit the licence to androgenetic alopecia (see 3.1). [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)
- **Refer if:** smooth shiny skin with no regrowth after the styles have stopped, pustules or marked inflammation, or the picture could be FFA or CCCA (author's synthesis from the sources above).

### 1.8 Alopecia areata (AA)

- **What it is:** an autoimmune, non-scarring hair loss. [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/)
- **How it presents:** one or more small, round, coin-sized bald patches that are smooth, not inflamed and not scaly. Short tapered "exclamation mark" hairs at the edge. Eyebrows, eyelashes and body hair can be affected. Some people have small pits in the nails. It can also be diffuse. [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/)
- **Course:** without treatment, complete regrowth within a year is likely for about 4 in 5 people with limited patches, but it can recur. If more than half the hair is lost at the start, or all of it at any stage, the chance of full recovery is about 1 in 10. [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/)
- **Treatments are specialist:** steroid creams and injections, contact immunotherapy, and oral JAK inhibitors. Ritlecitinib is NHS-funded for severe AA in people aged 12 and over; baricitinib is not NHS-funded. [BAD AA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/alopecia-areata/) The BAD publishes a living guideline for AA. [BAD living guideline 2025 (Harries, published Jan 2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41234147/)
- **Tell it apart:** sudden, sharply edged smooth patches. FPHL and TE do not make bald patches. Tinea capitis makes scaly patches (PCDS: send scale and plucked hairs for mycology if tinea is suspected). [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- **Online service:** AA is outside a pattern-hair-loss prescribing service. Refer to the GP or dermatology: PCDS lists "some cases of alopecia areata" as a reason to refer. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) Fast-track if the loss is rapid or extensive, or if eyebrows and lashes are going (author's synthesis based on the prognosis figures above).

### 1.9 Frontal fibrosing alopecia (FFA)

- **What it is:** a scarring hair loss of the front hairline. It mainly affects white women after the menopause but also occurs in premenopausal women, men and other ethnic groups. [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
- **Numbers:** in 355 patients from a multicentre review, 343 were women (49 premenopausal); mean age 61 (range 23 to 86). Severe disease was linked to eyelash loss, facial papules (small bumps) and body hair loss. [Vañó-Galván 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24508293/)
- **How it presents:** the frontal hairline slowly moves back. Eyebrow hair may be lost. There may be redness and flaky skin round individual hairs. The scarred skin looks paler and smoother than the forehead. Some people have itch, pain or burning; some have no symptoms. [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
- **Course:** "Once a hair has fallen out, it will not re-grow in this area." Treatment aims to slow or stop the loss. [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
- **Treatment (specialist):** potent topical steroids, topical tacrolimus, steroid injections, tetracyclines, hydroxychloroquine, immunosuppressants, and oral finasteride or dutasteride. [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/) In the 355-patient review, finasteride or dutasteride was used in 111 patients with improvement in 47% and stabilisation in 53%. [Vañó-Galván 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24508293/) A meta-analysis of 366 patients on oral dutasteride estimated stabilisation in 62.8% (95% CI 39.8% to 85.9%). [Seo 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38357767/)
- **Why it matters online:** a woman with FFA may ask for minoxidil for a "receding hairline." Minoxidil will not stop scarring. **Any hairline recession or eyebrow loss in a woman = refer for face-to-face assessment.**

### 1.10 Other scarring types

| Type | Who and where | Clues | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lichen planopilaris (LPP) | 2 to 5 times more common in women; usually age 40 to 60; crown and top of scalp | Itchy, painful, burning or tender scalp. Redness and scale round the base of hairs. Later smooth, shiny patches. Loss is usually permanent | [BAD LPP](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/lichen-planopilaris/) |
| Central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia (CCCA) | Mainly women of African descent, usually aged 30 to 40, with tightly curled hair | Starts as slight thinning on the midline of the crown and spreads outward. Often no symptoms; some have burning, itch or tenderness. Hair breakage may come first. Loss is permanent | [BAD CCCA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/central-centrifugal-cicatricial-alopecia/) |
| Discoid lupus, folliculitis decalvans, dissecting cellulitis, pseudopelade | Listed scarring causes | Pustules, crusting, tufted "doll's hair" (mainly folliculitis decalvans), boggy swellings. PCDS: refer dissecting cellulitis urgently because early treatment can prevent scarring | [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) |

**The CCCA trap.** Early CCCA looks like FPHL on the crown: thinning in the middle with an often normal-looking scalp. In a woman of African descent with crown thinning, ask about tenderness, itch and breakage and have a low bar for referral, because early treatment limits permanent loss. [BAD CCCA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/central-centrifugal-cicatricial-alopecia/)

### 1.11 One-page red-flag list for the online service

Stop and refer for face-to-face assessment (GP or dermatology) if any of these are present. Each line is traceable to a section above.

1. Scalp pain, burning, tenderness, marked itch, redness, scale round hairs, pustules, crusting or smooth shiny skin (possible scarring alopecia: urgent). [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
2. Hairline moving back, or eyebrow or eyelash loss (possible FFA or AA). [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
3. Discrete bald patches (AA, tinea, traction, hair pulling). [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
4. Signs of virilisation, rapid onset of androgen signs, or new androgen signs after the menopause (urgent endocrine or gynaecology referral). [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism), [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
5. Male-pattern loss in a woman. [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)
6. Systemic symptoms, or diffuse shedding for more than 6 months with no cause. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
7. Age under 18. Licensed topical minoxidil is for ages 18 to 65. [Regaine for Women 5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)
8. Diagnosis uncertain from history and photos, or moderate to severe distress. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

## Part 2. Tests

### 2.0 The short version

- **No blood test diagnoses female pattern hair loss.** The diagnosis is made from the history and the pattern, with a normal-looking scalp. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
- **Diffuse shedding or diffuse thinning: full blood count, ferritin and thyroid function.** PCDS: "all patients with diffuse alopecia require a FBC, ferritin and TFT." [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
- **Androgen tests only when there are signs of androgen excess.** [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)
- **Safety bloods depend on the medicine,** not on the hair loss (kidney function and potassium before spironolactone; liver tests with bicalutamide). See Part 3.
- The US and UK sources disagree on how much to test. A US review says: with no risk factors for nutrient deficiency, "further laboratory evaluation searching for nutritional deficiencies is not warranted." [Guo and Katta 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28243487/) PCDS suggests a wider panel for diffuse, female pattern or undetermined hair loss: FBC, ferritin, U&E, LFT, TFT, zinc and vitamin D. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

### 2.2 Ferritin: the debated thresholds

**Why the debate exists.** Laboratories flag iron deficiency at low cut-offs, but some hair specialists aim much higher. The studies conflict.

| Source | What it found or recommends |
|---|---|
| [BSG iron deficiency guideline 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/) (general medicine, not hair) | Ferritin below 15 µg/L shows absent iron stores. Below 30 µg/L generally shows low stores. A cut-off of 45 µg/L "has been suggested." Above 150 µg/L, true iron deficiency is unlikely even with inflammation |
| [Kantor 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14708596/) (cross-sectional, women) | Mean ferritin was lower in pattern hair loss (37.3 ng/mL, n = 52) and alopecia areata (24.9, n = 17) than in 11 women without hair loss (59.5). Telogen effluvium (50.1) was not significantly different. Small control group |
| [Olsen 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20947203/) (381 women with FPHL or chronic TE, 76 controls) | **No difference.** With ferritin ≤15 as the definition, iron deficiency was found in 12.4% of premenopausal women with FPHL, 12.1% with chronic TE and 29.8% of controls. With ≤40, it was 58.8%, 63.8% and 72.3%. "Iron deficiency is common in women but not increased in patients with FPHL or CTE" |
| [Treister-Goltzman 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35415182/) (meta-analysis, 36 studies, 10,029 women) | Women with non-scarring hair loss had lower ferritin: mean difference −18.51 (95% CI −25.85 to −11.16; the abstract prints the unit as ng/dL) |
| [Rushton 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12190640/) (expert review) | Recommends a ferritin of 70 µg/L, with a normal ESR (below 10 mm/h), in women with increased shedding. The author states the right level "is yet to be definitively established" |
| [Trost 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16635664/) (review) | "Insufficient evidence to recommend universal screening for iron deficiency in patients with hair loss" and insufficient evidence to recommend iron for hair loss with iron deficiency but no anaemia |
| [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/) (review) | Most authors supplement when ferritin is below 40; "there is insufficient evidence on the efficacy of the replacement of iron on the outcome of TE" |
| [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) (UK primary care) | "A trial of iron supplements is worthwhile for ferritin levels < 40," while noting the link is "controversial" |
| [Sinclair 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15787815/) (80 women on anti-androgens) | Serum ferritin did not predict who responded to treatment |

**A defensible position for the service (author's synthesis of the table above):**

1. Ferritin below 30 µg/L (or below the lab's lower limit): this is iron deficiency by general-medicine standards. Treat it and find the cause, whatever the hair is doing.
2. Ferritin 30 to 40 µg/L with shedding: a time-limited iron trial is supported by PCDS. Tell the woman the evidence that it helps hair is weak.
3. Ferritin above 40 µg/L: do not promise that pushing it to 70 will regrow hair. The "70" target is one expert's recommendation, and the best controlled study found no link.
4. Ferritin rises with inflammation, infection and cancer, so a "normal" result can hide deficiency. [Trost 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16635664/)
5. **Low haemoglobin with low ferritin (iron deficiency anaemia) is a referral, not a sale.** In men and postmenopausal women with new iron deficiency anaemia, gastroscopy and colonoscopy are generally first-line because about a third have an underlying abnormality, most often in the gut. All premenopausal women with iron deficiency anaemia should be screened for coeliac disease. [BSG 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/), [BSG 2021 abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34497146/)

### 2.3 Androgen tests: who, what and when

- **Who.** Women with signs of clinical hyperandrogenism (acne, hirsutism, irregular periods). [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia) Hair loss on its own, without hirsutism, is a relatively weak predictor of raised blood androgens. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/) The Endocrine Society suggests testing androgens in all women with an abnormal hirsutism score, and suggests against testing women with regular periods and only local unwanted hair. [Martin 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29522147/)
- **What.** Early-morning total testosterone and SHBG (sex hormone binding globulin), then the free androgen index = total testosterone ÷ SHBG × 100. Total testosterone alone has low sensitivity because SHBG levels change it. [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism) The international PCOS guideline recommends total and free testosterone (free can be estimated by the free androgen index), and androstenedione and DHEAS only if testosterone is not raised. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **When.** If repeating a raised result, preferably in the first 5 days of the cycle. [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)
- **The pill trap.** The combined pill raises SHBG and lowers androgen production, so testing on it is unreliable. If testing is essential, the pill should be stopped for at least 3 months, with other contraception used meanwhile. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/) Online, this means: do not order androgen bloods in a woman on the combined pill and then reassure her with a normal result.
- **What the result should do.** See the PCDS action table: lower-normal testosterone, no further evaluation; higher-normal or slightly raised, calculate the free androgen index and assess for PCOS; above 4.8 on repeat, evaluate for an androgen-secreting tumour (urgent referral). [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)

### 2.4 Two traps with supplements and tests

- **Biotin spoils blood tests.** "Exogenous biotin interferes with some laboratory tests, creating false negative or false positive results," and the US FDA linked biotin interference to a missed heart attack diagnosis. There is no evidence-based data that biotin supplements promote hair growth in people who are not deficient. [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/), [Patel 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28879195/), [FDA: biotin interference with troponin tests](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference) Ask every woman what supplements she takes before ordering thyroid or hormone tests. How long to stop biotin before a test depends on the dose and the lab's assay **[no single UK figure verified: ask the laboratory]**.
- **Too much of some supplements causes hair loss.** Too much vitamin A causes hair loss, and selenium toxicity can cause hair loss. "Excessive intakes of nutritional supplements may actually cause hair loss and are not recommended in the absence of a proven deficiency." [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/), [Rushton 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12190640/)

### 2.5 Baseline checks that are not blood tests

- **Blood pressure and pulse** before low-dose oral minoxidil (a blood pressure below 90/60 mm Hg and a history of fast or irregular heartbeat are named precautions). In people with no precautions, the international consensus says baseline blood tests and a baseline ECG are **not** routinely needed. [Akiska 2025, LDOM consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)
- **Pregnancy status and contraception** before any anti-androgen or oral minoxidil. See Part 3.
- **Mood.** Hair loss lowers self-esteem; PCDS lists moderate to severe distress as a reason to refer. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)

### 3.1 Topical minoxidil 2% and 5%

**Best evidence.**

- Cochrane review (47 trials, 5,290 women; 17 trials of minoxidil). Against placebo, more women on minoxidil (2% in five of the six pooled trials, 1% in the other) reported moderate to marked regrowth: 157 of 593 against 77 of 555 (risk ratio 1.93, 95% CI 1.51 to 2.47). That is about 26% against 14% (percentages calculated from the counts). Investigators rated the same way (risk ratio 2.35, 95% CI 1.68 to 3.28). Hair count rose by 13.18 more hairs per cm² than placebo (95% CI 10.92 to 15.44). [van Zuuren 2016, Cochrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225981/)
- 2% against 5%: the Cochrane review found no difference in effect or in adverse events (risk ratio for adverse events 1.02). [van Zuuren 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225981/) In the largest head-to-head trial (381 women, 48 weeks, both applied twice daily) 5% solution beat placebo on all three main measures; 2% beat placebo on hair count and investigator rating but not on the women's own rating; 5% caused more itch, irritation and unwanted hair. [Lucky 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15034503/)
- 5% foam once daily against vehicle foam (404 women, 24 weeks): 9.1 more hairs per cm² than vehicle at week 24. [Bergfeld 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27391639/) The SmPC gives +13.4 against +4.3 hairs per cm². [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)
- 5% foam once daily against 2% solution twice daily: two trials that do not fully agree **[corrected in fact-check]**. In the larger one (322 women) the gains were almost the same, +23.9 against +24.2 hairs per cm² at week 24, but the difference was −0.3 (95% CI −6.0 to 5.4) and the trial **missed its pre-set non-inferiority test** (the lower limit had to stay above −5.0). [Blume-Peytavi 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27391640/) In the earlier, smaller one (113 women) the foam was non-inferior on hair count and caused less itch and dandruff (it has no propylene glycol). [Blume-Peytavi 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21700360/) So "the foam once a day works like the 2% solution twice a day" is likely, but not formally proven.
- PCDS estimates that topical minoxidil benefits about 40% of patients and does not change the natural history of the hair loss. [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)

**Plain meaning.** Roughly one woman in four notices clear regrowth, about twice the placebo rate; PCDS puts the share who benefit in some way at about 40%. The gain is modest and lasts only while she keeps using it.

**Dose.**

- 2% solution: 1 ml twice a day to the affected scalp, maximum 2 ml a day. [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc)
- 5% foam: 1 g (half a capful) once a day; do not exceed 1 g a day. [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)
- 5% **solution** is not licensed for women, and the UK product documents go further **[corrected in fact-check]**: the SmPCs for the men's 5% solutions list use "in women" as a **contraindication** (one gives the reason: "occasional signs of cosmetically distressing, reversible, facial hair growth"). [Regaine for Men Extra Strength 5% solution SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc), [Minoxidil 5% spray SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100306/smpc) The BAD says the 5% solution "can be used under the advice of a healthcare professional", and PCDS says the 5% formula is used once daily in women. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia) So giving a men's 5% solution to a woman is off-label use against a listed contraindication. It is a prescriber's decision with recorded reasons (tier B in Part 4), not a general-sale supply. The licensed 5% product for women is the foam.
- Apply to a dry scalp, not to the hair. [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc), [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**How long before judging it.** Regrowth may take 12 to 24 weeks with the foam; stop if there is no improvement after 24 weeks. With the 2% solution results may take four months or more; stop if there is no improvement after one year. [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc) The BAD says use it for at least 6 months before judging. PCDS: best results at 6 to 12 months. [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [PCDS pattern alopecia](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)

**The early shed.** Shedding can rise 2 to 6 weeks after starting. The SmPC tells users to stop and see a doctor if shedding persists for more than 2 weeks; the BAD says the early hair fall in the first 4 to 6 weeks usually settles as new hairs grow. Warn every woman before she starts, or she will stop. [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**Side effects.** Very common: headache. Common: itch, rash, dermatitis, unwanted facial hair, hypersensitivity, chest pain, swollen ankles, weight gain. [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc) In trials of 1,333 women, 4% reported unwanted facial or body hair, more with 5% than 2%; it reversed and did not always need treatment to stop. [Dawber and Rundegren 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12702063/)

**Do not supply if** **[wording corrected in fact-check]**. The listed contraindications (SmPC section 4.3) are: treated or untreated high blood pressure; any scalp abnormality (including psoriasis and sunburn); a shaved scalp; use with occlusive dressings or other scalp medicines; allergy to an ingredient. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are **not** in the contraindications list, but section 4.6 says the product "should not be used during pregnancy or lactation", so the practical rule is the same: do not supply. People with cardiovascular disease or an arrhythmia should see a doctor first. [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc)

**A licence limit that is easy to miss (added in fact-check).** Both SmPCs (section 4.4) say topical minoxidil is only for androgenetic alopecia. The foam SmPC says it "is not indicated when there is no family history of hair loss, hair loss is sudden and/or patchy, hair loss is due to childbirth, or the reason for hair loss is unknown." [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc) What this means for the service:

- A licensed (tier A) supply needs a positive diagnosis of female pattern hair loss, not just "hair loss".
- Using it for telogen effluvium, shedding after childbirth or traction alopecia is **off-label**, even though the product is on general sale.
- A woman with typical FPHL but no family history falls outside the SmPC wording: record why you made the diagnosis.

**Counsel.** Stop and seek advice for chest pain, fast heartbeat, faintness, dizziness, sudden weight gain or swollen hands or feet. Keep it off children: babies have grown excess hair after contact with a carer's treated skin, and swallowing it can cause serious heart effects. Wash any spill off the face. Both products are flammable (the foam is an "extremely flammable aerosol"): do not use while smoking or near a flame or strong heat. [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc), [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**Monitoring.** None by blood test. Review photos at about 6 months (author's synthesis from the timings above).

### 3.2 Low-dose oral minoxidil (LDOM)

**Licence.** Off-label. Loniten tablets are licensed only for severe hypertension, at a starting dose of 5 mg a day, alongside a diuretic and a beta-blocker. [Loniten SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc) The BAD lists it as an option and says low doses "may be at least as effective as topical and well tolerated, in certain scenarios." [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)

**Best evidence (there is no placebo-controlled trial in women that this search could find **[unverified: none found]**).**

- Oral 1 mg against topical 5% solution once daily in women with FPHL, 24 weeks, randomised but **open-label and small** (25 women in the oral arm) **[corrected in fact-check]**. A review describes the result as a trend towards more improvement with the tablet that "was not significantly superior to topical minoxidil." The trial was not built to show that the two are equal, so it does not prove the tablet is "as good as" the solution. [Ramos 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31473295/), [Gupta 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10806356/) The review's table gives total hair density rising from 164.6 to 184.7 hairs per cm² on oral 1 mg, with hypertrichosis in 7 of 25 (28%) and leg swelling in 1 of 25 (the trial letter itself has no abstract and could not be opened, so these are second-hand). [Gupta 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10806356/)
- Oral 1 mg against topical 5%, 65 patients, 6 months: both improved hair diameter with no significant difference; the authors judged topical to have the slightly better overall effect. [Asilian 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38031516/)
- Network meta-analysis of single-drug treatment in women (13 trials), ranked by chance of being best for hair density: finasteride 5 mg, then 5% topical minoxidil solution twice daily, then oral minoxidil 1 mg, then 5% foam; oral minoxidil 0.25 mg ranked low. Rankings like this are weak evidence. [Gupta 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37386777/)
- 148 women on 0.25 to 2 mg (median 1 mg; 84.5% also on other treatments): 79.7% improved (64.2% slightly, 15.5% markedly). Hypertrichosis in 17%; 2% stopped. No control group. [Rodrigues-Barata 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7325226/)
- 100 women on minoxidil 0.25 mg with spironolactone 25 mg for 12 months: hair-loss severity fell by a mean of 1.3 points on the Sinclair scale (baseline 2.79) and shedding score by 2.6. Mean blood pressure change −4.52 systolic and −6.48 diastolic mm Hg. Side effects in 8 women; 2 stopped (urticaria). No control group. [Sinclair 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29231239/)
- In men, 5 mg oral was not better than 5% topical twice daily for hair density in a blinded trial. [Penha 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598226/)

**Plain meaning** **[corrected in fact-check]**. At about 1 mg a day it has not been shown to work better than topical minoxidil, and the trials are too small to prove it works equally well. There is no placebo-controlled trial in women. Its real advantage is convenience and no scalp irritation, paid for with more face and body hair and a small risk of systemic effects.

**Safety numbers.**

- 1,404 patients (943 women) across several centres: hypertrichosis 15.1% (0.5% stopped for it); lightheadedness 1.7%; fluid retention 1.3%; fast heartbeat 0.9%; headache 0.4%; swelling round the eyes 0.3%; insomnia 0.2%. Systemic effects led 1.2% to stop. No life-threatening effects. [Vañó-Galván 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33639244/)
- A 2026 review puts hypertrichosis at 24%, temporary shedding at 16 to 22% and mild ankle swelling at 2%, and calls pericardial effusion "rare at doses used for alopecia." [Ong 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41118052/)
- **Extra hair is much more common when a trial looks for it [added in fact-check].** The figures above come from retrospective case-note reviews, which count only what was recorded. In a double-blind trial in women that questioned and examined participants at each visit, the group on oral minoxidil 1 mg alone (with placebo) had facial hypertrichosis in 27 of 37 (73%) and hair growth elsewhere on the body in 15 of 37 (41%) at 24 weeks; early shedding was recorded in 14 of 37 (38%) at week 4. [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/) In the small open-label trial hypertrichosis was reported in 7 of 25 (28%). [Gupta 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10806356/) So tell a woman that some extra facial hair is likely at 1 mg, not that it is an uncommon side effect.
- With spironolactone (432 women; average doses when an adverse effect occurred were 87.6 mg spironolactone and 1.8 mg minoxidil): any adverse effect 37.7%; hypertrichosis 12.3%; dizziness or lightheadedness 12.0%. Taking another blood-pressure-altering medicine tripled the odds of dizziness on standing (odds ratio 3.29). [Dewey 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41654020/)
- **Fluid round the heart (pericardial effusion).** At blood-pressure doses it was seen in about 3 to 5% of patients not on dialysis. [Loniten SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc) At hair-loss doses there are published case reports. [Dlova 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36117778/), [Trüeb 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36425662/)

**Dose.**

| Source | Starting dose for women | Range |
|---|---|---|
| [International consensus (43 hair specialists, 12 countries)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/) | 1.25 mg daily | 0.625 to 5 mg daily. The panel refused to set strict upper limits and no titration scheme reached consensus |
| [Royal Berkshire NHS leaflet, Oct 2025](https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/n3onzj1d/minoxidil-for-hair-loss_oct25.pdf) | 0.625 mg every other day (a quarter of a 2.5 mg tablet, cut with a pill cutter) | 0.625 to 2.5 mg |
| [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia) | 0.25 mg a day | Up to 1 mg a day if tolerated |

**Making small doses from UK tablets [corrected in fact-check].** The smallest UK tablet is 2.5 mg (5 mg and 10 mg tablets also exist). What the product documents allow:

- Loniten 2.5 mg: "The tablet can be divided into equal halves." That gives 1.25 mg, the consensus starting dose. [Loniten 2.5 mg SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc)
- A generic 2.5 mg tablet: "The score line is not intended for breaking the tablet." So with this brand even halving is outside the product information. [Minoxidil 2.5 mg Tablets SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13821/smpc)
- No SmPC supports cutting a tablet into quarters. The 0.625 mg dose (a quarter tablet cut with a pill cutter, as in the Royal Berkshire leaflet) is therefore an approximate dose made outside the product information.
- The 0.25 mg dose that PCDS mentions cannot be made from tablets at all. It needs an unlicensed special.

For the service (author's synthesis): name the brand on the prescription if the dose depends on splitting, tell the woman a cut-tablet dose is approximate, and record why a special was chosen if one is used.

**Do not prescribe if (consensus contraindications):** history of pericardial effusion, tamponade or pericarditis; congestive heart failure; pulmonary hypertension with mitral stenosis; phaeochromocytoma; pregnant or breastfeeding; a medicine with a significant interaction. **Take advice first (consensus precautions):** history of fast heartbeat or other arrhythmia; blood pressure below 90/60 mm Hg; kidney impairment; dialysis. [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/) ([open-access copy](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2pp1b5gc/qt2pp1b5gc_noSplash_71a7c4141708a558a1b509800bdb7742.pdf)) UK sources add: cardiac disease and severe liver impairment ([PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)); people already on blood-pressure medicines ([Royal Berkshire](https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/n3onzj1d/minoxidil-for-hair-loss_oct25.pdf)). The SmPC says minoxidil "is not recommended during pregnancy and in women of childbearing potential not using contraception." [Loniten SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc)

**Older women (gap found in fact-check).** The file sets an upper age of 65 for licensed topical minoxidil but none for the tablet. The SmPC says there are "no extensive clinical studies with minoxidil in patients over age 65" and that older patients "may be sensitive to the blood pressure lowering effect" with a risk of dizziness on standing. [Loniten SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/4294/smpc) The service should decide and write down its own upper age limit or extra checks for oral minoxidil (author's note).

**Monitoring.**

- With no precautions, baseline blood tests and a baseline ECG are not routinely needed (90.7% and 93.0% of the panel agreed). Blood pressure monitoring "may be recommended" when a precaution is present. [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)
- Be clear about what the consensus does **not** say (added in fact-check): the paper states that a baseline blood pressure measurement, ECG and specialist opinion "were not recommended by at least 90% of experts when prescribing LDOM unless a precaution was identified." The rule in this file (a real blood pressure and pulse reading before starting) is stricter than the consensus on purpose, because a remote service cannot see or examine the woman. It is the author's choice, supported by the NHS leaflet below, not a consensus requirement. [Akiska 2025, open-access copy](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2pp1b5gc/qt2pp1b5gc_noSplash_71a7c4141708a558a1b509800bdb7742.pdf)
- A UK NHS service is stricter: "you should have your pulse and blood pressure monitored regularly by your GP." [Royal Berkshire](https://www.royalberkshire.nhs.uk/media/n3onzj1d/minoxidil-for-hair-loss_oct25.pdf)
- Tell her to watch for lightheadedness and a fast or irregular heartbeat for at least 3 to 7 days after starting or raising the dose, and for swollen feet or face, weight gain, chest pain or breathlessness for at least 4 to 6 weeks. [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)
- Expect the first benefit at 3 months at the earliest; if she had an early shed, perhaps not until 6 months. [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)

**When it is a reasonable choice (consensus):** topical minoxidil caused irritation, residue or styling problems, did not work or plateaued, or is less convenient. [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)

**Online service view (author's synthesis).** LDOM is a cardiovascular medicine used off-label. A safe online service needs a real blood pressure and pulse reading, a medicines list checked against the GP record, a contraception check, a two-way consultation and a letter to the GP. That follows from the GPhC safeguards quoted in 3.0.

### 3.3 Spironolactone

**Licence.** Off-label for hair loss, acne and hirsutism. [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc)

**Best evidence (no placebo-controlled trial in FPHL; the Cochrane review asked for one).** [van Zuuren 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225981/)

- Meta-analysis of trials and observational studies: 56.60% of women improved overall; 65.80% when combined with another treatment and 43.21% on spironolactone alone. 37.80% had no or only modest improvement. High heterogeneity. [Aleissa 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37719557/)
- 80 women with biopsy-proven FPHL on spironolactone 200 mg or cyproterone for at least 12 months, blinded photo scoring: 44% regrew hair, 44% stayed the same, 12% kept losing hair. No difference between the two drugs. No placebo arm. [Sinclair 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15787815/)
- 60 women, randomised: spironolactone 100 mg plus 2% minoxidil did better than finasteride 5 mg plus 2% minoxidil at 4 months (treatment judged "excellent" in 56.7% against 0%). Small and short. [Sadeghzadeh Bazargan 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37650533/)
- Systematic review (7 studies, 618 patients): oral doses ranged from 25 to 200 mg, mostly 80 to 110 mg; better with minoxidil than alone. [Wang 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36923692/)

**Plain meaning.** Spironolactone mostly stops things getting worse; about four in ten regrow some hair, more when it is added to minoxidil. The evidence is low quality.

**Dose.**

- UK NHS dermatology leaflet: start 50 mg once daily for a month; if tolerated and bloods are normal, 100 mg for a month, then 150 mg, to a maximum of 200 mg. Usual dose 100 to 200 mg once daily. Most women notice improvement after about 6 months; continue for 12 to 18 months to see the full effect. [Gloucestershire Hospitals leaflet](https://www.gloshospitals.nhs.uk/your-visit/patient-information-leaflets/spironolactone-for-female-pattern-hair-loss/)
- International PCOS guideline: 25 to 100 mg a day "appears to have lower risks of adverse effects." [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)

**Side effects.** In the UK SAFA acne trial (410 women, 50 mg rising to 100 mg): any adverse reaction 64% against 51% on placebo; headache 20% against 12%; dizziness 19% against 12% (not significant); no serious adverse reactions. [Santer 2023, SAFA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10543374/) The NHS leaflet lists sore breasts, irregular periods and dizziness on standing as common. [Gloucestershire Hospitals leaflet](https://www.gloshospitals.nhs.uk/your-visit/patient-information-leaflets/spironolactone-for-female-pattern-hair-loss/) Pooled hair-loss studies: menstrual disorders 11.85%. [Aleissa 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37719557/)

**Cancer worry.** Rats given high doses long-term developed tumours ([Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc)). In people, a meta-analysis of 7 studies (4.5 million people) found no link with breast cancer (risk ratio 1.04, 95% CI 0.86 to 1.22; certainty very low). [Bommareddy 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35138351/)

**Is potassium monitoring needed in healthy young women?**

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| [Plovanich 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25796182/): 974 healthy young women on spironolactone for acne | 13 high potassium results in 1,802 tests (0.72%), the same as the 0.76% background rate. 6 of the 13 were normal on repeat; no action was needed in the rest. "Routine potassium monitoring is unnecessary for healthy women taking spironolactone for acne" |
| [Thiede 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31360748/): 124 women with before-and-after tests | High potassium in 1 of 112 women aged 18 to 45 (under 1%) and 2 of 12 aged 46 to 65 (16.7%). Monitoring "is warranted for women over 45" |
| [Gregoire 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655839/): 398 women aged 45 and over | High potassium in 10.1%: 7.9% at ages 45 to 64, 22.4% at 65 and over; 6.3% in healthy women aged 45 to 64. 97.5% were mild |
| [SAFA trial authors](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10543374/) (UK) | Baseline kidney function and potassium are advisable; "ongoing monitoring is unnecessary for most young women" |
| [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc) (written for heart failure) | Potassium and creatinine 1 week after starting or raising the dose, monthly for 3 months, quarterly for a year, then 6-monthly |

**A defensible rule for the service (author's synthesis of the table):** check U&E (kidney function and potassium) before starting in everyone. No routine repeat tests in a healthy woman under 45 with normal baseline results and no interacting medicines. Repeat after starting and after dose rises, then periodically, in women aged 45 and over, with kidney, heart or liver disease, diabetes, or any interacting medicine (this includes a contraceptive pill that contains drospirenone: check potassium in the first cycle, see the interactions below). Record the reason whenever you depart from the SmPC schedule.

**Do not prescribe if:** acute or significant kidney impairment, Addison's disease, high potassium, or with eplerenone or another potassium-sparing diuretic. [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc)

**Interactions that raise potassium or matter** **[checked and extended in fact-check]**. The SmPC names: ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, other potassium-sparing diuretics and aldosterone blockers, potassium supplements, salt substitutes that contain potassium and a potassium-rich diet, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), heparin and low molecular weight heparin, and co-trimoxazole (the SmPC warns of severe or "clinically relevant" high potassium with these); lithium (levels rise: monitor); digoxin (levels may rise). Aspirin, indometacin and mefenamic acid may also reduce its effect. [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc)

- **Trimethoprim alone** is the part of co-trimoxazole that would be expected to raise potassium, but the Aldactone SmPC names only co-trimoxazole **[trimethoprim alone: unverified, BNF could not be opened]**. Treat it with the same caution until the BNF has been checked.
- **Pills that contain drospirenone (for example Yasmin)** (gap found in fact-check). Drospirenone is itself "an aldosterone antagonist with potassium sparing properties." The Yasmin SmPC says its use with aldosterone antagonists or potassium-sparing diuretics "has not been studied. In this case, serum potassium should be tested during the first treatment cycle." [Yasmin SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1607/smpc) This matters here because women with acne, hirsutism or PCOS are often on a drospirenone pill, and they are the same women who are offered spironolactone. Ask which pill she takes; if it contains drospirenone, check potassium in the first cycle of the combination.

**Contraception and pregnancy.**

- Spironolactone and cyproterone "should be avoided in pregnancy since they can cause feminisation of a male foetus." [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf) The SmPC wording is: "Spironolactone should not be used during pregnancy, unless the potential benefit justifies the potential risk"; animal studies showed reproductive toxicity from its anti-androgenic effect. [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc) Hair loss never justifies that risk (author's note). The UKTIS pregnancy monograph could not be opened and still needs to be read (Part 5).
- Women who can become pregnant "should be strongly counselled to use effective contraception" with any anti-androgen. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/) The Endocrine Society recommends against anti-androgen monotherapy unless adequate contraception is used. [Martin 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29522147/)
- NHS leaflet: use reliable contraception; the combined pill or a hormonal coil may also help the irregular periods; it is usually considered safe to try for a baby one month after stopping. [Gloucestershire Hospitals leaflet](https://www.gloshospitals.nhs.uk/your-visit/patient-information-leaflets/spironolactone-for-female-pattern-hair-loss/)
- In the SAFA trial, seven pregnancies still happened despite contraceptive advice at every visit. Ask at every review. [Santer 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10543374/)
- If she uses tirzepatide (Mounjaro) and relies on the pill: the SmPC advises switching to a non-oral method or adding a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose increase. [Mounjaro SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15484/smpc)
- Breastfeeding: sources disagree. The SmPC and the BAD say do not use. LactMed says it is poorly excreted in milk and "appears to be acceptable." [Aldactone SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1619/smpc), [LactMed spironolactone](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30000160/)

### 3.4 Finasteride and dutasteride in women

**Licence and regulator position.** Finasteride 1 mg "is not indicated for use in women" and is contraindicated in women because of the risk in pregnancy. The same SmPC states: "Lack of efficacy was demonstrated in post-menopausal women with androgenetic alopecia." [Propecia SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2194/smpc) Dutasteride is contraindicated in women. [Avodart SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc) NHS: "Women should not use finasteride." [NHS hair loss](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/hair-loss/)

**Evidence by dose.**

| Dose | Study | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Finasteride 1 mg | Randomised, placebo-controlled, 137 postmenopausal women, 12 months | **No benefit** on hair count, photos or biopsy. [Price 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11050579/) Cochrane pooled result: risk ratio 0.95 (95% CI 0.66 to 1.37), "no more effective than placebo." [van Zuuren 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225981/) |
| Finasteride 1.25 mg | Retrospective sample, 3 years | Hair thickness rose in 81.7% of women. No control group. [Boersma 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25382509/) |
| Finasteride 2.5 mg | 37 premenopausal women also on a drospirenone and ethinylestradiol pill, 12 months | 62% improved on photos. No control group, and the pill itself is anti-androgenic. [Iorizzo 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16549704/) |
| Finasteride 5 mg | 87 women, 12 months | Hair density 90 to 107 per cm²; 81.4% improved on photos (most only slightly). No control group. [Yeon 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20569283/) |
| Finasteride 5 mg | 40 postmenopausal women, 18 months | Most improved on photos; 4 reported lasting loss of libido; 1 had raised liver enzymes. No control group. [Oliveira-Soares 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23960392/) |
| Dutasteride 0.15 mg | Retrospective sample, 3 years | Hair thickness rose in 83.3%; better than finasteride 1.25 mg in women under 50. No control group. [Boersma 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25382509/) |

A network meta-analysis ranked finasteride 5 mg first and finasteride 1 mg last among single-drug options for women. [Gupta 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37386777/) A systematic review found 65 studies (2,683 women) with doses from 0.5 to 5 mg and called for long-term studies. [Hu 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30604525/)

**Plain meaning.** The only good trial (1 mg) was negative. Higher doses look helpful in uncontrolled studies only.

**Pregnancy risk.** These medicines can cause abnormal external genitalia in a male fetus. Crushed or broken finasteride tablets should not be handled by women who are or may be pregnant. Dutasteride is absorbed through the skin, so women must avoid leaking capsules, and its half-life is about 3 to 5 weeks. [Propecia SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2194/smpc), [Avodart SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6758/smpc) One expert table (a letter, so opinion rather than a guideline) gives the gap to leave before pregnancy as 1 month for finasteride, 6 months for dutasteride, 1 month for spironolactone and 3 months for bicalutamide. [Gil-Redondo 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12493240/)

**Other safety.** The MHRA (May 2026) states finasteride is "associated with depression, suicidal ideation and sexual dysfunction which may persist after treatment is stopped," asks prescribers to ask about a history of depression or suicidal thoughts and to review regularly, and has added a precautionary mood warning to dutasteride. The Yellow Card data were mainly from men on 1 mg. [MHRA Drug Safety Update, 11 May 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a01bbda2a6137e93226b965/DSU_Finasteride_and_Dutasteride.pdf)

**Who can have them.** One expert review says finasteride is off-label in women "and should only be considered in post-menopausal females," with dutasteride as a later option. [Gupta 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39880789/) They also have a specialist role in frontal fibrosing alopecia (1.9).

**Online service view (author's synthesis).** Do not offer oral finasteride or dutasteride to women in a first-version online service. The licence contraindicates it, the one good trial is negative, and a pregnancy would be a serious harm. If it is ever added, restrict it to women who cannot become pregnant, with a specialist's recommendation, MHRA mood counselling and GP sharing.

**Topical finasteride.** In 30 postmenopausal women, 0.25% finasteride with 3% minoxidil gave thicker hairs than 3% minoxidil alone at 24 weeks, but blood DHT fell, which shows the drug is absorbed; the authors said it "should be reserved for postmenopausal women." [Suchonwanit 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30206824/) A 26-woman uncontrolled series reported density rising from 149 to 173 hairs per cm² at 52 weeks. [Piraccini 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42876360/) The US FDA warned in April 2025 about adverse events with compounded topical finasteride, most of which were reported to persist after stopping. [FDA alert](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded) UK licence status of any topical finasteride product for women: **[unverified, none found on emc]**.

### 3.5 Bicalutamide

- **Licence:** prostate cancer only; "contraindicated in females." [Casodex SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5491/smpc)
- **Best evidence (the only randomised trial):** 74 women; bicalutamide 25 mg plus oral minoxidil 1 mg against placebo plus minoxidil 1 mg for 24 weeks. Hair density rose by 18.1 against 21.5 hairs per cm² (P = 0.86). **No added benefit.** It did reduce minoxidil's facial hair growth (49% against 73%) and the early shed (16% against 38%). [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/)
- **Liver:** "Severe hepatic changes and hepatic failure have been observed rarely... and fatal outcomes have been reported." [Casodex SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5491/smpc) At hair-loss doses, a 316-woman series (mean 13.1 mg, range 10 to 50 mg) reported a mild rise in liver enzymes in 3%, and a pilot using mostly 50 mg reported 11%, as summarised by the trial authors. [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/), [Ismail 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32213304/) The international PCOS guideline warns of "an increased risk of severe liver toxicity" with flutamide and bicalutamide. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **Monitoring used in the trial:** liver tests and a pregnancy test at baseline, 4, 12 and 24 weeks, and highly effective contraception. [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/)
- **Online service view:** not suitable. No proven benefit over minoxidil, a rare risk of fatal liver injury, and blood tests needed at set times.

### 3.6 Cyproterone and combined pills

- **Trial evidence.** 66 women randomised for 12 cycles: minoxidil 2% (plus a combined pill) increased thick hairs by 6.5 per 0.36 cm²; cyproterone 52 mg with ethinylestradiol 35 micrograms **reduced** them by 2.4. Cyproterone did better only in women with other signs of androgen excess or a high body mass index. [Vexiau 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12072067/) In the 80-woman open study, cyproterone (50 mg daily, or 100 mg for 10 days a month) and spironolactone performed the same. [Sinclair 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15787815/)
- **Meningioma (a brain-lining tumour).** MHRA, June 2020: the risk rises with cumulative dose and is highest at 25 mg a day and above. Incidence per 100,000 patient-years: 4.5 at under 3 g cumulative dose, 23.8 at 3 g or more, 129.1 at over 60 g. Do not use in anyone with a current or past meningioma. [MHRA Drug Safety Update, June 2020](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/cyproterone-acetate-new-advice-to-minimise-risk-of-meningioma) The international PCOS guideline says cyproterone at 10 mg or more "is not advised." [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **Liver.** Dose-related liver toxicity, including hepatitis and liver failure. [Androcur SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15996/smpc)
- **Co-cyprindiol (Dianette).** Licensed for moderate to severe androgen-sensitive acne and/or hirsutism, only after topical or antibiotic treatment has failed for acne; not recommended solely for contraception. Blood clot (VTE) risk is 1.5 to 2 times that of levonorgestrel pills. Contraindicated with a current or past meningioma. Symptoms take at least three months to improve. [Dianette SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1122/smpc) The PCOS guideline places it second-line to other combined pills. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
- **Combined pills in general.** The PCOS guideline says a combined pill could be recommended for hirsutism or irregular cycles, and that anti-androgens combined with the pill "may be trialled" for female pattern hair loss. [2023 International PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/) No randomised trial showing that a combined pill alone regrows scalp hair was found **[unverified: none found]**. Stopping the pill is a listed trigger for telogen effluvium. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)
- **Online service view:** do not start cyproterone 50 mg. A woman with acne, hirsutism and hair thinning who needs contraception is a GP or sexual-health decision, not a hair-loss sale.

### 3.7 Topical spironolactone and other topical anti-androgens

- **Topical spironolactone** (1% gel or 5% solution twice daily in the studies): a systematic review found it helped in small studies with "significantly fewer side effects" than oral, and worked better with minoxidil. The review covered only 7 studies for oral and topical together. [Wang 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36923692/) No licensed UK product was found on emc **[unverified]**, so it would be an unlicensed special.
- **Topical finasteride:** see 3.4.
- **Clascoterone (cortexolone 17α-propionate)** and other topical anti-androgens: a 2020 review called them promising for male pattern hair loss and said there is "a critical need to include more women in study populations." [Marks 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31832993/) No UK licence for hair loss was found **[unverified]**.
- **Online service view:** low-quality evidence and unlicensed products. Not a first-line offer.

### 3.8 Ketoconazole shampoo

- **Evidence:** a systematic review found 5 human studies (318 participants) reporting thicker hair shafts and better photos, with no randomised controlled trials: "a promising adjunctive or alternative therapy... Randomized controlled trials are needed." [Fields 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31858672/)
- **Licence and legal status [corrected in fact-check]:** ketoconazole 2% shampoo does **not** need a prescription. The same 2% strength is sold in three legal classes: a general-sale pack licensed for dandruff ([Nizoral Antidandruff Shampoo SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6456/smpc)), a pharmacy pack licensed for dandruff and seborrhoeic dermatitis ([Nizoral Dandruff Treatment Shampoo SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/6764/smpc)) and a prescription-only pack that also covers pityriasis versicolor ([Nizoral 2% Shampoo SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1510/smpc)). None is licensed for hair loss.
- **Dose (the same in all three):** wash twice weekly for 2 to 4 weeks, leave on for 3 to 5 minutes, then once every 1 to 2 weeks to prevent return. "No known risks" in pregnancy or breastfeeding. [Nizoral 2% Shampoo SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/1510/smpc)
- **Use it for what it is licensed for.** Treat a flaky, itchy scalp. Do not sell it as a hair-growth treatment. One expert review notes that "eliminating scaling problems is important" in managing hair loss. [Rushton 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12190640/)

### 3.9 Hormone replacement therapy (HRT) and hair

- A 2026 systematic review (40 studies) found FPHL and frontal fibrosing alopecia had the strongest links with the menopause. Data on HRT were "limited and inconsistent," most studies used older HRT types, and HRT was linked to an **increased** risk of frontal fibrosing alopecia. [Roster 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41331233/)
- A pilot in 11 postmenopausal women found the frontal hairline score improved after 6 months of estradiol HRT; no control group. [Endo 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37915403/)
- A review for dermatologists describes a "paucity of data and discrepancy in the literature" on how specific hormone treatments affect hair. [Desai 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33465769/)
- **Bottom line (author's synthesis):** do not start or change HRT to treat hair. If a woman has menopausal symptoms, HRT is decided on those with her GP or menopause service. Record what HRT she takes, and remember that stopping a hormone treatment can trigger shedding. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)

### 3.10 Iron and other nutritional correction

- **Iron.** Correct proven deficiency. For iron deficiency anaemia the BSG recommends one tablet a day of ferrous sulfate, fumarate or gluconate (one tablet every other day if not tolerated), continued for about 3 months after the haemoglobin is normal. [BSG 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/) For low ferritin without anaemia and with shedding, PCDS supports a trial of iron below 40. [PCDS overview](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview) Be honest about the evidence: "There is insufficient evidence on the efficacy of the replacement of iron on the outcome of TE." [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/)
- **Vitamin D.** NHS advice for everyone: consider 10 micrograms a day in autumn and winter (all year for people who are rarely outdoors, cover most of their skin, or have dark skin); do not exceed 100 micrograms (4,000 IU) a day. [NHS vitamin D](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/vitamins-and-minerals/vitamin-d/) Supplement a proven deficiency; do not promise regrowth. [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/)
- **Zinc, B12, folate, biotin.** Correct a proven deficiency only. See 2.1 and 2.4.
- **Avoid harm.** High-dose vitamin A and selenium can cause hair loss; biotin distorts blood tests. [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/)
- **Diet.** People with risk factors (restrictive diets, rapid weight loss, malabsorption) should be assessed; without risk factors, hunting for deficiencies is not warranted, and some supplements carry a risk of worsening hair loss. [Guo and Katta 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28243487/)

### 3.11 Microneedling, laser devices and platelet-rich plasma (PRP)

| Treatment | Best evidence and effect size | Caveats | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|

A network meta-analysis put the gain at 13 hairs per cm² over minoxidil alone at 24 weeks. In women only (14 trials, 919 patients), microneedling plus minoxidil ranked first, with evidence certainty "low to very low" | Mostly small trials, many in men. **Online service view (author's synthesis).** None of these needs a prescription, and none has evidence as solid as topical minoxidil. If the service mentions them, describe them as optional add-ons with uncertain visible benefit.

[Maas 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43048788/) This file follows the SmPCs.

**Shedding after childbirth.** Treat it as telogen effluvium: explain the timing, check for iron deficiency and thyroid disease if it is severe or goes on beyond 6 months, and reassure. Medicine does not speed regrowth in telogen effluvium. [BAD TE page](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/telogen-effluvium/)

**Online service rule (author's synthesis).** A woman who is pregnant, trying to conceive or breastfeeding gets advice and safe scalp care only. Follow the UK SmPC (no minoxidil), even though LactMed is more relaxed about topical minoxidil in established breastfeeding.

### 3.13 Hair shedding linked to GLP-1 weight-loss medicines

**How often.**

| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| [Wegovy (semaglutide) 2.4 mg SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13803/smpc) | Hair loss in 2.5% on semaglutide 2.4 mg against 1.0% on placebo. With the 7.2 mg dose, 5.3% against 1.0%. Mostly mild; most recovered while continuing treatment. More frequent in people who lost 20% or more of their weight |
| [Mounjaro (tirzepatide) SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15484/smpc) | Hair loss in 4.9% on tirzepatide against 1.0% on placebo in the weight-management trials. Mostly mild; most recovered while continuing; nobody stopped the drug because of it |
| [Cheng 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42155605/) (meta-analysis, 9 interventional studies, 4,114 users) | Risk ratio for hair loss 3.25 against placebo (95% CI 1.44 to 7.36); 3.9% of users affected |
| [Viquez Burboa 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42621629/) (meta-analysis, 17 observational studies) | Odds ratio 1.40 for any non-scarring hair loss. At 12 months: telogen effluvium 1.76, pattern hair loss 1.64, alopecia areata not raised |
| [Gupta 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41998799/) (systematic review, 24 studies) | Highest rates with semaglutide and tirzepatide; women "disproportionately affected"; rapid weight loss a likely contributor |

**What it is.** Mostly telogen effluvium, probably driven by the speed of weight loss; causality is not proven and no prospective controlled study has looked at it directly. [Piraccini 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42249225/) "Marked weight loss and extreme dieting" is a classic trigger. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)

**What to do (author's synthesis; no UK guideline on this was found).**

1. Confirm the timing: shedding starting about 3 months into rapid weight loss fits telogen effluvium.
2. Check the pattern in photos. A widening parting means FPHL is also present and can be treated in the usual way.
3. Check full blood count, ferritin and thyroid function (the core set for diffuse shedding), and ask about food intake.
4. Reassure: the SmPCs report that most people recovered while continuing treatment.
5. Do not advise stopping the weight-loss medicine for hair alone. That is a decision for the woman and its prescriber.
6. If the woman is on tirzepatide and relies on the pill, remember the contraception warning in 3.3 before adding spironolactone or oral minoxidil.

### 3.14 What to tell a woman to expect (summary for counselling)

| Treatment | Honest expectation | First review |
|---|---|---|
| Topical minoxidil | About 1 in 4 see clear regrowth (about twice placebo). Possible early shed. Works only while used | 6 months |
| Oral minoxidil (off-label) | Not shown to be better than topical; small trials cannot prove it is equal. Extra face or body hair in roughly 15 to 25% in case-note reviews, but 28% and 73% (facial) in two small trials at 1 mg | 3 to 6 months |
| Spironolactone (off-label) | Mostly stops progression; about 4 in 10 regrow some hair | 6 months; full effect 12 to 18 months |
| Iron for low ferritin | May reduce shedding; evidence weak | 3 months |
| Telogen effluvium | Regrows by itself; shedding lasts 3 to 6 months | Review if shedding lasts beyond 6 months |

Sources for each row are in the sections above (3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.10 and 1.3).

## Part 4. A best-practice online pathway for women

**Status of this part.** No UK guideline sets out an online hair-loss pathway for women **[unverified: none found]**. Everything below is the author's synthesis. Each action points back to the sourced section that justifies it. The regulatory frame is the GPhC guidance for online pharmacies. [GPhC guidance, Feb 2025](https://assets.pharmacyregulation.org/files/2025-02/gphc-guidance-registered-pharmacies-providing-pharmacy-services-distance-february-2025.pdf)

### 4.0 Design rules that come from the regulator

Quoted or closely paraphrased from the [GPhC guidance, Feb 2025](https://assets.pharmacyregulation.org/files/2025-02/gphc-guidance-registered-pharmacies-providing-pharmacy-services-distance-february-2025.pdf):

1. Check identity with a robust process, so the medicine goes to the right person.
2. For medicines that need ongoing monitoring, do not base prescribing on a questionnaire alone. Verify the information independently: timely two-way communication, the clinical record, or the GP.
3. Get the GP's details and consent to contact them, and actively share prescribing information. If there is no GP or no consent, decide whether it is still safe to prescribe and record why.
4. Decide whether a physical examination or a diagnostic test is needed before prescribing. If the method of consultation cannot give you what you need, direct the person to an appropriate provider.
5. Some monitoring can be self-reported (the guidance gives blood pressure as its example), but consider whether readings need independent verification.
6. Follow national guidance on off-label prescribing, and record your reasoning.

**What this means for the formulary (author's synthesis).**

| Tier | Medicine | Consultation model |
|---|---|---|
| A | Licensed topical minoxidil (2% solution, 5% foam) for a confirmed diagnosis of FPHL in a woman aged 18 to 65. Any other use (traction, shedding, over 65, a men's 5% solution) is off-label and moves to tier B | Structured questionnaire plus photos, reviewed by a clinician |
| B | Off-label oral minoxidil; off-label spironolactone | Tier A plus a two-way consultation (video or phone), verified blood pressure and pulse, baseline bloods where needed, contraception check, and a letter to the GP |
| Not offered online | Finasteride, dutasteride, bicalutamide, cyproterone 50 mg | Refer (reasons in 3.4, 3.5 and 3.6) |

## Part 6. Key facts: the 25 most decision-relevant facts

1. **Scarring hair loss is permanent and needs urgent referral.** "Once scarred the alopecia is irreversible." [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
2. **FPHL shows as a wider parting with a kept front hairline and a normal scalp; it is diagnosed clinically.** [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf)
3. **FPHL is common:** 6% of women under 50 and 38% of women aged 70 and over in a UK clinic sample. [Birch 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11251562/)
4. **Telogen effluvium starts about 3 months after a trigger, sheds for 3 to 6 months and usually recovers without treatment;** no cause is found in 1 in 3. [BAD TE](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Telogen-effluvium-PIL-Oct-2025.pdf)
5. **A receding hairline or eyebrow loss in a woman is frontal fibrosing alopecia until proven otherwise;** lost hair does not regrow. [BAD FFA](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/condition/frontal-fibrosing-alopecia/)
6. **Male-pattern loss in a woman is very uncommon unless androgens are raised.** [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-male-and-female-pattern-alopecia)
7. **Rapid, progressive virilisation, or a repeat early-morning testosterone above 4.8 (the page gives no unit), needs urgent referral for a possible androgen-secreting tumour.** [PCDS hyperandrogenism](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/hyperandrogenism)
8. **22% of women with PCOS had pattern hair loss, but hair loss without hirsutism is a weak predictor of raised blood androgens.** [Quinn 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24534277/), [2023 PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
9. **Core bloods for diffuse hair loss are FBC, ferritin and thyroid function.** [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview)
10. **The ferritin target is disputed.** The best controlled study found iron deficiency no more common in women with FPHL or chronic shedding than in controls; PCDS still supports an iron trial below 40; "70" is one expert's recommendation. [Olsen 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20947203/), [PCDS](https://www.pcds.org.uk/clinical-guidance/alopecia-an-overview), [Rushton 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12190640/)
11. **Iron deficiency anaemia in a postmenopausal woman needs gut investigation, not just iron.** [BSG 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119/)
12. **The combined pill makes androgen blood tests unreliable;** stop it for at least 3 months if testing is essential. [2023 PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
13. **Biotin supplements can give false blood test results and have no proven benefit for hair without deficiency.** [Almohanna 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380979/)
14. **Topical minoxidil is the only treatment licensed for hair loss in women in the UK (2% solution and 5% foam, ages 18 to 65, general sale).** [2% SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/102/smpc), [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc)
15. **Topical minoxidil roughly doubles the chance of noticeable regrowth** (157 of 593 against 77 of 555; risk ratio 1.93) and adds about 13 hairs per cm² over placebo. [Cochrane 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27225981/)
16. **5% foam once daily gave almost the same hair-count gain as 2% solution twice daily** (+23.9 against +24.2 hairs per cm² at 24 weeks), **but that trial missed its pre-set non-inferiority test;** an earlier, smaller trial did show non-inferiority. [Blume-Peytavi 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27391640/), [Blume-Peytavi 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21700360/) **[corrected in fact-check]**
17. **Licensed topical minoxidil is contraindicated with treated or untreated hypertension and an abnormal scalp, and its SmPC says it "should not be used during pregnancy or lactation". It is licensed only for pattern hair loss: not for sudden or patchy loss, loss after childbirth, or when there is no family history. The men's 5% solutions are contraindicated in women.** [5% foam SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5082/smpc), [Regaine for Men 5% solution SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/5765/smpc) **[corrected in fact-check]**
18. **Oral minoxidil is off-label; in a small open-label randomised trial in women, 1 mg a day was not significantly better than topical 5% once daily. The trial could not prove the two are equal, and there is no placebo-controlled trial in women.** [Ramos 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31473295/), [Gupta 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10806356/) **[corrected in fact-check]**
19. **Oral minoxidil safety in 1,404 patients (case-note review): extra hair 15.1%, lightheadedness 1.7%, fluid retention 1.3%, fast heartbeat 0.9%; 1.2% stopped for systemic effects. A trial that examined women on 1 mg found facial hair growth in 73%, so the case-note figure understates it.** [Vañó-Galván 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33639244/), [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/) **[corrected in fact-check]**
20. **Oral minoxidil consensus: usual start for women 1.25 mg daily; contraindicated with past pericardial effusion or pericarditis, heart failure, phaeochromocytoma, pregnancy or breastfeeding; no routine baseline bloods or ECG without precautions.** [Akiska 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39565602/)
21. **Spironolactone is off-label: 56.6% of women improved in pooled studies (43.2% alone, 65.8% in combination); there is no placebo-controlled trial.** [Aleissa 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37719557/)
22. **Healthy young women on spironolactone do not need routine potassium tests (0.72% high results against 0.76% background), but monitoring is warranted from age 45 (10.1% of women aged 45 and over developed high potassium).** [Plovanich 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25796182/), [Thiede 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31360748/), [Gregoire 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655839/)
23. **Anti-androgens can feminise a male fetus: effective contraception is required and must be re-checked.** [BAD FPHL](https://www.skinhealthinfo.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/Hair-loss-female-pattern-PIL-Oct-24.pdf), [2023 PCOS Guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10505534/)
24. **Finasteride is contraindicated in women in the UK and 1 mg did not work in a placebo-controlled trial in postmenopausal women; bicalutamide 25 mg added nothing to oral minoxidil in the only randomised trial.** [Propecia SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/2194/smpc), [Price 2000](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11050579/), [Libório 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11875198/)
25. **Hair loss is a listed side effect of weight-loss injections: 2.5% on semaglutide 2.4 mg and 4.9% on tirzepatide against 1.0% on placebo; most people recovered while continuing.** [Wegovy SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/13803/smpc), [Mounjaro SmPC](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15484/smpc)

Two regulatory facts that frame all of the above:

- The GPhC expects online prescribers not to rely on a questionnaire alone for medicines needing ongoing monitoring, and to share prescribing with the GP. [GPhC guidance, Feb 2025](https://assets.pharmacyregulation.org/files/2025-02/gphc-guidance-registered-pharmacies-providing-pharmacy-services-distance-february-2025.pdf)
- Cyproterone carries a dose-related meningioma risk (129.1 per 100,000 patient-years above 60 g cumulative dose against 4.5 below 3 g). [MHRA 2020](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/cyproterone-acetate-new-advice-to-minimise-risk-of-meningioma)

## Summary (10 lines)

1. Sort every woman first by scarring or not, patchy or diffuse, shedding or thinning; pain, redness, scale, patches, a receding hairline or eyebrow loss mean refer, because scarring is permanent.
2. Female pattern hair loss is a slow widening of the parting with a normal scalp; telogen effluvium is sudden shedding about 3 months after childbirth, illness, crash dieting, weight-loss injections or a stopped hormone, and it usually recovers alone.
3. Rapid virilisation, male-pattern loss or new androgen signs after the menopause need androgen bloods and urgent referral, not a hair product.
4. Core bloods for diffuse loss are FBC, ferritin and thyroid function; androgens only with androgen signs (and not on the combined pill); vitamin D, B12 and zinc only for a clinical reason.
5. Ferritin targets are disputed: treat true deficiency, offer an honest iron trial below about 40, and refer iron deficiency anaemia rather than selling iron.
6. Topical minoxidil (2% solution or 5% foam, ages 18 to 65) is the only UK-licensed treatment, and only for pattern hair loss; it about doubles the chance of visible regrowth, needs 6 months, causes an early shed, is contraindicated with hypertension and must not be used in pregnancy or breastfeeding. The men's 5% solutions are contraindicated in women.
7. Low-dose oral minoxidil is off-label and has not been shown to beat topical (small trials only); start low (0.625 to 1.25 mg, knowing that quartered tablets give an approximate dose), expect extra facial hair to be common (15 to 25% in case-note reviews, up to 73% when looked for at 1 mg), exclude cardiac and pericardial history, low blood pressure and pregnancy, and check blood pressure and pulse.
8. Spironolactone is off-label with low-quality evidence (about half improve, best with minoxidil); it needs effective contraception, baseline kidney and potassium tests, and repeat potassium tests from age 45, with risk factors, or when she takes a drospirenone pill such as Yasmin.
9. Do not offer finasteride, dutasteride, bicalutamide or cyproterone 50 mg to women online: contraindicated licences, weak or negative trials, and pregnancy, liver, mood or meningioma risks.
10. A safe online pathway asks the stoppers first, uses photos plus a two-way consultation for any off-label medicine, verifies blood pressure, shares care with the GP as the GPhC expects, reviews photos at 6 months and stops when a red flag appears.

### Important things that were missing

Added to the file during this check:

1. **The licence for topical minoxidil is narrower than "hair loss in women".** It excludes sudden or patchy loss, loss after childbirth, an unknown cause and no family history (3.1, 4.1 question 17).
2. **Men's 5% minoxidil solutions are contraindicated in women** in their SmPCs (3.1).
3. **Drospirenone pills (such as Yasmin) with spironolactone:** the Yasmin SmPC asks for a potassium check in the first cycle (3.3, 4.1).
4. **Extra facial hair on oral minoxidil is far more common in trials than in case-note reviews** (73% against about 15%) (3.2, 3.14).
5. **Tablet splitting:** only one brand of 2.5 mg tablet is documented as dividing into halves; quarters are approximate (3.2, 4.3).
6. **No upper age limit was set for oral minoxidil,** although the SmPC urges caution over 65 (3.2).
7. **Flammability** of the solution and the foam, for counselling (3.1).

Still missing, and not fixed because no source could be read:

1. **NICE CKS and the BNF.** These are the two references a UK prescriber is most likely to be judged against. Neither has been read by the author or the reviewer.
2. **UKTIS.** The pregnancy rules in 3.12 rest on SmPCs, a patient leaflet, LactMed and one expert table. There is no sourced advice here on what to do if a woman becomes pregnant while taking spironolactone or oral minoxidil.
3. **The unit for the testosterone cut-off** (1.6, 2.3, 4.2).
4. **Medicated shampoo with licensed minoxidil.** The minoxidil SmPCs contraindicate use with "other topical medical preparations" on the scalp. Whether a rinse-off ketoconazole shampoo counts is not stated in any source read. Decide a rule before pairing them **[unverified]**.
5. **The European S3 guideline and the BAD alopecia areata guideline** remain unread (Part 5).
6. **No search for newer evidence** was possible in this check, so a trial published after the cited reviews could have been missed.

### Result

- **Confirmed: 13** (claims 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20). Two of these carry a part that is still unverified and is marked in the text: trimethoprim alone with spironolactone (claim 12) and the testosterone unit (claim 19).
- **Corrected: 7** (claims 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 11, 17).
- **Unsupported: 0** whole claims. No figure in the 20 was invented or wrongly copied; every error was one of wording, overstatement or omission.

**The corrections that matter most.**

1. Men's 5% minoxidil solutions are contraindicated in women, not merely unlicensed (claim 4).
2. Licensed topical minoxidil is only for pattern hair loss; supplying it for shedding, post-childbirth loss or traction is off-label, and pregnancy and breastfeeding are "should not be used" warnings, not listed contraindications (claim 3).
3. Oral minoxidil has not been shown to equal topical minoxidil, and extra facial hair is likely at 1 mg (73% in a trial that examined for it), not a 15 to 25% risk (claims 8 and 10).
4. A 0.625 mg dose made by quartering a tablet is approximate and outside every SmPC; one generic tablet should not even be halved (claim 11).
5. The 5% foam trial against the 2% solution missed its non-inferiority test (claim 6), and ketoconazole 2% shampoo does not need a prescription (claim 17).
