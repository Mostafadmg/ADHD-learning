# Market, competitors, pricing and unit economics: online hair-loss treatment

**For:** a founder planning a new UK online hair-loss company for men and women, competing with Hims UK, Voy,
Hair + Me, Sons and Numan.
**Written:** 23 November 2026. **Status:** complete first version. Gaps are listed in section 10.

**Rules used in this file**

- Every figure has a source link and a year beside it. Sources were opened on 23 November 2026 unless another date is shown.
- Nothing is invented. Where a figure is worked out by the author it is labelled **ESTIMATE** and the working is shown.
- Where something could not be checked it says **[unverified]** or **unknown**.
- Company claims (for example customer numbers) are labelled as claims.
- Prices change often, and late November is Black Friday season. Each price shows where it was read.

**Words used**

- **POM** = prescription-only medicine (finasteride tablets, minoxidil tablets, dutasteride).
- **Special / compounded** = a medicine made up to a prescriber's order. In UK law it is an unlicensed medicine.
- **Off-label** = a licensed medicine used for something its licence does not cover (for example minoxidil tablets for hair).
- **Gross margin** = what is left from each £1 of sales after the direct cost of the goods. 78% means 78p is left.
- **CAC** (customer acquisition cost) = marketing money spent to win one new paying customer.
- **Churn** = the share of subscribers who cancel in a period. **Retention** is the opposite.
- **LTV** (lifetime value) = the total gross profit one customer brings before they leave.
- **CAGR** = average growth rate per year.

## 0. The short version

1. **Hair loss is very common and mostly untreated.** About 50 million men and 30 million women in the US have
   pattern hair loss ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/androgenetic-alopecia/)). In a European
   survey, fewer than 1 man in 10 with hair loss was on treatment ([Alfonso 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16307704/)).
2. **The UK online players are now large, but their size comes mostly from weight-loss medicines, not hair.** Voy's
   parent turned over £227.4m in 2025 and Numan's £69.3m in 2024 (Companies House, section 3). Hims & Hers UK,
   which sells mainly sexual health and hair treatment, turned over £23.9m in 2025.
3. **Hims & Hers is the giant.** US$2.35bn revenue in 2025 and 2.8 million subscribers in September 2026. It bought
   ZAVA (which runs Superdrug Online Doctor) in July 2025 and Eucalyptus (Juniper UK) in June 2026 (section 3).
4. **Plain medicines are a commodity.** Finasteride costs about £10 a month at the cheapest UK pharmacy and £23 to
   £28 at the subscription brands. A compounded finasteride and minoxidil spray costs £13 to £36 a month (section 4).
5. **Hair and skin prescribing has a high gross margin (about 78%).** Marketing and staff then use most of it
   (section 5).
6. **You cannot advertise a prescription medicine to the public in the UK.** Brands advertise the condition, the
   service, a licensed non-prescription product, or a price "from £X". Regulators tightened hard in 2025 and 2026
   (section 6).
7. **The openings are women, trust and honest clinical care.** The threats are the spending power of Hims and Voy,
   falling prices, and tighter rules on advertising, finasteride and compounded medicines (section 8).

## 1. How many people have hair loss, and who seeks treatment

"Pattern hair loss" (androgenetic alopecia) is the main target of every company in this file. It is the gradual,
inherited thinning that gives men a receding hairline and bald crown, and women a wider parting.

|---|---|---|---|

Other kinds of hair loss matter for a women's service: shedding after illness, childbirth, low iron or fast weight
loss (telogen effluvium), and patchy immune hair loss (alopecia areata). Alopecia UK is quoted as saying about
100,000 people in the UK were living with alopecia areata in July 2025
([Grand View Research summary](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/alopecia-market)).

### 1.3 What share seek treatment

| Finding | Source and year |
|---|---|
| In five European countries including the UK, 47% of men aged 18 to 45 reported hair loss. Of those, **fewer than 10% were pursuing treatment** and **three in four had never tried any treatment**. | [Alfonso 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16307704/) (telephone survey, 1,536 men) |
| On a German online prescribing platform, 55.4% of 2,904 patients had never seen a doctor about their hair loss and 76.1% had never taken finasteride before. | Abeck 2022, summarised in a [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12716827/) |
| On a US online platform, 32% of 8,983 patients had never thought about treating their hair loss until they heard of the platform. | Young 2023, summarised in the same [2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12716827/) |
| About 4 million people in the US used over-the-counter minoxidil in 2025 and about 1 million used prescription finasteride or oral minoxidil. **ESTIMATE:** 5 million US users against 80 million people affected is about 6% on medical treatment (some people
use both, so the true share may be lower). If the same share applied to the UK estimate above, well under
1.5 million UK adults would be on treatment. The message is the same from every source: **most people with hair
loss have never been treated, and online services bring in people who would not have gone to a doctor.**

Why people choose online: in the German platform's own survey, 79.1% gave convenience as the reason and 32.8%
discretion ([Abeck 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37041819/)).

## 2. Market size and growth

### 2.1 Read this first: research firms disagree by a factor of 17

Two firms published "global hair loss" figures that differ wildly because they count different things. Neither
shows its working in public. Treat them as rough guides.

| Market | Figure | Year | Growth | Firm and link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global "alopecia market" (broad: all treatment types) | US$53.3bn | 2025 | to US$100.6bn by 2033, 8.5% a year | [Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/alopecia-market) |
| UK "alopecia market" | US$2.03bn | 2025 | to US$3.55bn by 2033, 7.5% a year | [Grand View Research, UK page](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/alopecia-market/uk) |
| US "alopecia market" | US$12.02bn | 2025 | to US$21.62bn by 2033, 7.9% a year | [Grand View Research, US page](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/alopecia-market/united-states) |
| Global "hair loss treatment products" (narrow: mostly shampoos and conditioners, 87.6% of the total) | US$3.14bn | 2026 | to US$4.69bn by 2032, 6.8% a year | [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/hair-loss-treatment-products-market) |

Useful detail inside those reports (same links, same caution):

- Pattern hair loss is about 71% of the Grand View global total (70.8%) and 73% of its UK total (72.74%), 2025.
- Men were 63.2% of Grand View's global market in 2025. Women are forecast to grow faster (9.1% a year).
- Retail pharmacies held 50.9% of sales in 2025. **Online pharmacies are the fastest-growing channel (12.7% a year).**
- In Mordor's narrower products market, women are 71.6% of sales, because that market is mostly shampoos.

The author's view: Grand View's UK figure (about £1.6bn) looks too high to be prescription treatment alone. It
probably includes cosmetics, devices and procedures. Do not use it as the size of the online prescribing market.

What the notes in those accounts add:

- **Voy / Menwell.** No ultimate controlling party. It bought Trueskin GmbH on 1 October 2025 for £89.7m (mostly
  paid in shares) and Morelife UK Limited on 13 November 2025 for £19.3m. The accounts say the shares used as
  payment were valued with "a revenue multiple of four", benchmarked against a similar listed company. It
  has subsidiaries in Brazil and Germany. Debt was £35.5m at the end of 2025. Share premium (money and shares put
  in by investors over the company's life) stood at £160.9m, of which about £95m was shares issued for those two
  purchases. The accounts do not split sales by condition. The fall in gross margin from 53% to 46% fits a growing
  share of lower-margin weight-loss medicines, but that is the author's inference, not a statement in the accounts.
- **Numan / Vir Health.** The directors say 2024 growth "was facilitated by the launch of new products" and that
  the lower margin reflects "changes in product mix". Investors had put in £42.5m by the end of 2024 (share
  premium). On 21 July 2025 it issued new preferred shares for about £22.8m in cash (**ESTIMATE** worked out from
  the share allotment form: 1,640,773 shares at £3.4985, 3,537,204 at £4.6647 and 666,666 at £0.90). The founder
  stopped being a "person with significant control" the same day, which usually means a holding or voting share
  of 25% or less after the new shares were issued.
- **Skin + Me group.** It makes its own products (Feel Good Pharma Ltd is the group pharmacy). It closed two
  units after the year end (AndBegin and Retail) and said "hyper personalisation was found to not be a key
  requirement from customers". Hair + Me launched on 31 March 2025
  ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/exclusive-why-skin-me-s-next-big-break)).
- **Hims & Hers UK.** Wholly owned by Hims, Inc. The directors say more customers chose "personalised treatment
  offerings", which raised revenue per customer. It bought Health Bridge Limited (ZAVA UK) from its own parent in
  November 2025 and launched a weight-loss programme in the UK in December 2025. It names "strict limitations on
  the promotion of prescription medicines to consumers" as a main risk.
- **ZAVA UK / Health Bridge.** Growth in 2024 came from "our GLP1 weight loss business". Its sales include
  £12.6m of "platform fee revenue" (the fees it earns for running other brands' online doctors). Staff by role:
  25 clinical and pharmacy, 45 customer support, 35 development, 15 general and administrative, 22 marketing.
  Superdrug Online Doctor is run by Health Bridge (this project's Superdrug file, read from
  [onlinedoctor.superdrug.com](https://onlinedoctor.superdrug.com/)), and ZAVA also runs Asda Online Doctor
  ([Asda Online Doctor](https://onlinedoctor.asda.com/uk/hair-loss-treatment.html), per this project's UK sweep).
- **Sons.** Parent is Careforsons Holding (Ireland) Limited. The directors expect operating profit during 2026 and
  net profit in 2027, and have a £4m credit facility (£1.3m unused at the end of 2025). Sons holds its own
  licences for finasteride 1 mg and minoxidil 5% and its minoxidil is stocked by many UK online pharmacies
  (this project's Sons file and UK sweep, `companies/sons/company.json`, `market/uk-sweep.md`).

### 3.4 What the scale figures tell a new entrant

1. **Weight loss changed everything.** ZAVA says so directly. Juniper UK went from £15.6m to £72.0m in a year.
   The companies that used to live on hair and erectile dysfunction now earn most of their money elsewhere, at
   lower margins (46% to 56% against about 78% for hair and skin).
2. **Hair is not their growth story any more.** That can mean less management attention on hair. It also means
   they can fund hair advertising from a much larger business.
3. **A small, focused UK book can make money.** Hims UK made £3.2m operating profit on £23.9m of sales in 2025.
4. **Losses are normal while growing.** Voy lost £26.5m at operating level in 2025, Sons is still loss-making in
   its eighth year, and the Skin + Me group swung to a loss in the year it launched Hair + Me.

### 4.4 What the prices show

1. **A UK "normal" price is £30 to £36 a month** for a combined or personalised product at list price (Numan £36,

2. **Hims UK undercuts everyone at £16.50 a month** for the combined spray or chew, but only on a 6-month plan
   paid up front (£99).
3. **The floor is £10 to £13 a month** at Dr Fox, with no subscription.
4. **First orders are sold at about half price.** Voy 50% off, Hair + Me 57% off for three months, Keeps 30% to
   50% off. A new entrant should plan for this as a cost of winning each customer.
5. **Long prepaid plans are the norm** (6 or 12 months). They bring cash forward and hide churn.
6. **US research finds online brands dearer than ordinary pharmacies:** about 1.5 to 1.6 times the price for
   finasteride ([2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12716827/), citing two 2024 to 2025 studies).
   The UK table shows the same pattern (£23 to £28 against £10).

### 5.6 A worked example (ESTIMATE, for planning only)

| If it costs this much to win a customer | Months of paying needed just to earn it back |

And for lifetime value: a customer who stays 6 months brings about £120 before overheads, 12 months about £240,
24 months about £480. **None of these retention lengths is a measured figure.** The first thing a new service
should measure is how many customers are still paying at months 3, 6 and 12.

## 6. How these companies win customers in the UK

### 6.1 The law in one paragraph

"A person may not publish an advertisement that is likely to lead to the use of a prescription only medicine"
([Human Medicines Regulations 2012, regulation 284](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/1916/regulation/284)).
The advertising code repeats it: "Prescription-only medicines or prescription-only medical treatments may not be
advertised to the public" (rule 12.12). It also bans using "health professionals or celebrities to endorse
medicines" (rule 12.18) and bans medicinal claims for unlicensed products (rule 12.20)
([CAP Code section 12](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/12.html)). Finasteride tablets,
minoxidil tablets, dutasteride and every compounded spray fall under these rules. Minoxidil 5% lotion and foam are
licensed non-prescription medicines, so they can be advertised.

### 6.2 What the MHRA allows an online clinic to do

From the MHRA's guidance for services that may lead to a prescription
([Blue Guide, Appendix 6, November 2020](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6012d8c9d3bf7f05c2040b4e/Appendix_6.pdf)):

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Promote the **service** and the **condition** ("medical consultation for hair loss") | Naming a prescription medicine on the home page, including in small print or hover text |
| A balanced overview of all treatment options on pages the visitor chooses to open | Product claims such as "the best / fastest / strongest treatment" |
| An **indicative price for the condition** on the home page ("treatment from £X") | Prices tied to a named prescription medicine on the home page |
| A factual price list on inner pages | "Special offers on prices of medicines"; free offers of any medicine |
| Icons that invite a consultation | "Buy now" or "Add to basket" for prescription medicines |
| Paid search adverts that promote the service | Search adverts or web addresses that name a prescription medicine |
| Factual comment on news | Naming a prescription medicine in social posts linked to the service |
| | Mentioning unlicensed medicines at all, or calling a treatment that uses one "clinically proven" |

The last row matters most for a compounded business. The guidance says "Unlicensed medicines should not be
mentioned" and that such treatments "may not be described as 'clinically proven' or similar". The MHRA says it
looks mainly at home pages and acts on complaints about other pages.

### 6.3 The platform rules

| Platform | Rule for the UK | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Ads** | "Advertisers cannot promote prescription drugs in their ads or destinations." Online pharmacies "must be registered with the General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC)" and be certified by Google. Telemedicine providers need LegitScript certification or NABP accreditation, plus Google certification. | [Google Ads healthcare and medicines policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/176031?hl=en-GB) |
| **Meta (Facebook, Instagram)** | Adverts that promote prescription medicines may only target the United States, Canada or New Zealand. So none in the UK. Online pharmacies and telehealth providers must be LegitScript-certified. Adverts that only promote a telehealth service in general, or that educate, need no special authorisation. Over-18s only. | [Meta advertising standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/drugs-pharmaceuticals/) |
| **Influencers** | "Promoting prescription medicines via influencers is strictly prohibited in the United Kingdom, the European Union, and Canada" (Hims' own risk statement). | [Hims 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375126000022/hims-20251231.htm) |

Budget for certification before launch: without GPhC registration and LegitScript (or equivalent) a new brand
cannot buy Google or Meta adverts as a pharmacy or telehealth service.

### 6.4 What the regulator has been doing (2025 to 2026)

The Advertising Standards Authority's rulings are about weight-loss medicines, but the reasoning applies to any
prescription medicine. From its advice dated 29 May 2026
([ASA advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/weight-control-prescription-only-medicines.html)):

- "Any claims, images or references that consumers could understand to refer to prescription only medicines are
  likely to breach the Code." Nicknames, pictures of the product and "treatments from £X per pen" all counted.
- Even "weight loss treatments" broke the rule when the landing page made a prescription medicine the clear
  outcome (rulings of 9 July 2025).
- "A consultation for weight loss" may be acceptable, but "it should not indicate that the likely outcome will be
  the prescription of a POM".
- Discounts that apply only to prescription medicines count as promoting them.
- Brands are responsible for affiliates and influencers who earn rewards for new customers (rulings against
  Health Bridge Ltd, Menwell Ltd and UK Meds Direct Ltd, 11 February 2026).
- Urgency ("respond quickly") was ruled socially irresponsible (Juniper Technologies UK Ltd, 8 April 2026;
  Menwell Ltd, 27 May 2026).
- Before-and-after photos are not allowed for prescription medicines.

Three of the companies named in those rulings are hair-loss rivals or sister companies of rivals: Menwell (Voy),
Health Bridge (ZAVA) and Juniper (Eucalyptus). ZAVA and Eucalyptus are both now part of Hims & Hers.

| Route | How it works | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid search on the condition** | Bid on "hair loss treatment", "receding hairline", not on drug names. Advert text sells the consultation. | Rules in 6.2 and 6.3. ## 7. Trends for 2025 to 2027

### 7.1 Oral minoxidil goes mainstream

- After a New York Times article in August 2022, first-time prescriptions in eight US health systems rose from
  0.5 to 0.9 per 10,000 visits within eight weeks ([JAMA Network Open 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10170338/)).
- In Spain in 2024, oral minoxidil was the most prescribed treatment for men (89.1% of 202 dermatologists) and
  women (92% to 93%), and topical use was falling ([Pindado-Ortega 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41831780/)).
- In a US survey, 83% of dermatology prescribers rated it better than the topical form
  ([Sanfilippo 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38443122/)).
- In the UK it is off-label. Every named rival except Numan now sells it, from £13 to £35 a month (section 4).
- A purpose-made extended-release minoxidil tablet (VDPHL01) has reported positive Phase 3 results in men
  ([Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/veradermics-wraps-phase-3-program-men-better-expected-hair-growth-1-year),
  per this project's US sweep; not re-read today).

### 7.2 Topical finasteride: big demand, growing scrutiny

- Hims prescribed compounded finasteride and minoxidil sprays to 638,629 US men in four years
  ([Hims study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12826577/)). In the UK a compounded spray now costs as
  little as £13 a month (section 4).
- The US FDA warned in April 2025 that it had 32 side-effect reports (2019 to 2024), that "there is no
  FDA-approved topical formulation of finasteride", and that product can transfer to women
  ([FDA alert](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded)).
- The European regulator "found no evidence linking suicidal ideation to finasteride skin sprays"
  ([EMA 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/measures-minimise-risk-suicidal-thoughts-finasteride-dutasteride-medicines)).
- A claim that a licensed finasteride spray now has a UK licence could not be confirmed (**[unverified]**, see
  `market/uk-sweep.md`).

### 7.3 Women

- Women are a minority of today's medical market but the faster-growing part (9.1% a year in Grand View's
  forecast) ([Grand View Research](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/alopecia-market)).
- Most UK online pharmacies offer women only Regaine and supplements (this project's UK sweep).
- The subscription brands moved in during 2025 and 2026: Hair + Me (men and women, March 2025), Hers UK (from £13
  a month; [Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/must-read/hers-launches-personalised-hair-loss-treatment-service-for-women-9287470),
  per the UK sweep), Voy's women's plans (from £15), and Dermatica (February 2026, per the UK sweep).
- Hers UK already sells a capsule combining oral minoxidil 1.5 mg with spironolactone 44 mg (section 4).
- In the US, women are served mainly through menopause brands using oral minoxidil plus spironolactone (this
  project's US sweep).

### 7.4 Hair shedding after weight-loss injections

- In trials, hair loss was reported by 4.9% to 5.7% of people on tirzepatide against 0.9% on placebo, and 7%
  against 3% on high-dose oral semaglutide ([2026 systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13100445/)).
- In US health records (547,993 matched adults), users of these medicines had higher odds of shedding (telogen
  effluvium, odds ratio 1.76) and of pattern hair loss (1.64) at 12 months
  ([JAAD International 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12997224/)).
- A 2026 review found rates from 3% to 7% in trials up to over 70% in some real-world groups, with female sex
  and losing more than 10% of body weight as the main predictors ([Acta Diabetologica 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873315/)).
- Why it matters: Voy, Numan, ZAVA, Juniper and now Hims UK all sell weight-loss medicines (section 3). They hold
  the customer who is about to shed hair and can cross-sell. A hair-only entrant needs its own route to these
  people, and needs to be honest that this shedding is usually temporary.

### 7.5 Diagnostics and blood tests at home

- Hims bought a laboratory testing business (Trybe Labs, February 2025) and YourBio Health (January 2026), and
  now offers "laboratory testing services to measure a broad set of biomarkers"
  ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375126000022/hims-20251231.htm)).
- Traya in India ties a home blood test to its hair plan, and a UK pharmacy sells a DNA test to guide the formula
  (this project's sweeps).
- For women, blood tests (iron, thyroid) have a real clinical role (see `foundations/clinical-women.md`). For
  men with typical pattern loss they are mostly marketing.

### 7.6 Photo tracking and AI

- Voy ties monthly progress photos to its money-back promise ([Voy guarantee page](https://www.joinvoy.com/hair-loss/guarantee)).
- Coaching and AI photo tracking were not found at the big UK brands (this project's US sweep, list of ideas not
  found in the UK). A 2026 letter in a leading dermatology journal criticised "medical triage and screening
  deficiencies in direct-to-consumer hair loss platforms" ([JAAD 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40945742/)).
  Better triage is a way to stand out.

### 7.7 New medicines

Recorded in this project's US sweep with their sources (not re-read today): clascoterone 5% scalp solution passed
two Phase 3 trials in 1,465 men, with a US filing planned for early 2027
([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/clascoterone-5-delivers-strong-phase-3-results-for-androgenetic-alopecia)); the
extended-release minoxidil tablet above; and PP405, an early-stage topical
([Dermatology Times](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/pelage-s-pp405-demonstrates-efficacy-in-phase-2a-trial-for-androgenetic-alopecia)).
None is licensed in the UK. If they arrive, compounded mixes lose part of their appeal.

### 7.8 Regulation is tightening on every side

| Area | What changed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Finasteride safety | The MHRA strengthened warnings on 11 May 2026: depression, suicidal thoughts and sexual problems that "may persist after treatment is stopped". Prescribers should ask about mental health history and review patients regularly. UK Yellow Card reports to May 2025: 170 of suicidal thoughts, including 19 deaths by suicide. | [MHRA Drug Safety Update](https://www.gov.uk/drug-safety-update/finasteride-and-dutasteride-updated-safety-warnings-for-psychiatric-side-effects-and-sexual-dysfunction) |
| Finasteride in Europe | Suicidal thoughts confirmed as a side effect; 313 finasteride cases reviewed against about 270 million patient-years of use; a patient card goes in every 1 mg pack. | [EMA 2025](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/measures-minimise-risk-suicidal-thoughts-finasteride-dutasteride-medicines) |
| Online prescribing | GPhC guidance (February 2025): some medicines "are not suitable to be prescribed by a questionnaire model alone" (for example weight-management medicines, medicines needing monitoring, heart medicines). Every site must give a consultation before any prescription medicine is supplied. | [GPhC](https://assets.pharmacyregulation.org/files/2025-02/gphc-guidance-registered-pharmacies-providing-pharmacy-services-distance-february-2025.pdf) |
| Advertising | Wave of ASA rulings on prescription medicine adverts in 2025 and 2026 (section 6.4). | [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/weight-control-prescription-only-medicines.html) |
| Compounding | FDA alert on topical finasteride (2025); a German court case over batch-made finasteride and minoxidil solution (this project's US sweep); in February 2026 the FDA moved against compounded weight-loss ingredients and named Hims. | [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-alerts-health-care-providers-compounders-and-consumers-potential-risks-associated-compounded), [Hims 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1773751/000177375126000022/hims-20251231.htm) |

Hair-loss medicines are not named in the GPhC's list. But oral minoxidil is a blood-pressure medicine used
off-label, and finasteride now carries mental-health warnings, so a careful service should expect to be judged by
the higher standard.

## 8. Opportunities and threats for a new UK entrant

### The three biggest opportunities

1. **Women, including shedding after weight loss, childbirth and menopause.** Perhaps 3 to 6 million UK women
   have pattern hair loss (section 1, estimate). Most online pharmacies offer them only Regaine. The segment is
   forecast to grow fastest. Women need proper triage (iron, thyroid, hormones, pregnancy plans), which a
   pharmacist prescriber can do well and a two-minute form cannot.
2. **Trust as the product.** Regulators are tightening on finasteride, on advertising and on questionnaire-only
   prescribing, and three rivals or their owners have ASA rulings against them. A service built to the higher
   standard from day one (real consultation, mental-health screening, scheduled reviews, plain words about
   unlicensed medicines, side-effect support) is aligned with where the rules are going. It also answers the
   published criticism of weak screening on these platforms.
3. **Good economics in a part of the market the giants now treat as secondary.** Hair and skin prescribing earns
   about 78% gross margin. Customers prepay for 6 or 12 months. Hims UK shows a UK book of about £24m can be
   profitable. The largest rivals are busy with weight loss, where margins are a quarter to a half as much.

### The three biggest threats

1. **Rivals with far more money.** Hims & Hers spent US$919m on marketing in 2025 and now owns Hims UK, ZAVA, the
   platform behind Superdrug and Asda online doctors, and Juniper UK. Voy turned over £227m. They can sell the
   first order at half price for longer than a start-up can.
2. **Falling prices and substitutes.** Finasteride is £10 a month and a compounded triple spray £13 a month at
   Dr Fox. Hims UK sells its combined spray for £16.50 a month. Licensed new medicines may arrive from 2027 and
   weaken the case for compounded mixes.
3. **Rules that can remove your main sales tools or products.** You cannot advertise prescription or unlicensed
   medicines, name them in adverts, discount them loudly or call them clinically proven. Finasteride warnings may
   grow. A regulator could restrict large-scale compounding, as has started in the US and Germany.

## 11. Summary in ten lines

1. Roughly 12 million UK men and 3 to 6 million UK women have pattern hair loss (estimate); in the US, 50 million men and 30 million women.
2. Most are untreated: under 10% of affected European men were on treatment, and over half of online patients had never seen a doctor about it.
3. Market-size reports range from US$3bn to US$53bn worldwide, so trust company accounts more than forecasts; online is the fastest-growing channel.
4. Hims & Hers (US$2.35bn revenue, 2.8 million subscribers) now owns Hims UK, ZAVA, the Superdrug and Asda online doctor platform, and Juniper UK.
5. Voy (£227m, 2025) and Numan (£69m, 2024) are big because of weight loss; Hims UK (£24m) and Hair + Me's group (£43m) are the closer hair comparisons.
6. A UK combined or personalised hair product lists at £31 to £36 a month; Hims UK charges £16.50 prepaid; Dr Fox sets the floor at £10 to £13.
7. Hair and skin prescribing earns about 78% gross margin, but marketing takes 39% to 46% of revenue at Hims and first orders go at half price.
8. No rival publishes CAC or churn; Hims lost about 103,000 subscribers in one quarter when it cut marketing, so retention must be measured from day one.
9. UK law bans advertising prescription and unlicensed medicines; brands sell the condition and the service, and regulators ruled against Voy's, ZAVA's and Juniper's owners in 2025 to 2026.
10. Best openings: women, trust and clinical quality, and a high-margin niche the giants treat as secondary. Biggest threats: rivals' money, falling prices and tighter rules.
