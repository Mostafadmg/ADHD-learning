Trying for a baby

Finasteride may affect male fertility while you are taking it.
These changes are usually reversible after stopping treatment, but recovery may take several months. If you are planning to try for a baby, it is best not to use this or to stop it several months before trying to conceive.

Depression

Some people have experienced severe depression when taking finasteride. Before taking finasteride, read and keep the finasteride patient cards and patient information leaflet within your pack and inform your doctor if you have any personal history of depression or suicidal thought. You should stop taking this medication immediately if you notice that your mood is affected and speak to your doctor.

Finasteride and your prostate

Finasteride can lower the level of a prostate protein called PSA (prostate-specific antigen) in your blood. Because of this, it’s recommended that you see your GP for a prostate check (rectal examination) and a PSA blood test before starting treatment, and then at regular intervals while taking it.

How often this is needed will depend on your age and personal risk factors. These checks help to detect any prostate problems, including prostate cancer, as early as possible.

Sexual problems

If you experience decreased sex drive, difficulty having an erection or ejaculation problems on finasteride, you should contact your doctor for medical advice.
