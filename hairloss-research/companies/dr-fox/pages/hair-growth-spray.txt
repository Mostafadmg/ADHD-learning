About
 Dr 
 Fox Dual Action Hair Growth Spray
 Dr 
 Fox Dual Action Hair Growth Spray reduces male pattern hair loss and stimulates new hair growth, resulting in thicker, longer hairs, and more visible hair coverage.
The spray contains finasteride 0.3%, minoxidil 5%, and tretinoin 0.01%.
Apply once daily to the scalp (suggest at night time).
Supplied on prescription as an unlicensed 'special' medicine.
Reduced application of 6 sprays (1ml) daily.
Read the
 patient leaflet [#leaflet] 
 for instructions, possible side effects, cautions for use, and potential interactions with other medicines.
Who can use
 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray
Adults (aged 18+).
Assigned male at birth.
Affected by male 'pattern' hair loss.
Read the

How to use
 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray
Wash and dry your scalp.
Apply 6 pumps of product (equivalent to 1ml) to the scalp daily (suggest at night time).
Rub in gently then
 wash your hands thoroughly .
Avoid breathing the spray in during application.
Most beneficial stages of hair loss to use the Hair Growth Spray
 When the spray is started early enough in the hair loss process and is used continually, hair thinning/excess hair loss can be prevented. The treatment works best at the mild-to moderate stages of hair loss: stages 2-5 of the Hamilton-Norwood scale (see illustration below).
Stages of hair loss information
No notable hair loss or recession of the hairline.
Often referred to as the 'adult' or 'mature' hairline: some recession at the temples and some sparsity at the front-centre.
Two variants:
 Either 
 development of deeper recession at the temples with increased sparsity at the front-centre.
 Or 
 recession at the temples and front-centre remains at stage 2, but there is notable hair loss/sparsity at the crown (vertex).
Recession at the temples and front-centre is developed beyond that of stage 2 and there is hair loss/sparsity at the crown.
Recession at the temples and front-centre hairlines is developed beyond stage 4 along with increased hair loss/sparsity at the crown. The band of hair between these areas is still present but sparse.
The areas of hair loss at the front, temples, and crown have extended and merged: the band of hair crossing the crown is lost, with only sparse hair remaining.
The remaining hair is an (often sparse) band around the back of the head connecting the remaining hair at the sides.
When can I expect to see results?
 Hair on the scalp grows at approximately 1cm per month. Thicker, longer, hair with greater scalp coverage, may be seen as early as 3 months with daily use. Excess hair loss has usually stopped after 6 months of daily use and changes are more obvious, with maximum benefit by 1-2 years.
 In the unlikely event your hair loss continues beyond 12 months treatment, with no notable increase in hair thickness or coverage, you are likely to be a non-responder and it is unlikely you will benefit from continuing treatment.
 Once improved hair coverage has been achieved, continued treatment is required to maintain the benefits. If treatment is stopped, pre-treatment appearance will likely return within 3 to 6 months.
How
 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray works
 In the average adult male approximately 10% of the male sex hormone testosterone is converted to the more potent hormone Dihydrotestosterone ( DHT ).
 DHT 
 is responsible for adult male characteristics including male hair thinning and hair loss.
 DHT 
 affects hair follicles in a way that prevents hairs from reaching their full growth potential.
 For more information see
 Dihydrotestosterone (Society for Endocrinology) [https://www.yourhormones.info/hormones/dihydrotestosterone/] .
 The active ingredients in the
 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray work to reverse hair thinning and hair loss using a combination of the following treatments:
Finasteride
 Finasteride reduces the conversion of testosterone to
 DHT , therefore less
 DHT 
 reaches hair follicles and hair is allowed to reach its full growth potential.
 For more information see
 Common questions about finasteride ( NHS ) [https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/finasteride/common-questions-about-finasteride/] .
 Finasteride has traditionally been taken in
 tablet form [https://www.doctorfox.co.uk/hair-loss-treatments/finasteride.html] 
 (orally) and 1mg finasteride is licensed for use to treat male pattern hair loss. Clinical trials have shown topical finasteride has comparable results with oral finasteride from doses of 0.5mg to 5mg daily and may have fewer side effects than oral treatment however is not yet licensed.
Minoxidil
 Minoxidil relaxes small blood vessels where it is applied to the scalp, increasing blood flow to hair follicles. Blood carries essential oxygen and nutrients which stimulate new hair growth, encouraging thicker, longer, more visible hair. Minoxidil has been proven to stimulate hair regrowth, likely by enhancing cell multiplication.
 Topical minoxidil is already licensed to treat male pattern hair loss (e.g. as
 Regaine [https://www.doctorfox.co.uk/hair-loss-treatments/regaine.html] ).
Tretinoin
 Tretinoin is a vitamin-A derivative that promotes hair growth indirectly by increasing minoxidil uptake. Minoxidil spray alone is usually used twice daily, but combined with tretinoin allows minoxidil to be used once daily with equivalent results in
 clinical trials [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17902730/] .
Other ingredients
 In addition to minoxidil, finasteride, and tretinoin, the spray contains glycerol, sulphuric acid, industrial methylated spirit, and water. These ingredients are commonly used in topical formulations and help deliver the active medications effectively to the scalp.

Were assigned female at birth.

 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray
Keep away from children and animals. If swallowed, minoxidil can lead to heart toxicity.
Pregnant women and women with childbearing potential should avoid using or handling as finasteride may affect development of a baby's genitals.
Inform your doctor if you have a
 PSA 
 (prostate specific antigen) blood test, as topical finasteride may alter the test result.
Avoid exposure to
 UV 
 light (sunlight) as tretinoin can make the skin more likely to burn.
If treatment is stopped the beneficial effects will likely reverse within 6 months.
Consult your
 GP 
 before using if you:
Have heart disease, treated or untreated high or low blood pressure (cautions for minoxidil 5%).
Have a history of urinary obstruction, or difficulty passing urine, this may be caused by an enlarged prostate gland (caution for finasteride).
Have any male breast changes: growth of breast tissue (gynaecomastia), lumps, pain, enlargement, or nipple discharge.
Possible side effects of
 Dr 
 Fox Hair Growth Spray
 Side effects may be less likely when using topical rather than tablet (oral) medication as active ingredients are delivered directly to the scalp with a smaller amount entering into the bloodstream.
 Skin irritation (itching, dryness, redness) at the application site can occur and is most likely due to the alcohol base, but may be due to minoxidil or tretinoin. Using a scalp oil such as jojoba or coconut oil, or a lightweight, non-oil-based scalp serum, can help reduce these effects. If you develop bothersome skin redness, rash, or other irritation, stop treatment. If irritation is severe or does not improve after stopping treatment, you should seek advice from a pharmacist or your GP .
Stop treatment and seek advice from your GP if you experience: 
Bothersome dizziness or feeling faint which persists after stopping treatment.
More rapid weight gain, or weight gain associated with breathlessness.
Persistent, bothersome nausea.
Low mood, depression or feeling depressed (feelings of sadness and unworthiness).
Any change in breast tissue.
Rare sexual side effects:
Reduced male fertility - advise stop treatment 3 months prior to planned conception.
Reduced libido and erectile dysfunction.
Please stop treatment and seek advice from your GP urgently if you experience: 
Chest pain and/or palpitations (an uncomfortable awareness of your heart beating in your chest).
Severe depression/suicidal thoughts.
An allergic reaction: swelling of the lips, face, tongue, or throat; breathing difficulties or difficulty swallowing.
 For the full list of potential side effects read the Patient Information Leaflet [https://www.doctorfox.co.uk/pdf/Dr-Fox-Hair-Growth-Spray-PIL.pdf] ( PIL ).

The hair growth cycle
 Hair growth is a continuous process which can be explained via 4 cycling phases:
 Anagen (growth) 
 - a single shaft of hair grows from a hair follicle for 2-8 years. Hair length depends upon the length of this phase (unless the hair is cut).
 Catagen (regression) 
 - over 1-2 weeks, the supporting hair follicle regresess and detaches from the hair. The hair however remains in place, secured by a small white bulb of keratin.
 Telogen (resting) 
 - the old hair rests for 2-3 months. while the old hair is resting, a new hair develops at the base of the hair follicle (the start of anagen for the new hair).
 Exogen/Anogen restart (shedding) 
 - as the new hair grows, it pushes the old hair out. On the diagram above, exogen occurs between anagen restart and anagen.
 For individuals not experiencing hair loss, it is normal to shed over 100 scalp hairs per day!
If the anagen/growth phase moves into the catagen/regression phase prematurely, excess hair shedding and thinning occurs. This is characteristic of male pattern hair loss.
If the percentage of hair follicles remaining in the telogen/resting phase is increased, hair loss decreases.
