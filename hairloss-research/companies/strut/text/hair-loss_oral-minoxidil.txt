Frequently Asked Questions
What is oral Minoxidil?
Minoxidil is an oral prescription vasodilator that is FDA-approved to treat certain cases of severe high blood pressure. At lower doses, clinicians may prescribe oral Minoxidil off-label for hair loss. Studies of low-dose oral Minoxidil suggest it may help increase hair growth in some patients. Because oral Minoxidil for hair loss is an off-label use, your clinician will determine whether it is appropriate for you based on your medical history and treatment goals.
How do I take oral Minoxidil tablets?
Take your dose of oral Minoxidil at the same time each day, once a day. You may take Minoxidil with or without food, but you may want to try taking it with food if you notice stomach upset after your dose. If you miss a dose and are only off by a few hours, you may go ahead and take your missed dose. However, if you missed a dose and it has been more than a few hours skip your dose and continue on like usual the next day.
How does oral Minoxidil work?
The exact mechanism by which oral Minoxidil may support hair growth is not fully understood. Minoxidil is a vasodilator, meaning it relaxes blood vessels. Research suggests that it may also affect the hair-growth cycle and prolong the growth phase of hair follicles.
How to store Minoxidil tablets?
Store your Minoxidil in a closed container at room temperature in a dry environment. Avoid bathroom storage due to a potentially high humidity environment. Store Minoxidil tablets safely away from pets and children.
What are the potential side effects of Minoxidil tablets?
Commonly reported side effects of low-dose oral Minoxidil may include increased hair growth on the face or body, lightheadedness or dizziness, headache, increased heart rate, and swelling caused by fluid retention. Oral Minoxidil can also cause serious cardiovascular adverse effects. Seek medical care promptly if you develop chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, a rapid or unusual heartbeat, significant swelling, or rapid unexplained weight gain. See the Important Safety Info tab for additional risks, precautions, and situations in which oral Minoxidil may not be appropriate.
How long until I may see an effect from oral Minoxidil tablets?
Individual response and timing vary. When oral Minoxidil is prescribed for hair loss, changes may take a few months to become noticeable. Your clinician can help assess your response over time. Continued treatment may be necessary to maintain any benefit, and hair loss may resume if treatment is stopped.

What are the potential benefits of using oral Minoxidil?
Low-dose oral Minoxidil is sometimes prescribed off-label for pattern hair loss. Clinical studies suggest it may improve hair density or growth in some patients, although individual response varies and oral Minoxidil has not been FDA-approved for the treatment of hair loss. Your clinician can determine whether its potential benefits and risks are appropriate for you.
Can you use oral Minoxidil with a topical Minoxidil formulation?
A clinician may consider topical Minoxidil together with low-dose oral Minoxidil for some patients. Especially people who are not achieving their desired results with their current treatments. While taking both forms together is an off-label regimen, your assigned clinician will review your medical history, current health conditions, and treatment goals during your online evaluation to evaluate if a combination plan is safe and suitable for you. Many patients prefer to start with either a topical or an oral treatment before considering a combined routine. If your provider prescribes both oral and topical Minoxidil, it is essential to follow their specific dosing instructions, review all safety and side-effect information, and contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience any adverse effects.
What are the potential risks of using oral Minoxidil with a topical Minoxidil?
Using oral and topical Minoxidil together may increase exposure to the medication and could increase the likelihood of adverse effects such as dizziness, low blood pressure, rapid heart rate, or fluid retention. Your clinician will review your medical history, medications, and cardiovascular risk factors before determining whether combination treatment is appropriate.
Why would someone use an oral Minoxidil over a topical Minoxidil formula for hair loss?
Some patients may prefer an oral treatment because they find daily topical application difficult or inconvenient, experience scalp irritation with topical products, or have not achieved their desired response with topical treatment alone. Oral Minoxidil also has different systemic risks than topical Minoxidil, so your clinician will determine which option is appropriate based on your medical history, preferences, and treatment goals.
